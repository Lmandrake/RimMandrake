Here are the two cases I'd add. I worked only from the frozen statement and didn't read the code, so treat each one as a risk to check, not a confirmed defect.

## 1. The prisoner gets better: tended, wakes up, stands, then tries to escape

This is the most revealing gap. Everything listed as implemented assumes the prisoner stays downed. The feeding fix is "downed pit prisoners qualify for WardenFeed", and the 60000-tick sample can pass simply because the raider never got up. A real player catches a bleeding raider, a doctor tends them, and within a day or two they're standing in the hole. That's when the feature either works as a prison or turns out to be a holding pen for unconscious people.

What the player does next, and what each step depends on:
- **Doctor tends the wound** from the rim, using medicine. A bleeding prisoner nobody can reach dies, and from the player's side that looks like the prison killed them.
- **The prisoner wakes up and eats on their own.** WardenFeed no longer applies once they're standing, so food dropped by DeliverFood at their cell has to be something they can reach and will eat.
- **A warden chats to recruit or convert.** This is why a player takes prisoners at all, and it needs the warden to actually get to a prisoner who is now on their feet.
- **The prisoner tries to break out.** Can they climb the lip and leave? Does the bed check you suppressed (TakeToBed) come back once they're no longer downed?

Probes:
- Capture a raider with one bleeding wound. Assert that a real Doctor tend job runs and bleeding stops.
- Run until the prisoner is no longer downed, with an assertion that fails if they never get up.
- Then over about 2 days, assert:
  - their food need recovers through self-feeding from the delivered meal, without WardenFeed;
  - with interaction mode set to Recruit, resistance drops through real chat jobs;
  - no TakeToBed or escape job is started;
  - their position stays inside the hole.
- Force a break-out (`PrisonBreakUtility.StartPrisonBreak`) and assert one of two things: the hole holds, or the escape is visible and readable. They must not end up on the rim with no sign.
- Run the disabled control at the "awake" stage too, not just at capture.

## 2. The hole stops being a drained, enclosed room while someone is in it

This one is specific to FlowWorks. A drained moat sits in a flow network, so the player can refill it: open a gate, let rain or connected water return, mine through a wall, add a ramp. The prison status comes from "enclosed proper pit room", so any of these can quietly remove it while the prisoner is inside. A downed prisoner plus rising water is the sharpest version. Your standing rule is that no pawn vanishes without a readable sign, and this is exactly where that could break.

Probes:
- With a downed prisoner held, let liquid flow back in. Assert a readable outcome: they get moved, a warning fires, or they show a drowning or wet effect. Fail if they silently die or despawn.
- Breach one wall into an ordinary room. Assert the room loses prison status, and that the warden behaves the same way vanilla does with a prisoner outside a cell, not stuck in an error or job loop.
- **Save and reload mid-sentence.** Your reload test compares terrain, liquid and thing hashes, but it never reloads a save that has a prisoner held in the pit. Save with one, reload, run 2500 ticks, then assert:
  - the room is still reported as a prison;
  - the prisoner is still a prisoner, at the same cell;
  - no TakeToBed is started.

  Room roles and guest state are recomputed on load, so this is a cheap place for the feature to fail.

If you only add one, add the first: it decides whether this is a prison or just a place to keep downed raiders.
