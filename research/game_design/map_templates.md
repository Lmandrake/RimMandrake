# Map templates — celebrated dungeon layouts worth studying
(DUNGEON_DESIGN_RESEARCH_1)

Each: the map, what structurally makes it work, and how it transfers to a
hand-authored RimWorld tile map. [S#] = SOURCES.md entry.

## 1. Caverns of Thracia (Jennell Jaquays, 1979) — the loop-and-faction masterwork
Structure [S1][S9]: three entrances to level 1; loops on every level; multiple and
discontinuous connections between levels; living factions (beastmen vs the dead
city's remnants) occupying different regions with their own conflicts.
Why it works: no chokepoint ever forces one experience; every party writes a
different route; the factions make the dungeon a SITUATION, not a gauntlet.
Transfer: the reference topology for a large vault — 2–3 breaches in, ring routes,
and the Arsenal-vs-flesh war as the resident faction conflict (type-② vaults).

## 2. Spencer Mansion (Resident Evil, 1996) — recursive unlocking hub
Structure [S7]: ~116 rooms in four areas; a central hub (main hall) with wings
that re-open one layer deeper as emblem keys are found; quantified visit data shows
most rooms visited only ~2× despite the whole game being about re-traversal.
Why it works: the map IS the progression system; re-entry always crosses changed
ground; the player's growing mental map is the real reward.
Transfer: the vault as security system rather than residence — sigil-gated wings
off a central atrium; template for metric D2's "2–3 live options" sweet spot.

## 3. Undead Burg + Firelink loop (Dark Souls, 2011) — critical path bent into circles
Structure [S8]: a strong critical path with short optional side loops rejoining it;
verticality; the destination visible long before it is reachable; a shortcut back
to the hub unlocked at the end.
Why it works: "a path that could be a straight line is forcibly made a circle" —
exploration always pays off in ROUTE, the one currency every player values.
Transfer: on a flat colony map, verticality becomes wall-and-window layering:
overlooks via embrasures, parallel corridors with breach points, and the
end-of-dungeon wall breach that opens the haul route home.

## 4. Eagle's Tower (Link's Awakening, 1993) — the self-restructuring dungeon
Structure [S10]: four pillars + a carried wrecking ball; destroying them collapses
the top floor onto the map, rewriting the layout mid-visit.
Why it works: one legible goal ("topple it"), pursued through lateral thinking
rather than key-hunting; the player CAUSES the map's second state.
Transfer: grav-anchor demolition dropping the vault core's defenses — RimWorld
collapse mechanics (roof support) make "destroy supports, change the map" native.

## 5. Stone Tower Temple (Majora's Mask, 2000) — one map, two states
Structure [S10]: the entire temple inverts; every room is authored twice in one
geometry; demands all four transformation masks.
Why it works: maximal content-per-tile; re-traversal is never repetition because
the state changed; the inversion is also the fiction's blasphemy made playable.
Transfer: the two-state principle (power on/off, flooded/drained, contracted/
relaxed flesh) is the cheapest way to double a hand-authored map's content.

## 6. Forest Temple, Ocarina of Time (1998) — hub-and-spokes with atmosphere load-bearing
Structure [S10]: central chamber with four torch-spokes (the Poe sisters), each
spoke a self-contained challenge that feeds a central state change.
Why it works: the hub shows progress at a glance (torches lit); spokes are
parallelizable, letting the player pick their order — breadth without confusion.
Transfer: a four-reactor vault core room whose lit/unlit status IS the progress
UI; readable at RimWorld zoom with lamp/power visuals.

## 7. Tomb of Horrors (1978) — set-piece anthology, negative pacing template
Structure [S9]: a short linear crawl of extraordinary one-room set-pieces (the
chessboard floor, the reverse-gravity chamber, the false crypt, the sphere of
annihilation mouth) with uncued lethality.
Why it works / fails: the set-pieces are unforgettable; the experience is hateful
because the map teaches nothing and telegraphs nothing.
Transfer: strip-mine the rooms, invert the ethics — every stolen set-piece gets a
readable cue (metrics B3). The false crypt is the best red-herring template ever.

## 8. Castle Ravenloft (1983) — the villain-centric vertical dungeon
Structure [S9]: a castle organized around one intelligent resident who moves,
watches and stages encounters; strong verticality; tone unified from gate to crypt.
Why it works: the dungeon has an author INSIDE the fiction; every room speaks
about its owner; players feel hunted in a home, not spelunking in a hole.
Transfer: the flesh dungeon should be Ravenloft-shaped in spirit — the Assailant's
biology IS the resident intelligence; rooms are organs with jobs; the place
responds (spawns, seals, digests) rather than merely containing spawns.

## 9. Brogue's machine rooms & guardian vaults — the priced side room
Structure [S12]: procedurally placed but hand-designed "machines": lever-cage loot
vaults, flooding rooms, guardian puzzles — each a self-contained contract (risk X
for reward Y) legible on sight.
Why it works: modular drama; each room is optional, so difficulty is self-selected;
the dungeon becomes a market of priced choices.
Transfer: author 5–10 reusable RimWorld "machine" room designs (turret cage,
cryo-cache, breaker room) and compose dungeons partly from them — Skyrim's kit
discipline [S6] applied at room granularity.

## 10. Left 4 Dead campaign maps — the rhythm template
Structure [S15]: safe room → traversal → crescendo event → traversal → finale
(hold-out then run-for-extraction), with the director modulating intensity between
authored anchors.
Why it works: the intensity curve is architected INTO the map (anchors) while
moment-to-moment pressure stays unpredictable; troughs are guaranteed by design.
Transfer: the pacing skeleton for every dungeon act: sanctuary room (authored
trough) → priced crescendo (the alarm you pull) → extraction finale under the
undercave-style collapse clock [S29].

## 11. Darkest Dungeon expeditions — corridor-node graph with a resource clock
Structure [S20]: rooms as nodes, hallways as edges with per-tile events; light/
stress as depleting resources; camp as mid-run reset; leave-early always on the
table with partial reward.
Why it works: the MAP is simple, the DECISION structure is rich — push deeper vs
bank what you have; every step prices itself against the clock.
Transfer: the raid-in-and-leave economics the vault concept already proposes —
make partial success a first-class outcome with its own loot table and letters.

## 12. RimWorld Anomaly: Labyrinth & Undercave — the engine's own proof [S29]
Structure: pocket maps entered via obelisk/pit gate; labyrinth = disorienting
corridor maze with high loot density and a find-the-exit objective; undercave =
winding organic caves, boss (dreadmeld), then a 10-hour collapse timer forcing
extraction.
Why it works: shipped and player-tested ON THIS ENGINE — proves pocket-map entry,
loot-vs-extraction tension, timed collapse, and organic cave-flesh aesthetics all
read correctly in RimWorld's camera and control scheme.
Transfer: the direct mechanical baseline; our dungeons differ by being FIXED,
authored, faction-storied sites on the frozen world rather than disposable
generated pockets — everything Anomaly proved, plus permanence and canon.

## 13. Five-Room Dungeon — the minimal complete site [S5]
Structure: guardian → steel-proof puzzle → red herring → climax → twist, in five
spaces.
Why it works: smallest shape that still has pacing, variety and a reversal; proven
across thousands of tabletop sessions as the unit of one evening's play.
Transfer: the template for minor sites (crashed ships, outposts, small caches) so
the two flagship dungeons aren't the only authored-site language on the map.

## Cross-cutting structural laws the templates agree on

- **Loops beat trees** everywhere above five rooms [S1][S2][S8]; author the loop
  first, hang rooms off it.
- **Show the payoff early, gate it hard** [S8][S2] — foreshadowing is the cheapest
  motivation machine.
- **Re-traversal must cross changed state** [S5 anti-pattern][S7][S10] — else cut
  the backtrack.
- **The exit shortcut is part of the reward** [S6][S8] — doubly true where loot
  must be hauled.
- **One new mechanic per dungeon, taught safe then tested hot** [S18][S10].
- **Set-pieces are remembered; corridors are not** [S7][S9] — budget art and
  authoring time toward 3–5 tableau rooms per site.
- **A resident intelligence beats a spawn table** [S9-Ravenloft][S15] — even
  faked with staged scenes and patrol scripting.
