# The Fever Wood's Tenant: what is now written (digest, 2026-10-10)

_BENCH research pass, read-only, for re-asking story-sitting card 19
(`design/Jawa/story_sitting_cards_2026-10-10.md:204`). The owner's answer to that card, typed:
*"Look deeper into that Biome. There is much written now about the creature beneath. It is not an
unknown mystery, but a mystery still for most of the game."* (`story_sitting_cards_2026-10-10.md:278`)._

**Short version.** The card quoted the kit spec's F1 heading, *"one aquifer, one entity, never seen"*.
That heading is from 2026-09-11, and an owner sitting overturned it on 2026-09-23. The creature
is now **named** (free tier: the **sekkulaath**; campaign: a **giant dianoga**). It acts
**in ordinary play** through six kinds of tentacle. It **farms** players with bait. Towns keep its
**young** in prison tanks. Since 2026-10-02 its story is the **ransom of its young**. One
thing is still held back: the **whole animal rising**, the plot-reserved full emergence. It has
never been spent, and no spine card has claimed it yet.

Legend: **OWNER** = his verbatim words or a decision by question card, recorded at a sitting.
**AGENT** = an agent's draft, proposal or build note. When sources conflict, the newer one wins.

### 1.2 What it is, per the newest sources

- **Species, not an enigma.** A colossal sekkulaath (a giant dianoga in the campaign). It lives under
  the **whole** Fever Wood's water table, and every pool is a window it can reach through. Its body is
  never drawn. Only tentacles are drawn, in six kinds: feeler, snare, lash, porter, sentinel and bloom.
  Of these, only the porter's look carries an owner ruling (`deep_and_mud:142-158`).
- **It cannot leave** the water, because it dries out (canon). It **cannot be killed** on the planet. It can
  be driven off, or killed **on one map** through the eye set-piece.
- **It has intent but never speaks.** It farms with bait, keeps count of its young, and *"pays its debts"*
  (campaign line, `src/RimUtinni/UtinniPatches/Patches/RUT_BroodRansom_Campaign.xml:124`).
- **What is still a mystery:** the **size of the whole animal** and **what is at the bottom**.
  The 2026-09-23 sitting moved the mystery from *what is it* to *how big is it, and what does it
  want*. The owner's 2026-10-10 words describe exactly that state.

### 1.3 Contradictions and stale text

1. 🔴 **The card's own context was stale.** The kit spec F1 heading and its *"Player experience. Nothing."*
   paragraph (`kits/fever_wood_kit_spec.md:45-51`) were overturned by `deep_and_mud` §0. F4 (`:126-153`) is
   overturned too: its *"labels are evasive descriptors, never a name"* is wrong. The kit spec carries no
   pointer to the overturning. `the_fever_wood.md:132` does, and `deep_and_mud:845` says F4's
   rationale is void. ⇒ **The kit spec's F1 and F4 prose is owed a correction**, deleting the false text
   (deletion rule). `CAMPAIGN_ARC_GATHER.md` G33 (`design/Jawa/campaign/CAMPAIGN_ARC_GATHER.md:116-117, :267`)
   still cites F1 as the Tenant's home and ruling. That is how the stale quote reached card 19.
2. ⚠️ **"The Tenant" collides with the ship's "nine tenants."** The ship's mind is *"nine tenants and no
   landlord"* (`design/Jawa/reconciled_lore/06_the_ship.md:17`, and *"feeding a god grows a tenant"*,
   `09_arcs_dungeons_quests.md:41`). Any spine text that calls the Fever Wood creature "the Tenant" will
   read as a tenth ship god. ⇒ In story docs, call it **the sekkulaath / the deep** and keep
   "Tenant" for the C# class names (`RUT_MapComponent_TheTenant`, `RUT_TenantEmergenceSpawner`).
3. ⚠️ **The Atlas entry's lore pulls the opposite way from §1.** `src/RimUtinni/Atlas/Defs/AtlasEntryDefs/RUT_Atlas_Wilds.xml:82-88`
   says *"The clan does not name the thing. Traders who do, name it differently each time"*. It also calls the biome
   *"the poison wood"*. The owner ruled the species *"known and named"*. The trader line can stand as
   folk register, since the dread is in the scale. But "poison wood" is wrong: the **Poison Forest is a
   different biome** (terminator, `the_one_map.md:170`), and the Fever Wood's fever is **fungal spore**
   (`_freeze_rulings_2026-09-07.md:337-341`). AGENT text; owed a one-word fix.
4. ⚠️ **The acoustic scanner's quiet line** (`src/RimUtinni/UtinniPatches/Patches/AcousticPayload_UtinniBiomes.xml:226`,
   *"Nothing under the pools is awake"*) is fine as a *quiet* reading. Note it for any option that has the
   deep "wake".
5. ⚠️ **The two "great emergences" are different events, and the names are close.** The built
   `RM_MapComponent_TentacleWatch` "Great Emergence" (`src/RimMandrake/FeverWood/Source/RM_MapComponent_TentacleWatch.cs:156-160`)
   is the §2b **eye set-piece**: many limbs plus the eye, and it can be killed per map. The
   **plot-reserved full emergence**, where the whole animal rises, is a separate, dormant capability:
   `RUT_TenantEmergenceSpawner` (`src/RimMandrake/EnvironmentalHazards/Source/RUT_TenantEmergenceSpawner.cs:35`),
   whose own comment says the mass and tentacle defs are *skipped*. Nothing references it.
6. ⚠️ **Card 19's option B would break two standing rules.** B made the creature *"a sleeper in the same family as the
   Rakata sleepers."* But the woken ancients are **Rakatan people**, given a pawn xenotype (`ANCIENTS_AS_RAKATA_SPEC.md:2-12`).
   And any claim of Rakatan authorship needs a ruled row in `rakatan_legacy_index.md`, which has 7 rows and none for
   the sekkulaath (`:21-25`). It would also fight the owner's *"map it to the Dianoga."*

## 3. How it touches the campaign spine as now ruled

Today's rulings (`story_sitting_cards_2026-10-10.md:244-279`):

- **Round 1–2:** the War Lab is the **single impact site** of the Assailant, under the Chill's propane lake.
  The Rust Cathedral has fought a **long war of attrition** against it and wants the last armed originals
  destroyed. The Spire is the Scald's **dark tower**, in the mountains, and the player enters it only on
  **Cathedral trust**. Blowing the lake early brings Imperial heat and a garrison.
- **Round 3:** when the lake opens, the Utinni feels it, the narrator speaks and the map changes.
  The crater cut-scene is **"every region notices, then cut to the crater"** (card 4, B).
- **Round 4:** the **Reclamation** starts when **enough ancients are woken** (a count). The **ship's mind
  reacts at each of the five Antiquities ladder stages** (card 15).
- **Round 5:** the Deeps are an optional side-world, with **sea-floor vaults yielding ladder finds**, and they
  are the Empire-detection **refuge** (card 20).

**What touches the sekkulaath, measured:** **no ruled spine line names it.** Three existing lore
facts sit right beside it:

1. **Everything scavengeable on this planet is Rakatan** (`design/Jawa/reconciled_lore/03_deep_history.md:32`, OWNER
   2026-08-20). The deep's gifts *"from the very bottom"* (ruled 2026-10-02: an ancient ship component,
   a sealed crate) are therefore **Rakatan leavings** by existing canon. No claim about the creature is needed.
2. **The ship's substrate relaxes near Rakatan vaults or salvage, and it draws the ship toward them**
   (`06_the_ship.md:51-53`, OWNER 2026-08-15). The ladder now gives the ship a reaction at each stage.
3. **Card 4's "every region notices"** needs a per-region beat. The Fever Wood holds the one built-but-unspent
   reaction on the planet: the whole animal rising.

What it must **not** be counted as: one of the **woken ancients** for the Reclamation. Those are Rakatan
sleepers, and see §1.3 item 6. It is also not Assailant flesh. The Assailant's living residue is the
"strange biomes" and the poison forest (`03_deep_history.md:35-41`), and claiming it would need a legacy-index row.

## 4. Options for the sekkulaath's story role

Each option is built only from existing lore and existing or ruled mechanisms. None adds a place, faction or
mechanism. All three keep the ruled Brood Ransom unchanged and treat it as the mid-game.
They differ in **who spends the plot-reserved full emergence, and on what.**

### A. The planet's flinch: the whole animal rises once, as the Fever Wood's beat in the crater cut-scene

The full emergence is the Fever Wood's frame in card 4's *"every region notices"*. When the
lake opens, or when the Cradle strikes the War Lab, the animal with *"excellent hearing"* (canon, `deep_and_mud:104`)
rises whole for the only time. Players who never left the desert see it in the cut-scene. A colony living
in the Fever Wood gets it live, as the dormant `RUT_TenantEmergenceSpawner` event.
- **Uses:** card 4 B (ruled), the emergence reserve (ruled, unspent), the canon hearing, and the kit's
  "one IncidentDef" promise.
- **Buys:** the mystery holds **literally until the endgame**. It is the cheapest spine tie. It spends the reserve on the
  most planet-scale moment there is, and it says nothing new about what the creature is.
- **Costs:** the tie is **thematic, not causal**: it rises because the world shook, not because of anything it
  wants. Most players meet it as a cut-scene frame rather than a fight. The acoustic line *"Nothing under the
  pools is awake"* gets a dated reading. The tentacle and mass art for a whole-body shot is still owed (F4).

### B. What it sits on: the deep's gifts feed the Antiquities ladder, and the ship knows

The great gifts from the bottom are, by canon, Rakatan. So the ship's substrate *"relaxes"* near the
Fever Wood pools, and a returned young's gift can be a **ladder find**, the way card 20 made the
sea-floor vaults ladder sources. For most of the game the mystery is *why the ship leans toward still water*.
Late in the game the ladder tells it. CARTOGRAPHY reveals vault sites (`antiquities_design.md:167`), and CULTURE
unhides Xenobiology (`:123, :166`), so the player learns the deep has been hoarding the old world's leavings
and trades them back for its children. The full emergence stays reserved.
- **Uses:** `03_deep_history.md:32` (all salvage is Rakatan), `06_the_ship.md:51-53` (substrate kinship), card 15
  (the ship reacts per ladder stage), card 20's precedent (a new ladder *source*, not a new mechanism), and the
  built `RM_DeepGiftLoot` table.
- **Buys:** a **causal** spine tie that runs through the whole mid-game. It makes the Brood Ransom
  matter to the main arc. It turns Sporefall's tank into a real dilemma with spine stakes: the doubled gift against
  Wildsteam peace. The creature itself stays non-Rakatan.
- **Costs:** it widens the ladder's sources a second time (Deeps, then Fever Wood), on top of a fixed budget of
  ~48 reads. The gift table needs a campaign-side antiquity entry. The Brood Ransom becomes spine-weighted, so its unset
  tuning (thresholds 1/3/6/10, INVENTED) starts to matter.

### C. Earned and spent by the player: the Wildsteam lend the deep, on trust

This is the bedazzle review's **Wildsteam Drum** (`feverwood_bedazzle_review_2026-10-02.md:273-277`), which the owner
held off the 2026-10-02 card as *"his call alone."* It is shaped like the ruled Cathedral→Spire gate (card 7).
Sporefall knows what the deep is. **Its trust**, measured as goodwill with the Wildsteam, is what lets the clan
call the whole animal up once at a pool, as a weapon on the two-front war. Freeing Sporefall's young
**forecloses** this, because it breaks the town's peace. So the ruled Brood Ransom's biggest temptation and this
option's reward are mutually exclusive.
- **Uses:** the bedazzle §3 drum pitch (AGENT, unticketed), the Sporefall display tank and goodwill break (built),
  the trust-gate pattern (card 7), and the two-front war (ruled).
- **Buys:** the player **authors** the reveal. The mystery lasts exactly as long as the player chooses, and that
  fits *"a mystery still for most of the game"* closely. It creates a **trust-or-ransom fork** that the Brood
  Ransom alone does not have.
- **Costs:** it spends the plot reserve on a biome-level tool rather than a spine moment. The drum is BENCH's
  invention, not owner lore. *"Awake on that map for a season"* means a harsher hazard on the player's own map.
  Players who skip the Fever Wood never see the whole animal.

**Not offered:** card 19's option B, a Rakata sleeper counted among the woken, for the reasons in §1.3 item 6.
