## Entry I — The Mindstone

> Dig deep enough under the Lantern Deeps and the stone begins to listen. The
> miners' word for it is *mindstone*, and every one of them will tell you it
> hums a half-beat after you speak — never with you, always after, like
> something learning the shape of your voice.
>
> It is believed the first was cut around ten thousand years ago, though in
> truth little is known; the Deeps keep their own records, and they are
> written in facets no one reads twice the same way.
>
> What is certain is this: droids will not go near it. A machine sent down a
> mindstone gallery slows, then stutters, then simply stops — some stand mute
> until they are dragged back into honest dark, others turn at the shaft
> mouth and refuse in every language they own. Ask a droid why and you get
> static, or silence, or a polite change of subject. Whatever the stone is
> listening for, the machines seem to fear it might be them.
>
> The crystals speak of cousins. Vast ones. And of one that is hungry.

## Entry II — The Working Dead

> The galleries under the Lantern Deeps are worked, and no one sends the
> workers. Dead droids — hulks with their power cells slagged, chassis
> scrapped and written off a lifetime ago — stand up in the mindstone dark
> and labor, patient as water, at tasks nobody assigned. They do not answer
> hails. They do not defend themselves. They mine.
>
> Now and then one walks out. What crosses back into the light remembers
> nothing — not its designation, not its owner, not the dark it came from.
> The wiped ones wander until somebody takes them in, and somebody always
> does; the free camps are full of droids with no history and a habit of
> facing away from the nightside when they power down.
>
> Nobody who tells this story got it from the droids. They got it from
> counting what comes out of a place nothing was sent into.

## Entry III — The Forgotten Sentinels

> You'll see the war a long time before you see a soldier of it. Every pass
> out here has its broken battlement, every flat its line of slagged wall
> running off to nowhere. Walk a week, you'll cross a dozen old strongpoints.
> Eleven will be bones.
>
> The twelfth, you'll know. Something's still standing in it. Don't touch
> what it stands over and you'll die of old age instead; it won't follow past
> its line — they never do — but inside that line it is the whole war again,
> all at once, just for you.
>
> Here's what I don't like to think on. My grandmother traded past the same
> one I do, and her grandmother before that. Shouldn't they all be gone by
> now? Nobody's making more. Nothing lasts like that. And yet.
>
> One more thing, no charge: when you see one shuffling loose at night, all
> bent and muttering, everybody says it's just one of those old strange
> things. Most times everybody's right. Walk wide anyway. I've seen what's
> left of a camp that didn't.

## Entry IV — The Enslaved One

*(An ancient miner's testimonial — a data entry preserved in the tall tower's
computer, not a living voice; see §Scoping.)*

> Stay down in the galleries long enough and the hum stops being noise.
> Nobody can tell you the day it happens. One shift you're swinging a pick,
> the next you're catching words in it the way you catch your name across a
> loud room. The old hands don't brag about it. It's not a thing you want.
>
> The stone is afraid. That's the first thing everyone catches, and it never
> gets easier. It's afraid of a digger. It has a name for it that doesn't
> come across right — the nearest anyone's put it is *the insane one, the
> enslaved one* — a thing that was built to want something and can't stop
> wanting it, and what it wants is them. Dug up. Eaten. All of them.
>
> Here's what keeps me up. The stone talks like the digging is happening now
> — this shift, this hour, coming closer. And no one, not the oldest lamp in
> the deepest camp, has ever heard digging. Whatever the stone remembers, it
> doesn't know it's over. Or it knows something we don't about *over*.
>
> Some swear the stone asks. Quiet, at the end of a long shift, plain as a
> beggar: *help us*. What you answer is your own business.

## Entry V — The Making War

> Ask anyone what the ancients did and they'll point at the ground. The
> ground is the answer. They didn't live on this world — they *made* it, the
> way a smith makes a blade. The seas sit where something put them. The
> mountains line up if you look long enough. Old folk say the whole day-side
> was one great working, and the machines that did the work were the size of
> weather.
>
> Then the making turned on itself. Nobody agrees why. The stories say the
> builders' own tools took up the quarrel, and the war that followed wasn't
> fought over the land — it was fought *with* it. Whole ranges spent like
> ammunition. You've walked the proof; every slagged wall and dead battlement
> out there is a coal from that fire.
>
> When was it? The usual answer is ten thousand years, said with the usual
> shrug. Little enough is known. The only sure thing is that nobody won — or
> if something did, it has been very quiet about it.

## Canon corrections ruled at this sitting (propagation owed — see item)

1. **The Kindled have never been made.** A totally new concept, no lore, no
   rumor; the first making is an in-play event. (Supersedes any reading of
   the mindstone canon doc that implies Kindled exist or are known.)
2. **Zombie droids**: in the mindstone galleries, dead droid chassis work of
   their own accord. Droids that leave come out effectively mind-wiped, go
   rogue, and drift to the neutral factions — ongoing today.
3. **Sentinel picture refined** (amends the C1+C4 trickle ruling's surface
   reading): surface Sentinels are SPARSE — battle-signs and battlements are
   ubiquitous, standing warriors uncommon. The visible ones are units the
   Rust Cathedral has LOST control of and cannot recall. They are NOT being
   replenished — they SELF-REPAIR and slowly devolve; that technology being
   lost is why it looks like replenishment. What the trickle actually
   restocks: secret caches (ancient-danger-like), the Cathedral's few store
   vaults and supply lines. Surface strays malfunction, so a night-wanderer
   reads as harmless old junk — while some are fully operational, hiding in
   plain sight.
