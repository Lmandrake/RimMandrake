## 0. Position and the inherited law

Beneath the Grey Sea (terminator band, θ≈92, bearing 8 — hypersaline, terminal,
shrinking fastest). The base sheet's discipline binds everything below: **one
monoculture, one endemic giant lineage, solitary everything, a roster where
"do we already have one?" usually answers no more.** The Miasma's deltas
remain the lineage's crèche; the open deep is where its adults spend their
centuries alone.

## 1. What it is

Grey-green murk a few meters deep to the eye, floored with strange mineralogy
and death. Salt pillars rise from the bottom like a blind forest; **dense brine
pools — heavier than the murk itself — lie in the hollows with their own
surfaces, their own slow ripples, their own shores, underwater.** Old ruined
vehicles and skeletons stand encased in mineral where they sank. Something
enormous and crusted moves between the pillars, rarely, in a temper. And
through it all, a man-sized skeletal thing picks its careful way, watching you
longer than you've been watching it.

**[+2026-09-26] What the drop adds to the picture.** The floor is not bare sediment
with a few columns on it: it is *"a wonderland of odd pillars and formations"* —
salt domes *"like mushrooms growing, strange shapes everywhere"*, salt chimneys
*"shooting up from the sea floor, shooting out murky white super-brine"*, huge salt
crystals, and brine channels running from the chimney fields downhill into the
pools (§4b). Among the formations grows a **crystalline flora** — green tissue
living inside salt it has force-aligned into cubic armour, pink, violet and amber,
marred and never perfect (§4a). Salt falls out of the water like snow (§4c). An
abundant sessile layer — small shrimp, clam and mussel equivalents — picks through
what rains down (§4). The pools defend themselves: touch one and you are
crystallised where you stand (§4d). And from the deepest pool of every Grey Sea
tile rises a **Brine Elder** — a colossal branching salt-crystal organism older
than every faction, that trades in novelty and speaks only in chemistry (§4e).
The four reference images are the brief, cited by path in §9.

## 2. Planetary position

Terminator sea-bottom × **saturation**: the Grey Sea concentrates toward its
death, and the bottom is where the concentration lives — brine below brine,
minerals crystallizing out of a sea with no exit.

## 3. Driving forces

- ⭐ **Brine below brine** (owner): the pools are saturated-plus — too dense to
  mix, lying in the floor's hollows as **lakes at the bottom of a sea**.
  Everything that enters dies; everything that dies there *keeps*. The pools
  pickle — the sea's display cases.
- **The murk** (owner): low visibility, permanent — sight measured in meters;
  the biome is navigated, not seen.
- **The preservation register**: the planet's third — tar keeps the soft, the
  Scarlands keep the sealed, **the brine keeps the shape.** Anything not
  eaten within days is mineral-jacketed forever: the bottom is a statuary of
  everything that ever sank, from the war's vehicles to yesterday's diver.
- **The masons never stop** (§4) — the pillar forest thickens and shifts;
  charts age.

## 4. What lives here

*Cap released (owner ruling, 2026-09-10): the "three, and no more" law is retired.
The three below remain the anchor cast; the roster may grow beyond them. Schools and
swarms stay out of the Grey — anything that schools lives in the Twilight Deep.*

- **The pillar-mason** — the Grey's monoculture (the base sheet's "different
  one," now named): a crystal-binding film that *builds* — mineral columns
  rising from the floor toward light they never reach. The pillars are the
  only landmarks in the murk, so **all travel below is pillar-to-pillar**:
  waymark navigation through blindness, this world's lane-travel pattern
  (boughways, causeways, root-roads) taken underwater. Divers carve
  waymarks; a lost diver is someone who missed one pillar.
- **The crusted giant** — the endemic lineage (bred at the Miasma's crèches,
  returned here for its solitary centuries), **ill-tempered** (owner): brine
  that hurts, worn as a life condition. Centuries have **encased it in its
  own mineral armor** — a living thing wearing the biome's geology,
  indistinguishable from a drifting pillar until it moves. It **scrapes** —
  grinding crust off against the columns — so *fresh scrape-sign means the
  giant is near*: the tell that lets the observant survive. And in the murk,
  the base sheet's one-saturated-mark rule turns to dread: **you see the
  mate-mark's glow before you see anything else** — one point of impossible
  color approaching through the grey.
- ⭐ **The ossuary shrimp** (owner): **a man-sized, skeletal-seeming shrimp,
  moving along picking at everything it can — strangely intelligent and
  nimble, but shy and evasive.** The undertaker racing the geology: it works
  the freshly dead in the narrow window before the minerals take them, and
  it has watched every diver who ever came down here from two pillars away.
  It is never caught in the open, never fights, and remembers. (Solitary,
  single-spawn, per the inherited bans.)

## 4a. Flora — the crystalline idea [+2026-09-26]

Owner, 2026-09-26. The five full specs are his, **verbatim in the content doc §4.2–4.6,
and that text is the authority for any def**; this section is the ruling-shaped digest.
The sheet had no flora at all; this fills the empty slot.

**The idea:** *"green plants growing INSIDE the crystal, using proteins to force-align
the salt outside them as armour. Pink, violet and amber geometric solids sitting on the
sea floor with striking natural beauty."* 🔴 **His one aesthetic rule, verbatim, on every
art brief for this biome:** *"marred slightly, don't make them too perfect or they look
manufactured."* Every crystal form is chipped, clouded or asymmetric somewhere.

Seven forms:

1. **Crystalline cubic sculptures** — the general form; the Crucible Pod is its
   fully-specified case.
2. **Sphere plants** — propped on radial thin spines, *"both armour and weaponry — like
   a huge version of a microscopic creature."* **RULED 2026-09-26: does LOW contact
   damage.** The floor has two plants that hurt (this and the Brine Crown) and five
   that do not; a diver learns to read the crystal by shape.
3. **Glass Veil Kelp** — tall curtain-like stands *"over trenches and brine rivers"*
   (the brine channels, §4b): anchored deep, long transparent sheets hung with cubic
   beads and razor-thin plates, *"a hanging chain of salt prisms"*; the living cells sit
   in shadowed channels behind the crystal using filtered light; in current the veils
   *"clatter softly"*. *"Elegant, eerie, slightly ceremonial."*
4. **Brine Crown Anemoflora** — an anemone made of crystal thorns: squat base, a crown
   of long cubic spines, fleshy dark membranes between them that take light and
   nutrients. *"When the salinity shifts, the membranes retract deep into the crown,
   leaving only a deadly mineral porcupine behind."* Impales and traps small grazers
   (the sessile layer, §4) and feeds on their decay — partly a detritus-harvester.
   Hurts on contact.
5. **Mosaic Fan Palms** — broad fans built of *"arrays of small square crystal panes,
   each grown at slightly different angles"* over pigmented tissue; *"a tiled mosaic or
   ceremonial shield."* Some *"rotate portions of the fan by swelling internal canals."*
   *"Open toward weak filtered light from above"* is a POSE — the palm is built facing
   up; it tracks nothing. *"Some species"* are colour/pattern variants of one def
   (pink/violet/amber), not three defs.
6. **Salt Chimney Vines** — grow ONLY around the warm mineral seeps of the salt
   chimneys (§4b): creeping mats that find a seep and climb it in *"spiraled cubic
   bracing"*; dim-light harvesting plus *"chemosynthetic symbionts"*; *"white angular
   vines twining around black vent rock … mustafar-meets-salt-sea."* Chimneys must
   exist first.
7. **Crucible Pods** — a lobed sphere in sediment growing *"a thick cubical crystal
   cage"* around its organs; when mature it *"dissolves one face of the cube and
   releases drifting seed-cysts"*; juveniles *"settle in cracks between salt blocks"* —
   so they cluster beside the crystals and domes. Three visible stages: sealed cube,
   one face open, empty cage.

**The laws all hold.** Ban 4 (no light-source flora, no bioluminescent dressing): none
of the seven emits light; every one *uses* filtered light or chemistry, and the murk
stays blind. Two looked like crossings and are not:

- *Retraction and pane-rotation vs the inherited "no heliotropism, no sun-tracking, no
  plants that open and close" (`terminator_sea.md` §6).* That ban's object is a plant
  keeping a DAY on a world that has none — it sits in a sentence about the sun. The
  Brine Crown moves on a chemical cue (salt snow, a chimney venting), never on light or
  clock; the fan's panes are built facing up and shift inward light without tracking
  anything. Neither is a diurnal open-and-close, so the ban stands and these stand
  beside it. RULED additive, 2026-09-26.
- *Chemosynthetic symbionts vs the inherited "no cold gas vents … those belong to the
  poison forest."* The chimneys are WARM mineral seeps venting BRINE; the ban's object
  is the poison forest's cold-gas chemistry. Warm salt is not cold gas. RULED additive,
  2026-09-26.

## 4b. Terrain and formations [+2026-09-26]

Owner, 2026-09-26, six things in the order he gave them. Reference images by path in §9.

- **Salt chimneys** — *"real 'salt chimneys' shooting up from the sea floor, shooting

  plume of the same super-brine the pools are made of: a pool's vertical cousin, and the
  Salt Chimney Vines' only host. **RULED 2026-09-26: chimneys ALSO crystallise, at

  into it (§4d), so the chimneys are the visible teacher of a mechanism the pools
  deliver invisibly. (`RUT_ScaldVent` is the shipped venting-feature precedent to read
  first.)
- **Huge salt crystals** — *"on the sea floor, harvestable and rather valuable."* A
  treasury node, not bulk salt: the §7 evaporite treasury made mineable. Engine shape:
  the Odyssey `SolidIce` pattern (a `RockBase`-family mineable), the same family as the
  jacket mineral in §4d.
- **Coloured salts** — *"Different colours of salt make great RimCuisine ingredients."*
  MEASURED 2026-09-26: RimCuisine is not in the mod list; the only cuisine mod is ours,
  `mandrake.rsw.cuisine`. ⇒ The salts are `RM_`-tier items of the Grey's own mod,
  consumed by our cuisine mod through `MayRequire`-guarded recipes; no hard dependency
  either way. The palette matching the flora — pink, violet, amber, plus white — is a
  BENCH inference, not a ruling.
- **Salt domes** — *"like mushrooms growing, strange shapes everywhere"* (image 03):
  rounded, capped, sometimes overhanging mounds — a second formation family, squat
  where the pillars are tall. *"Strange shapes everywhere"* is a density instruction:
  the floor is crowded with mineral forms.
- **The pillar wonderland** — *"a wonderland of odd pillars and formations"* (image
  01): what the pillar-mason's work looks like. The pillar is a placed feature def, the
  mason its fiction; building it makes four existing descriptions stop lying.
- **Brine channels** — the *"brine rivers"* of the kelp spec. **RULED 2026-09-26: a
  brine CHANNEL TERRAIN STRIP, generated from a chimney field downhill into a pool** —
  the super-brine flows to the basin. No vanilla river machinery (the pocket map has no
  river genstep; the surface sea is `allowRivers false`). It gives the kelp its home and
  the floor a legible gradient toward the danger.
- **The crusted shoreline** — *"the crusty whiteness that should be on every Grey Sea
  shoreline"* (image 02): knobbly, cauliflower-textured salt grown over the stones, not
  a flat pan. Surface-and-shore, so it lands on `terminator_sea.md`'s ground (*"salt
  crust, white, and it takes a footprint"*) and its carrier is `RM_SeaShoreExtension`
  (`mandrake.rm.seashores`, already on `RM_GreySea`); `RUT_Jawa_SaltCrust` is a flat
  evaporite pan — right family, wrong texture. **RULED 2026-09-26: the shore = the
  white crust TERRAIN plus salt domes scattered on it** (image 03 where the mounds
  break the surface). **No harvestable shore salt** — the sea-floor salt stays the
  prize. "Every" is a coverage rule: no Grey Sea shore tile without the crust.

## 4c. Weather — salt snow [+2026-09-26]

Owner: *"On the sea floor, a special weather event: precipitating salt crystals, like
snow."* A pocket-map weather — salt crystallising out of the water column and drifting
down: the floor's one visible motion besides the pools breathing. It is the natural
trigger for the Brine Crown's retraction and the feed for the sessile layer.

*Why it does not cross the inherited "no rain, no thunderstorms, no snow, no ice"
(`terminator_sea.md` §6):* that ban is about WATER weather on the SURFACE, and gives its
own reason — *"at +14 °C with no lift, the only water delivery is thin fog."* Salt
falling out of supersaturated brine onto the floor is not weather-snow and not water;
this sheet already had *"the crackle of growing crystal"* and *"scrape-dust falling."*
RULED additive, 2026-09-26. Build: the Pyrelands `AshFall` pattern (own `<overlay>`
colour, `snowRate` stays 0). ⚠️ Pre-existing defect found in the same pass: `RM_GreySea`
carries vanilla `Rain 4`, which IS against the surface ban — fix it alongside.

## 4d. Brine pools — the crystallisation defence [+2026-09-26]

Owner called the pools *"the center of the weird mechanisms."* His three sentences:

> *"Brine pools and the creatures near them have a unique defence: they squirt out a
> protein shower causing ultra-rapid crystallisation around the player, freezing them
> in place and possibly smothering them — just like being frozen in ice already exists
> in the game."* · *"Touching a brine pool directly does the same thing."* · *"Loot
> within the pools is ultra-protected until the player figures out how to get at it."*

One mechanism, three triggers (a pool creature's squirt · direct contact with a pool ·
an Elder's pool disturbed, §4e), one consequence, one economy. This is §3's *"everything
that enters dies; everything that dies there keeps"* and ban 1 turned into a mechanism —
the same one that made the statuary. Entry is still not survivable; the pool encases you.

- **RULED 2026-09-26: a crystallised pawn is ENCASED AS AN OBJECT that must be mined
  out — not a hediff.** One mechanism for the living and the dead, matching the statuary
  and Odyssey's `SolidIce_Loot` shape (MEASURED: vanilla has no pawn-freezing hediff;
  `SolidIce` + `GenStep_FrozenRuins.EncaseStructuresInIce` is the thing-side precedent).
  Smothering is a timer on the jacket, not a second hediff. A downed pawn can be
  rescued; an encased one must be dug — which is what *"ultra-protected"* asks for. A
  colonist standing white among the war's vehicles is this sheet's image exactly.
- **The jacket mineral** — a salt twin of `SolidIce` (mineable, yields the salt-crystal
  item; defName unassigned), laid by a `GenMorphology`-style patch around pools and
  around the statuary. It is what the §7 *"jacketed salvage"* is jacketed in.
- **The keys known so far:** chisel/mine the jacket; or an Elder's exchange (§4e), which
  dissolves or ejects what the pool holds. Whether other keys exist is his to add.
- **Needs that do not exist yet:** the saturated-plus pool grade of liquid/terrain
  (`LIQUID_TYPES_MOD_1`'s owed grade; today only `RM_Liquid_Brine` shallow/deep), its
  shore cells where harvest happens, and the contact trigger on it.

## 4e. The Brine Elders [+2026-09-26]

Owner's paragraph is **verbatim and entire in the content doc §7.1 — the authority for
every word below.** Digest, ruling-shaped:

- **What it is.** *"An impossibly ancient native species of colossal branching
  salt-crystal organisms, each rising from the deepest ultra-dense brine pools as a
  trunk five to ten cells wide before dividing into massive cubic and faceted limbs
  that climb upward into the lighter saline ocean."* A body of *"living ionic
  lattices."* Sessile — *"they cannot leave these abyssal pools — the ultra-brine is
  both their habitat and their metabolic substrate"* — so in engine terms a multi-cell
  thing with a comp, not a pawn; it trades from where it stands. Preserved *"through
  catastrophes that erased younger civilizations"*: older than every faction.
- **Where. RULED 2026-09-26: Grey Sea ONLY — and ONE PER GREY SEA TILE.** Owner,
  verbatim: *"ONLY grey sea, but there's one per grey sea tile"*. Every Grey tile's
  floor has its deepest pool, and the Elder rises from it. 🔑 Consequence: an Elder
  values a novelty ONCE, so a specimen already traded to one tile's Elder is still
  novel to the next tile's. The Grey's tiles are a **distributed market**, and "travel
  to another Grey tile" becomes a real reason to move. The seen-set is per Elder and
  persists in the save.
- **The discharge.** *"They accumulate charge directly from the chemically extreme
  brine … and can release it in blinding area-wide discharges powerful enough to stun
  living creatures, disable droids, collapse shields, and cripple nearby ships."*
  **RULED 2026-09-26: BOTH** — a defence when its pool is disturbed (harvest at the
  shore is fine; mining the jacket at the pool's edge is a disturbance) AND a rare
  unprompted event — **with a visible charge build-up on the limbs as the tell**, so
  an observant diver leaves before it lands (this sheet's own principle: the tell that
  lets the observant survive). **RULED: "cripple nearby ships" is filed as its own
  engine check** (what a discharge can do to an Odyssey gravship is UNMEASURED) and does
  not block the other three effects. **Open:** how the tell renders against ban 4 (the
  only glow is the giant's mark) — the discharge is his word *"blinding"*, an event and
  not dressing; the tell should read as motion and crackle along the lattice rather than
  a standing light. His call if the art needs more than that.
- **Its memory. RULED 2026-09-26: "the Reshapers" ARE the Rakata** — the Elders' own
  exonym for the people who terramanufactured the world (`ASHKARR_WORLD_DEFINITION.md`
  §3b, `ANCIENTS_AS_RAKATA_SPEC.md`). Recorded as THEIR word, not a new faction: they
  *"interpret history through chemistry, pressure, charge, mineral deposition,
  biological composition, and disturbance rather than names, politics, or technology"*,
  and "Reshapers" is a description, not a name. The Elders are the only eyewitness on
  the planet to the arrival; that owes a row in `rakatan_legacy_index.md`. Voice
  register for any Elder text: it reports what changed in the water, never who or why.
- **What it does to its pool.** *"Deliberately alter the mineralization of their pools,
  dissolving entire blocks of crystallized seafloor to uncover ancient mummified
  bodies, wreckage, or artifacts, or violently ejecting long-entombed objects upward
  from the brine as part of an exchange."* The Elder is one of the keys to the pools
  (§4d): it removes jacket cells, or puts a thing on the shore.
- **The novelty trade.** *"What they value above all is novelty: a newly encountered
  life form, corpse, xenotype, tissue, mineral, or unfamiliar material may be
  extraordinarily valuable once, then nearly worthless thereafter."* Rule in one line:
  **each Elder keeps a set of what it has seen; the first specimen of a category it
  has not seen is worth a great deal; every later one is worth almost nothing.**
  *"Technology fascinates them but is conceptually opaque"* — valuation ignores market
  value and tech level entirely. A colonist of a new xenotype is an offering. Not a
  `TraderKindDef`; a new trade mechanism, and the exchange reads as *"negotiating with
  a geological intelligence that has spent tens of thousands of years quietly
  collecting one specimen of anything genuinely new."*
- **What it gives back.** (1) Matter modified *"in ways no conventional craftsman
  can"* — e.g. *"a resonant storage crystal that passively increases the effective
  capacity or charge retention of all nearby batteries"* (a building with an area
  comp; nothing like it exists in `src/`). (2) *"Singular treasures accumulated over
  millennia, typically only one example of each"*: *a functional lightsaber sealed
  since the Rakatan era · an intact pre-Republic navigation core · an unknown droid
  brain preserved in salt · a one-of-a-kind alien weapon whose operating principle is
  no longer understood · a sealed biological relic from an extinct species · some
  beautifully useless artifact whose significance only becomes apparent much later.*
  Tier (Q11a): the first three are canon IP and live in the `RSW_`/`RUT_` layer,
  patched onto the Elder's table with `MayRequire`; the last three ship `RM_`. One
  example of each is a per-world flag. **Open:** the *"beautifully useless artifact"*
  needs a plot home before it is a def, or it will be built as a trinket.

## 7. Uniquely available

- ⭐ **Soluble minerals** (owner: "a great place to gather") — the planet's
  evaporite treasury: lye salts, nitre, bitterns, crystallization products
  no dayside chemistry makes; **the brine pools are the concentrate wells**,
  dip-harvested from their underwater shores, richest exactly where
  deadliest.
- **The jacketed salvage** — wrecks and skeletons chiseled free in their
  mineral cases; half the value IS the case: a crystal-encased soldier of
  the old war is furniture, relic, and museum in one object.
- **Pillar stone** — the mason's pale mineral, quarried by divers.

## 9. Artistic theme

**"Grey murk, pale pillars, and the dead standing at attention."**

- **Light:** directionless grey-green dimness fading to blind; no beams, no
  glow — until the mark.
- **Palette:** every grey, bone-pale pillar mineral, crystal white on the
  statuary, the murk's green cast — and one impossible saturated color,
  moving, rare.
- **Silhouette language:** verticals in fog — pillars, and the things
  pretending to be pillars; the statuary's human shapes at the edge of
  sight; the shrimp's angular articulation, always half-behind something.
- **Motion:** almost none — drift, the shrimp's careful picking, scrape-dust
  falling; the pools' surfaces barely breathing.
- **Sound:** pressure-muffled near-silence; the crackle of growing crystal;
  scrape-groan carrying for miles through water; and the diver's own
  breathing, suddenly very loud.
