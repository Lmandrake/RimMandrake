# The Grey Deep — definition sheet

_Owner + BENCH, 2026-09-07, two rounds and ratified ("Ok! Write it up!"). Defines
**the bottom of the Grey Sea** — the underwater biome beneath `RUT_GreySea`'s
waters (381 Ocean-painted tiles plus its ice fringe; surface and shore canon
stays with `terminator_sea.md`, which this sheet inherits and never repeats).
🔑 **Implementation is deferred by standing ruling** (owner, 2026-09-07: the
ocean/lake-bottom biomes wait "for those mods when they need it") — this sheet
is the DESIGN, ready for the diving mods' arrival. Thematic handle: **the
statuary** — and its image: **pillars in grey murk, a lake with shores at the
bottom of a sea, and the dead standing in crystal where they sank.**_

## 0. Position and the inherited law

Beneath the Grey Sea (terminator band, θ≈92, bearing 8 — hypersaline, terminal,
shrinking fastest). The base sheet's discipline binds everything below: **one
monoculture, one endemic giant lineage, solitary everything, a roster where
"do we already have one?" usually answers no more.** The Miasma's deltas
remain the lineage's crèche; the open deep is where its adults spend their
centuries alone.

## 1. What it is

Grey-green murk a few meters deep to the eye, floored with strange mineralogy
and death. Salt pillars rise from the bottom like a blind forest; **dense brine
pools — heavier than the murk itself — lie in the hollows with their own
surfaces, their own slow ripples, their own shores, underwater.** Old ruined
vehicles and skeletons stand encased in mineral where they sank. Something
enormous and crusted moves between the pillars, rarely, in a temper. And
through it all, a man-sized skeletal thing picks its careful way, watching you
longer than you've been watching it.

## 2. Planetary position

Terminator sea-bottom × **saturation**: the Grey Sea concentrates toward its
death, and the bottom is where the concentration lives — brine below brine,
minerals crystallizing out of a sea with no exit.

## 3. Driving forces

- ⭐ **Brine below brine** (owner): the pools are saturated-plus — too dense to
  mix, lying in the floor's hollows as **lakes at the bottom of a sea**.
  Everything that enters dies; everything that dies there *keeps*. The pools
  pickle — the sea's display cases.
- **The murk** (owner): low visibility, permanent — sight measured in meters;
  the biome is navigated, not seen.
- **The preservation register**: the planet's third — tar keeps the soft, the
  Scarlands keep the sealed, **the brine keeps the shape.** Anything not
  eaten within days is mineral-jacketed forever: the bottom is a statuary of
  everything that ever sank, from the war's vehicles to yesterday's diver.
- **The masons never stop** (§4) — the pillar forest thickens and shifts;
  charts age.

## 4. What lives here — three, and no more

- **The pillar-mason** — the Grey's monoculture (the base sheet's "different
  one," now named): a crystal-binding film that *builds* — mineral columns
  rising from the floor toward light they never reach. The pillars are the
  only landmarks in the murk, so **all travel below is pillar-to-pillar**:
  waymark navigation through blindness, this world's lane-travel pattern
  (boughways, causeways, root-roads) taken underwater. Divers carve
  waymarks; a lost diver is someone who missed one pillar.
- **The crusted giant** — the endemic lineage (bred at the Miasma's crèches,
  returned here for its solitary centuries), **ill-tempered** (owner): brine
  that hurts, worn as a life condition. Centuries have **encased it in its
  own mineral armor** — a living thing wearing the biome's geology,
  indistinguishable from a drifting pillar until it moves. It **scrapes** —
  grinding crust off against the columns — so *fresh scrape-sign means the
  giant is near*: the tell that lets the observant survive. And in the murk,
  the base sheet's one-saturated-mark rule turns to dread: **you see the
  mate-mark's glow before you see anything else** — one point of impossible
  color approaching through the grey.
- ⭐ **The ossuary shrimp** (owner): **a man-sized, skeletal-seeming shrimp,
  moving along picking at everything it can — strangely intelligent and
  nimble, but shy and evasive.** The undertaker racing the geology: it works
  the freshly dead in the narrow window before the minerals take them, and
  it has watched every diver who ever came down here from two pillars away.
  It is never caught in the open, never fights, and remembers. (Solitary,
  single-spawn, per the inherited bans.)

## 5. Always true

- The pools have shores, and their stillness is the deadliest thing in the
  biome.
- The dead stand where they sank; the statuary only grows.
- The pillars are the map; fresh scrape on one is a warning; a glow in the
  murk is a mood arriving.
- The shrimp saw you first.
- Everything soluble concentrates downward — the bottom is the chemical
  works' floor.
- One day this will all stand in open air (the sea is dying), and someone
  will walk the statuary dry-shod.

## 6. Never true — 🔴 HARD BANS (checkable; the base sheet's bans all inherit)

1. 🔴 **No survivable brine-pool entry** — no def, gear, or story makes the
   pools swimmable; harvest happens at their shores.
2. 🔴 **No schools, swarms, or added kinds** — three residents; a fourth is a
   violation of the inherited roster cap.
3. 🔴 **The shrimp never attacks and is never tamed** — shy and evasive is
   its whole def; a combat or pet variant violates it.
4. 🔴 **No light-source flora, no bioluminescent dressing** — the only glow
   in the Grey Deep is the giant's mark; the murk stays blind.
5. 🔴 **No decay of the encased** — a jacketed thing is preserved forever
   until physically chiseled; no rot-timer touches the statuary.
6. 🔴 **No vanilla-Earth organisms** (standard; the shrimp is
   skeletal-*seeming*, never an Earth shrimp by name or read).

## 7. Uniquely available

- ⭐ **Soluble minerals** (owner: "a great place to gather") — the planet's
  evaporite treasury: lye salts, nitre, bitterns, crystallization products
  no dayside chemistry makes; **the brine pools are the concentrate wells**,
  dip-harvested from their underwater shores, richest exactly where
  deadliest.
- **The jacketed salvage** — wrecks and skeletons chiseled free in their
  mineral cases; half the value IS the case: a crystal-encased soldier of
  the old war is furniture, relic, and museum in one object.
- **Pillar stone** — the mason's pale mineral, quarried by divers.
- **The giant's shed crust** — mineral armor plates, scraped off and
  gathered from pillar bases by whoever dares collect where it grooms.

## 8. Inhabited objects

- **The statuary** — the encased dead of every age: divers upright where
  they drowned, vehicles mid-lurch, skeletons in crystal. The war's losses
  and the sea's whole history, standing.
- **The pillar forest** — with divers' waymarks, old and new, some carved by
  hands whose owners stand encased a few columns away.
- **The pools** — ringed by the thickest statuary (everything that came to
  drink the richness), their shores the harvest ground.
- **Deepwater's soundings** — weighted lines and marker buoys from above:
  the death-ledger's instruments, metering the sea's retreat from the
  surface canon's vigil.

## 9. Artistic theme

**"Grey murk, pale pillars, and the dead standing at attention."**

- **Light:** directionless grey-green dimness fading to blind; no beams, no
  glow — until the mark.
- **Palette:** every grey, bone-pale pillar mineral, crystal white on the
  statuary, the murk's green cast — and one impossible saturated color,
  moving, rare.
- **Silhouette language:** verticals in fog — pillars, and the things
  pretending to be pillars; the statuary's human shapes at the edge of
  sight; the shrimp's angular articulation, always half-behind something.
- **Motion:** almost none — drift, the shrimp's careful picking, scrape-dust
  falling; the pools' surfaces barely breathing.
- **Sound:** pressure-muffled near-silence; the crackle of growing crystal;
  scrape-groan carrying for miles through water; and the diver's own
  breathing, suddenly very loud.
