## 1. What it is

The deltas: a salt-crusted, stilt-rooted mangal forest standing in brackish
channels between a dying sea and the rivers that feed it, under a green-gold
haze of its own breath. Walking is hard, the air hangs thick, and the water
crowds with life — because **everything ends up here: the water, the
sins it carries, and the life running from the salt.** And threaded through the
stink, improbably, beauty: rainbow-hued plants of genuine loveliness, young
animals everywhere, and the stubborn sense that the place is on the verge of
something.

## 2. Planetary position

Mid-dayside coastal lowlands, arc 18.9–75.0, the mouths of the world's water. The
reasoned intersection: **river meets brine × the refugee crush × the compost of
a war.** Two engines, and they are the same engine:

- **The drain** — every drop of moving dayside water ends here or dies trying:
  the Contagion's sterilized mulch, the Greentide's washed-out fertility, the
  salt, and the war's biological leftovers, carried downriver for millennia and
  settled into the muck.
- **The lifeboat** — the Grey Sea is being pickled alive in its own basin, and
  the only refuge is the fresh end of the gradient: **an entire sea's endemic
  line crowding into 93 tiles of brackish fringe.** The record density is not
  abundance. It is refugee crowding — whole biomes smashing themselves into
  this tiny one, trying to find a way to survive (owner).

## 3. Driving forces

- **The breath-tide.** No moon, no clockwork tide — but when the terminator
  storms pile the Grey Sea against the Salt Gate shore, the sea **surges**:
  days apart, never on schedule. Brine shoves miles up the channels; the
  rivers shove back. 🔴 The salt line — the most important line in the biome —
  **moves miles per surge**, and nothing here ever reaches equilibrium. The
  physics itself is "being worked out."
- **The gradient as geography.** Every map has a fresh→brine axis; trees,
  fauna, disease load and muck-color all sort along it; the surge redraws it
  around whatever the player has built.
- **The Working.** The muck is the planet's compost heap at fever heat — the
  war's engineered biology, the sterile mulch, the refugees' dead, fermenting
  together under maximum selection pressure. Undirected, mindless, mostly
  failing — **the diseases ARE the failed experiments** — and occasionally
  not failing. The 15-day clock and the hope are the same process.
- **The trees run the plumbing.** Zero rain: the mangals drink river and sea
  directly, straining brine through bark and sweating salt from their leaves
  (Earth's halophytes prove the trick). The stilt-root maze is the terrain,
  the architecture, and the nursery cage all at once.

## 4. How the biology adapted — the three rings of refuge

- **The nursery.** The Grey Sea's roster breeds here — the root-maze is the
  crèche of every horror the sea still holds (`sea_beasts_roster.md`'s formal
  breeding ground: the young here, the adults in the terminator waters).
  Juveniles swarm the shallows, small and almost charming and cheap to hunt —
  and every scavenger knows the arithmetic: **don't kill what's small here;
  its mother is the reason ships sink.** The parents' desperate gamble with
  their own young is the biome's soul in miniature (owner): send them into
  the muck, and they die or come back stronger.
- **The warden mothers.** The adults that stay: brine-broken elders too old
  for the strengthening sea, hauled into the shallows to end their centuries
  guarding the crèches. Enormous, stationary, lethal within reach, and
  tragic — the sea's memory dying in its lifeboat. Placed set-pieces, never
  random spawns.
- **The stranded.** The surge's orphans: endemics cut off in pools when the
  salt line moves, forced ashore between waters — gills going leathery, fins
  splaying into feet that don't quite work. Creatures that *look
  transitional*: evolution auditioning, generation by generation, in the
  pools behind the player's base. The almost-breakthrough given a face.
- ⭐ **The crusty arthropods** (owner) — the small creature of the biome:
  little armored scuttlers in uncountable numbers, **eaten by everything**,
  the trophic floor that supports the great beasts' growth. Harvestable,
  renewable, faintly comic; the entire food pyramid stands on their backs.
- **The composters.** Burrowing muck-workers digesting the sin-soup; their
  castings are **delta loam** (§7). The hopeful mirror of the Scarlands'
  mortuary guild.
- **The fever-swarms** (insectoid `karr-` clade at the roster) — the disease
  vector and the mangals' only pollinator, one and the same swarm. You cannot
  have the trees without the fever.

## 4b. Fever-forged — the disease that pays

⭐ **Ruled (owner: "survive and grow stronger — very wild nature").** Uniquely
in the Miasma, surviving a disease sometimes leaves something behind: a
hardened immunity, a small permanent resilience, once in a great while
something genuinely strange. Sickness as crucible — the worst place to get
sick is the only place getting sick can pay. (`MIASMA_MECHANICS_1`; the boon
tables are the roster/mechanics item's to tune.)

## 5. Always true

- Everything ends up here — water, sins, refugees; density is crowding, not
  plenty.
- The salt line moves with every surge; no map state is permanent.
- The air is thick with sickness, and the sickness is the Working's
  spillage; survival sometimes forges.
- The young are everywhere, and the young are hope.
- The rainbow flora is genuinely beautiful and genuinely benign (§6).
- The trees drink what nothing else can; the roots are the only ground.
- The place is on the verge of something. It is not ready. It's being worked
  out.

## 6. Never true — 🔴 HARD BANS (linter-checkable)

1. 🔴 **No gene machine** — no mechanism-tier registry, reactor, or directed
   engine; the Slime owns that miracle. The Working stays undirected.
2. 🔴 **The rainbow flora never lies** — VERY beautiful and hopeful (owner),
   and always genuinely benign: never a trap, never a mimic, never toxic.
   (The deliberate inversion of the Scarlands' rainbow pools — there color is
   reaction and acid; here it is the truth of hope. Both stay absolute.)
3. 🔴 **No medicine economy** — ruled cut (owner: "we have given medicine
   many times today"): no signature pharmacopoeia, cure-herb, or medical
   trade good defs here; the silt's value is §7's attar instead.
4. 🔴 **No clockwork tide** — the surge is storm-driven and irregular,
   never scheduled.
5. 🔴 **No rain** — the swamp is fed by river and sea only.
6. 🔴 **No virus authorship in player-facing text** — engineered plagues may
   be known; whose, never (the war's §GM gates own it).
7. 🔴 **No vanilla-Earth flora or fauna** (standard eviction).

## 7. Uniquely available

- ⭐ **The attar** (owner's pick, replacing the cut medicine): the delta silt
  refines into **an expensive oil that restores beauty** — something
  artistic. Artists use it as a glaze that deepens any work's beauty; the
  vain use it as a balm that fades scars and weathering. The planet's
  loveliest luxury, pressed from its ugliest mud — the biome's thesis as a
  trade good. (Refinement is still-work: Bitterleaf's economy, ready-made.)
- **Delta loam** — the composters' castings: clean, fertile, exportable soil
  from the sickest ground; sits in the same economy as the Rot's fungal-soil
  trade.
- **The arthropod harvest** — the trophic floor as a food industry.
- **The flotsam yard** — everything the rivers carry that doesn't rot washes
  into the roots: the planet's driftwood-and-salvage beach.
- **Fever-forging** (§4b) — for those who dare to use it.
- **The nursery trade** — eggs and young of the sea's roster, priced in the
  mothers' memory.

## 8. Inhabited objects

- ⭐ **The Wildsteam pilgrimage** (owner, verbatim in intent): the Miasma is
  **feared but worshipped by the Wildsteam — "come here when all hope is
  lost, and you either die or return with hope found."** A desperate gamble,
  the same one the creatures take with their own young. Their shrine-stills
  stand at Bitterleaf and along the pilgrim channels; a Wildsteam pilgrim in
  the muck is on the worst day of their life, on purpose.
- **Bitterleaf** — stilt-and-steam architecture over the channels; stills
  running attar, liquor, and hope.
- **The Deepwater vigil** — a Require faction watching a sea die; their
  posts at the mouths measure the brine year by year, and whatever they know
  about the shrinking, they knew it here first.
- **The Jawa** — flotsam-combing crews working the root-lines after every
  surge; the delta is a harvest that restocks itself.
- **The nurseries and the warden mothers** — placed, mapped, and named; the
  biome's sacred-adjacent ground (harming a crèche is not sacrilege in the
  oasis sense, but everything living remembers it).

## 9. Artistic theme

**"Rainbow blooms in green-gold haze — hope, composting."**

- **Light:** low warm sun through the miasma's green-gold; water-glitter in
  the channels; everything slightly softened, as if seen through breath.
- **Palette:** muck-browns and mangal greens, salt-crust white on the sea
  side — and the rainbow flora burning through it: genuine, saturated,
  beautiful color that means exactly what it seems to mean.
- **Silhouette language:** stilt-roots and their reflections; the warden
  mothers' half-sunk bulks; juveniles boiling the shallows; steam threads
  off the stills.
- **Motion:** constant small life (arthropods, swarms, young), the slow
  heave of a surge arriving, the salt line's creep.
- **Sound:** the wet click-and-chitter of the arthropod floor, swarm-hum,
  channel-slap; a surge announces itself as a long exhale from seaward. The
  **miasma weather** mutes and thickens everything — the biome breathing.
