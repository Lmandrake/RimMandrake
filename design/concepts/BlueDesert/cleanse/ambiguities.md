# Settled ambiguities and the arguments that changed the definition

Every choice here is the Designer's unless explicitly attributed to an existing answer. No question was sent; the lone owner-page question records a conflict and the default proceeds. The important later evidence is `design/RimMandrake/designer_codex_2026-11-09/BlueDesert/ANSWERS_OWNER.md` (November 10 answers), not the rehearsal's earlier question guesses. No waiver was found that must be re-opened. Already cut soundscape/road/drip are not candidates.

1. **“Hydrocarbon ice.”** Reading A: the ground and mined blocks explode. Reading B: water ice supports hydrocarbon life. A makes normal water mining itself an unexplained bomb. Take B: item XML expressly says water ice is nonexplosive; the biome sheet distinguishes ancient water ice from living chemistry. Compose shorthand is figurative, not a new chemistry rule. B1/B7 and R1 pin the material picture.
2. **“Mine slowly and carefully,” “passion's heat.”** A: pick speed accumulates a special quarry heat stat; B: prepare containment and escape before ordinary mining. A adds a player-speed minigame. Take B from later owner answer one: separate dangerous growth and secure escape routes; ice is an ordinary mineral. The words about passion are figurative desire/overreach, not a new heat source. Prepared crew mining continuously can pass. No old tree leaf freezes the old speculation.
3. **Careful versus merely absent.** A: don't bring any pawn near fuel; B: the crew can do the actual useful extraction after preparation. A makes avoidance the only “strategy.” Take B: B4 pairs identical extraction and ignition tasks, changing preparation rather than attack strength or leaving hazards off.
4. **A “safe gap.”** A: a drawn strip of bare ground; B: no fuel/heat path into the retreat including animals and cargo. A permits a dead animal or carried wax to bridge it. Take B, delegated from coupled signature; the containment sweep determines feasible geometry. No fixed spacing becomes an owner bar.
5. **“Everything becomes a bomb.”** A: guaranteed map-wide destruction; B: connected fuel releases violently and may propagate; empty ice is not fuel. A makes containment meaningless and conflicts with the owner’s later preparation answer. Take B: water/metal/declared inorganic carbon remain inert; a deliberate ignition in a highly connected field can be catastrophic, but routine predation must not erase the biome repeatedly. This is not a covert reduction of blast numbers.
6. **Low flammability versus immunity.** A: cold plants cannot ignite; B: ignition is difficult but damage/heat can start the explosive chain. A would let a harvest test prove safety by making everything invulnerable. Take B from the life brief's engine facts: low Flammability is authored, ambient cold alone is no ignition ban. Sustained warmth and direct damage are separate paths.
7. **“Warning,” “a beat to run.”** A: an audible crack alone, or a warning on the same tick as unavoidable destruction; B: locatable pre-ignition state and an available stop-heat/retreat response in the reachable-site range. A fails offscreen cargo. Take B, delegated from meaningful preparation. Immediate EMP/damage attacks have described triggers rather than a mandatory invented delay. The whole map is not promised a pause before each blast.
8. **Cooling after warning.** A: cooling always unwinds any already lit wick; B: pre-ignition warming can be arrested, but irreversible wick/blast has begun once explicitly signalled. A contradicts the current vanilla wick and makes risk trivial. Take B; label the transition and preserve progress. Healing/resetting a native is not an implicit extinguisher.
9. **Reload during a warm countdown.** A: the unscribed field starts afresh; B: hazard progress has continuity. A turns saving into refrigeration. Take B from the signature inference and actual `CompPlantCharge` unscribed-field risk. A model must enumerate the boundary, not just cold-start load.
