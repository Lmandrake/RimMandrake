# Stillsand — proposed Design Version 0.1.0

Stillsand is an exposed ochre plain of deep sand, sparse living things and travelling giants under a fixed sun. Distant rock islands offer caves to explore and secure, rather than automatic safety or guaranteed treasure. Useful discoveries become supplies or equipment through ordinary collection, work or trade to provision the next expedition or outpost.

These are the Designer's definitions and scope choices under this cleanse brief. They are proposals, not an applied root change, owner certification, current proof or enjoyment verdict. No game, bridge, art or source work is part of this pass. Earlier Conductor instructions are authoritative historical decisions: this proposal deliberately replaces their blanket retention of built capabilities using the later cleanse mandate. It preserves their practical expedition emphasis and the distinction between ordinary animal products and later shared film. Following the review, it also defers the workshop named in CONDUCTOR:1794805294378261874: the owner’s practical-fortune answer makes lenses optional, and discoveries remain useful without that additional economy. It does not revise a standing ruling, reopen a recorded waiver (none is recorded), or claim the earlier Cold Reader exam passes the new contract.

**Land:** broad walkable sand and deep drift, corrugation, sparse rock, occasional physical bones and wreckage, dry weather and shared geometric sunlight/shade. Roads already allowed remain allowed; no new road or river is authored.

**Life:** existing patched native residents and ordinary plants, their food, reproduction, predation, burrowing, flight, shelter seeking and ordinary products. Giants carry moving shade; small things use shelter or depth. Roster membership is retained, including campaign animals. A species being kept does not keep a special quest or incident about it.

**The signature:** exposed crossings lead to uncertain caves which, once explored and physically secured, supply refuge and practical discoveries to provision the next undertaking through ordinary collection, work or trade.

**Runner-up:** the dune-gale carry/return/aftermath cycle loses because it creates another hazard, recovery and renewal economy alongside the expedition loop. Its ordinary wind and sand dependencies remain; the custom cycle defaults off for next version.

## Why a player reaches for it

Choose Stillsand to make distance, carried supplies and a known refuge matter. The prediction is that returning with something useful makes the next crossing feel earned; enjoyment remains a prediction. This version is whole without rituals, scripted leviathans, new quests or specialist sensing weapons. It still has ordinary predators and an uncertain cave. Vanilla extreme desert supplies scarcity and normal work; Stillsand adds the rock/cave/discovery loop and depth-and-shade life.

“Ocean”, “island”, “harbour” and “fortune” are metaphors. Ocean is extensive exposed sand between destinations, not literal water or empty terrain with nothing living. An island is a rock outcrop, not a generated sea tile. A harbour is a place the player has inspected, sheltered and defended; it is not an immunity area. Fortune is a usable change in provisioning or trade, not a wealth threshold. The counts, speeds and weights currently in defs are regression readings, not the definition of these words.

## Using it

**First hour.** Arrive carrying ordinary starting provisions. Locate actual cover, read exposure, inspect the nearest approach and the rock mouth, and decide whether exploration is affordable. An unfamiliar cave may contain a predator or hatchable clutch; a mouth is not a safety certificate. A roof or parasol blocks Direct Sun even at low solar elevation, but the thermometer and vanilla heatstroke still matter. The arrival guidance says this and never claims rock cools the air. No Debt obligation, swarm, Muurrok visit or gale starts at default settings.

**Routine.** Cross sand while reading wakes and listening to nearby movement. Swimmers surface on hard ground unless active drift makes it sand again. Oommok shade moves with its body and is temporary cover, not a taxi or a safe convoy command. Explore the chamber, remove actual threats, retain a protected route and make an ordinary stockpile/sleeping space. Harvest, fish, hunt, mine, salvage, haul, craft or trade what is useful; accept that a taken cave may add no supplies. Revisit to rest or collect the remaining finite contents, not to reroll its reward.

**At a 40-colonist scale.** Use ordinary hauling, work priorities, storage filters and bills. Workers share finite inputs and work sites; no new autonomous sieve chore is required. A prepared colony can haul finite cache contents, collect edible products, cook using ordinary vanilla equipment, allocate materials to ordinary construction or make an actual trade, and recover from a worker interruption without resetting the discovery. This does not promise a sparse desert feeds such a colony indefinitely: imported provisions, trade and deliberate agriculture remain normal colony decisions. Wildlife density is not increased to feed the settlement. A busy stockpile cannot silently destroy a collected find when placement is blocked. No solar workshop is required.

**Day one and day one hundred.** Resource scarcity and ordinary hunger persist. Some plants grow slowly; a century of ticks cannot reroll a cave or create supplies on reload. A saved empty cave stays empty. A roof protects workers from Direct Sun without cooling ordinary air. Special solar apparatus and receiver-dependent production remain deferred.

## What changes in the colony's story

The colony acquires a known route, a secured refuge and a material reason to return. A discovered seep remains environmental brine and a food-web/refuge site, not guaranteed drinking water or treasure. A grotto supplies mineable biosilica for sale to an actual accepting buyer; eggs can be eaten or carried; a sealed cache supplies ordinary components, steel or food. A cave already taken redirects effort instead of pretending every expedition succeeds. Those are the retained ordinary cave rows, not a promise about their numeric weights or an entitlement to useful loot on every map.

The furnace, lens bench, solar oven, plain still, brine dipping and refined glass/lens recipes move to next version, default off. Their deletion does not remove any necessary step from crossing, securing a cave or using a find: cache food/components/steel, edible clutches, ordinary catches and real trade already provision another undertaking. Vanilla cooking can prepare food. Naming a workshop in the delegated root does not make its loss a failure of that signature. This is my revised scope decision under the cleanse mandate, not an owner quotation or retirement.

Shared MovingDunes raw glass-sand clearing yield remains its existing dependency behaviour; ordinary biosilica harvest/mining and pearl catches remain natural products. Those raw products do not imply a retained lens-manufacturing promise. No alternate fine-sand supplier has been established: the local sieve produces it, but an ExoticMisc trade tag does not prove a trader stocks it. That bootstrap belongs to the deferred workshop tickets, not first-version acceptance. The ordinary grotto trade route still requires a real accepting buyer and measured exchange, never just a price field.

## The costs

Crossings cost time, ordinary food and exposure; attacks cost health and can kill. Discoveries cost exploration, defence, carrying capacity, extraction and ordinary work. Selling a find requires a real accepting buyer and an actual exchange; no trader visit or colony self-sufficiency is guaranteed. Ordinary eggs and sacs keep their existing ingestion and optional DBH integration independently of Debt or pouring. DBH is an optional third-party mod, not a DLC. There is no new thirst need.

Solar recovery is outside this first version. If later enabled, the existing still selects DBH bottles when supported; without DBH, RM_StilledWater has no ingestible block and is only carried/trade stock, not a drink or cooling treatment. Its use must be described that way until a separately scoped change supplies ordinary ingestion. No such change is commissioned here. No live crop-water pool is inferred from a wetness expiry timestamp.

## When it goes wrong and the readable sign

Exposed pawns show ordinary heat/exposure status; adapted natives can use their own environmental water, including brine, under the standing ruling. A visible wake, breach or rumble identifies concealed movement without identifying the exact species or promising safety when silent. Ordinary attacks and kills leave readable body/remains/messages; damage cannot be credited to a disappearing actor. A flyer flies under the standing flight system. Offmap disappearance is not a default Stillsand danger now that gale carry and event takes are off.

A dead worker releases ordinary reservations; another worker can continue valid collection, hauling or a bill. Blocked placement preserves the find or an accountable pending product rather than consuming it into nothing. Deferred solar appliances cannot silently supply the settlement or start jobs at defaults. A roofed cave preserves eligible contents only in its recorded cells after actual occupancy no longer holds the den. Taking an object out, carrying it, losing its roof or disabling preservation releases only the state this feature owns. Items already disabled by another owner are not acquired. The status/tooltip must distinguish cave preservation from refrigeration and from ordinary ambient heat; T_CAVE supplies that reader.

## The edges

No weather-cleared acceptance scenes count for the whole bar. Ordinary Clear, DryThunderstorm and Sandstorm are land; the custom dune gale is an extra. There is no magical safe-island flag, new enemy AI, research tree, planet generation, tile repainting, faction settlement work, alternative planet or multiplayer promise. Existing local-map furnishing hooks may lay out rock/cave/ordinary debris when a local map is supplied; that does not authorize planetary generation or a seed search. Models enumerate synthetic *situations*, not worlds. Zero world tiles for this biome are expected and irrelevant to acceptance.

No cave is promised on a local layout too thin or blocked to fit one safely. The existing largest suitable outcrop and optional rockless tor policy remains an attackable implementation choice. Such a case must be reported as no cave, not a painted doorway into an unusable room. At regional scale the authored desert must still have destinations separated by exposed travel; isolated no-cave layouts do not excuse a desert with no useful destinations anywhere. The layout model identifies this boundary rather than rolling new planets until it passes.

No general outdoor no-rot rule is retained. Ordinary dead giants remain readable under normal corpse handling; optional seasonal conversion and special desiccation move together. Existing HugeThings-classified titan sites remain solely HugeThings-owned with normal temperature-driven meat spoilage and its existing competition; this is a dependency boundary, not permission to rewrite it. Basic static bones remain landmarks and geometric cover. Harps, extra fossil scatter and new conversion effects are not needed to read them.

## The settings

Shipped defaults enable land, ordinary resident behaviour, geometric sun/shade, ordinary sand signs, basic cave/discovery/preservation and ordinary collection/use/trade of discoveries. Solar tables, recipes, ovens, dipping and stills are next-version extras, off. Settings group those major features with per-feature on/off and appropriate tuning, search, scrolling, collapse and reset. Each control reports whether it applies now, next activation or next local-map furnishing; never “worldgen”. Reset uses the proposed defaults, not the older source defaults. All-off leaves loaded defs and a readable ordinary desert; turning a feature off does not delete pawns or corrupt existing saves.

Every deferred extra has a separately labelled “Next version” default-off gate. The campaign tier cannot reactivate Debt or a cave/event row merely by loading its def. Recommended retirements are parked off until the owner decides; obsolete heat semantics are prohibited at every setting. Existing outside-biome controls stay off and labelled unsupported; no new cross-biome switch is built. Exact prior user settings, not shipped defaults, are restored after any test. Existing active extra entities on an old save finish or are safely released with a sign; there is no promise of enabling an unfinished extra as a supported first-version experience.

## First hour story

The party sees a low fixed sun and bare sand between itself and a rock mouth. A wake crosses the approach. They put up a quick roof, check remaining ambient heat, and send an equipped scout to inspect the cave instead of moving every bed into an unseen den. The roof works despite the low angle; finding rock has not yet won safety.

## Ordinary week story

After clearing the real occupant, they stock the roofed chamber and haul out a useful clutch and biosilica. They eat or cook the ordinary food, reserve cache steel/components for building, and take mined biosilica to an actual accepting buyer when available. The seep is habitat and potential shelter, not a free drinking-water source. A second sortie passes the same already-emptied chamber without a fresh reward. The colony keeps its prepared refuge and plans the next destination from actual supplies.

## Worst day story

A sandstorm catches the haulers while a moving drift reaches an approach that had been hard rock. They pause an exposed collection trip; a wake shows that hidden movement is possible again. A worker dies in an ordinary attack and leaves readable remains. Other workers release the failed reservation, secure the approach and resume the ordinary trip when the approach is safe. No ritual or scripted rescue is necessary to recover.

## Season's turn story

The sun still occupies its original place. Living things have fed, sheltered, reproduced or left; the settled cave retains its history and scoped preserved contents. Supplies have actually been spent and transformed. Reloading a save from the middle and preparing storage and ordinary work before the next exploration leaves those consequences intact. Emptiness still separates destinations; neither a carpet of new life nor an endless cache fountain has replaced the desert.

## Second attack: the laziest build

A screenshot of a beautiful empty desert could satisfy the opening sentence while hiding a dead food web, unroofed chamber and unusable discoveries. The bars therefore distinguish a full-map look, actual life transitions, connected chamber/approach, ordinary input-to-use work and scoped preservation, then require the season with everything enabled. A reward added by reflection cannot satisfy provisioning. A static shade blob cannot satisfy moving cover. A no-op ritual toggle cannot prove scope removal. The models settle unresolved geometry, ownership, provisioning and whole-map feedback before expensive live tuning; none is claimed built or calibrated here.

Definition boundary for the whole run: all viable supplied cave layouts, including viable tor layouts, receive the full-season/reload/reordered undertaking. A physically unsuitable no-cave local layout is a negative geometry control, not a passing cave-use scene. The region cannot be excused if the model finds no viable useful destinations at all. This restriction follows the existing local furnishing policy and does not authorize a new world generator or seed selection.
