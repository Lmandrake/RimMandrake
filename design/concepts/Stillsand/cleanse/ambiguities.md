## Reviewer correction and second attack

I accept all three refusals. The original workshop deletion arguments were circular: they protected the delegated sentence naming production, not a necessary step of the expedition experience. The revised B_SUPPLY requires usable finds or actual ordinary exchange, with no solar workshop. Five existing capability rows move to next version (glass materials/recipes, sun tables, solar oven, brine dipping and solar still); all have switch-off tickets. Natural raw sand yield, biosilica, pearl catches and edible eggs/sacs remain with their existing land/life/dependency owners. The seep is environmental habitat and possible refuge, never a guaranteed water fortune.

The alleged alternate fine-sand trade supply has no verified offer; the first version no longer relies on it. The feature_boundary argument now names independent ordinary provision routes, while actual buyer access/accounting remains unknown in the supply meeting and M_SUPPLY. The non-DBH water contradiction is corrected: deferred still stock has no ingestion; retained eggs/sacs do. No new drinking hook or thermal potion is specified.

Laziest-build attack after revision: a builder might gate buildings yet leave free lenses in trade or jobs running on saved appliances. The new tickets gate construction, recipe/offer/row producers and consumers, include fresh-map off/on state reads and safe old-save handling, and keep shared raw resources separate. Another builder might call biosilica valuable without finding a buyer: B_SUPPLY requires an actual ordinary sale/purchase or direct usable supplies, and T_SUPPLY rejects a mere tradeability flag. None of these arguments claims current game proof.
