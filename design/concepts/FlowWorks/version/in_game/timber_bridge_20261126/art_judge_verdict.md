# Art judge verdict: timber bridge redraw (RIMMANDRAKE_REDRAW_TIMBER_BRIDGE_FROM_HIS_THREE_PHOTOS)

Judge: blind (did not make the art), claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-26. One look.
Brief: design/RimMandrake/art_judge_brief.md (B1a, B2, B4, B6, D3, D4, E4).
Owner note (E4), 2026-11-25: "For the Timber bridge, it's just all wrong. Here are the right looks to emulate:
[Image #13] [Image #14] [Image #15] No ropes tied to the ground for the wood bridge."
References looked at: showings/FLOWWORKS_WALK3_20261125/13.png, 14.png, 15.png; rejected old art scratch/timber/current.png.

## Overall: KEEP

Every capture reads as a timber beam bridge of the kind in his photos: dense plank deck laid across the
direction of travel, heavy timber curbs along both sides, ends seated on a timber sill on the bank, and
(rails on) a post-and-rail fence over the span like photo 14. No rope, no bollard, no anchor in any capture.
The rejected art (bollard anchors with ropes, plank atlas patches) is gone entirely.

## One line per capture

- judge_scrapper_rails_off_with_pawn_below.png: KEEP. Red-brown cross planks, bolted timber curbs both sides,
  darker sill block at each end on the bank past the pit edge (photo 13's sill). No rope.
- judge_scrapper_rails_on.png: KEEP. Same deck; four-bay post-and-rail fence each side over the pit span, far
  fence standing up from the north curb, near fence over the south curb; posts meet the deck. Reads as photo 14.
- judge_scrapper_two_wide_rails_on.png: KEEP. Two-cell-wide deck stays one continuous plank field with curbs
  only on the outer edges; no seam down the middle between the two lanes; fences on outer edges only.
- judge_scrapper_north_south_rails_on.png: KEEP. Planks turn to run east-west (across travel), curbs and rails
  run north-south, angled braces at the pit edges, sills on both banks. Direction correct.
- judge_industrial_rails_on.png: KEEP. Darker timber planks, dark iron rails and curb straps with brass/amber
  clamp bands. Deck reads as timber with steel fittings. Distinct from the other three.
- judge_modern_rails_on.png: KEEP. Pale golden pine planks and rails, clean and plain; reads as a modern
  park-walk timber bridge (B6: no paint-red tier marker).
- judge_futuristic_rails_on.png: KEEP. Saturated orange varnished timber with pale metallic trim lines on the
  curbs. Reads as timber; distinct from Modern by tone and trim, though the gap is the smallest of the four.

## Flaws (refused)

None. No seam that reads as a break, no floating part, no rope, no wrong plank direction, nothing that reads
as not-a-bridge. B1a (no outlines), B4 (colour reads as wood), D4 (no baked ground props) pass on all seven.

## Polish (blocks nothing, files no ticket)

- polish: faint butt-joint lines in the curb timbers at cell boundaries (scrapper rails off, middle of the
  span). They read as timber joints, not as a tile seam.
- polish: Modern and Futuristic are the closest pair (both orange-gold wood); Futuristic could lean further
  from Modern (cooler trim, darker or greyer stain).
- polish: on Industrial the curbs read as dark steel channel more than timber; the planks still carry
  "timber". A timber curb with iron straps would sit closer to photo 13.
- polish: east-west fences stop at the pit edges, while north-south curb rails run the full length onto the
  sills. Photo 13 carries the curb to the ends. Both are plausible.
- polish: near-fence posts hang a few pixels below the south deck edge on Scrapper and Modern (reads as
  fascia-mounted posts like photo 14, so not a fault).
- note (not art): in the rails-off capture the pawn standing in the pit south of the bridge draws its head
  over the deck. This is the vanilla pawn-over-building draw order, not this art.
