# Material ambiguities settled by the Designer

All selections below are delegated calls. The evidence determines the default; none is written as new owner speech. The scope is Design Version 0.1.0, not permanent deletion. No question is pending. Historical open questions whose obvious reading is settled by a later ruling are not raised again.

1. **“Four water biomes” / one signature.** Reading A: four independent kits may each retain every special system. Reading B: the ruled bundle retains four lands/life casts but chooses one player chain for this pass. Consequence: A keeps the light economy, ship crust, cold-defence loops and Scald extras together. Take B under the cleansing card. The current/bank/sink chain is the signature; the others move. The September four-biomes-one-mod ruling remains intact.

2. **A sea / surface / floor / ice.** A: a playable place on top of the water or fuel. B: only a neighbouring beach or the sea floor. Consequence: A builds an extra map and puts weather and animals on a location the owner removed. Take B: November 1 floor/beach ruling and October 28 “sea IS its floor.” Chill ice is floor terrain or beach, never a new lake-top settlement. Ship travel uses the sea-floor planet layer; no pawn dive menu or hatch.

3. **“Perched” / “spills” in Scald descriptions.** A: outgoing planetary rivers. B: terminal volcanic basin with no outflow. Consequence: A changes the world’s hydrology or promises an outlet. Take B from the amended world definition and the_scald.md: eight rivers arrive; nothing leaves but sky. The older surviving prose is not authority. No world authoring occurs on this pass.

4. **“Clean steam” / Scald exposure.** A: clean means totally harmless temperature; B: free from the lake’s fouling while ordinary hot air can hurt. Consequence: A confuses purity with thermal safety, while the current custom hediff creates another currency. Take B using Direct Sun’s October 19 hot-air-on-thermometer ruling. Defer the custom steam exposure clock and gear progression; keep presentation and shared thermal/contact conditions. Do not infer a new biome-specific sun.

5. **“Cool bathing margin.”** A: the source description’s drinkable water. B: cooler traversal/resting contrast that is still Scald body water. Consequence: A defeats the frozen never-potable ban. Take B; generic lake classification/drinking jobs must agree, not just prose. A condenser’s distinct distillate does not render the lake drinkable; condenser manufacture itself is a later extra.

6. **“Steam sky.”** A: attach the lock to every Scald floor. B: beach steam above, calm submerged bubbles below. Consequence: A puts sky weather underwater and can fake a working shore carrier using an inaccessible background biome. Take B from the October 23 source-wiring owner-card note and November 1 floor/beach ruling. Actual delivery to neighbouring beach maps is unknown until the dependency is resolved; ticket T45.

7. **“Cold” / “fuel snow” / aurora.** A: every Chill effect is necessary floor weather. B: ordinary cold, oxygen-poor fuel/ice and drowned aurora are land; surge harvesting and warmth-triggered defence are extra loops. Consequence: A retains multiple signatures. Take B from the cleanse distinction, retaining the floor agenda’s actual environmental contract. Historical statements about built foreign features remain unknown here, not “unbuilt.”

8. **“Sparse” / “crowded.”** A: a bare Grey floor and a uniformly crowded Twilight. B: Grey may have crowded mineral forms and dispersed sessile life; Twilight shoals/gardens cluster at light while dark ground and paths remain. Consequence: A empties the Grey art or covers the Twilight bank routes. Take B from Grey’s mineral additions and explicit sessile-layer explanation, and Twilight’s light/dark descriptions. These are visual/behaviour comparisons, not density targets. No quota or roster eviction.

9. **“Fish” / “alive and moving.”** A: every rare-table object needs an animal and sessile animals must locomote. B: living fish-sized catches need living floor counterparts; mineral/salvage loot is not an organism and sessile animals can remain rooted while alive. Consequence: A invents creatures for relics or destroys the sessile clade; a too-lazy B leaves an item in place of life. Take B from the catch ruling plus the explicitly approved Grey sessile layer. Prove actual alive/feeding/growing/reacting state, not just a static def. Native water use covers every native roster entry, not only a convenient fish sample.

10. **“No swimmable river.”** A: a visual mud strip that occasionally pushes items. B: unsecured ordinary visitors cannot harmlessly cross the carrying centre. Consequence: A can pass the pure kernel while pawns cross between registration and first displacement. Take B from Twilight’s hard ban. M2 sweeps entry phase and ordinary path speed; T44 requires a real interrupted/carrying crossing rather than a debug teleport. A retreatable margin and a genuinely hazardous centre are compatible; a safe bank route remains outside surge reach.

11. **“Braided” / “downhill” / “current boundary.”** A: straight spokes to the lowest cell with hidden field bytes, regardless of walls or terrain. B: a connected carrying field whose source-to-sink route, visible bed, bank and obstructions agree. Consequence: A produces wall crossings, contradictory stakes or harmless-looking live water. Take B from the player river experience; the existing straight-line algorithm is R2’s attackable build choice, not an owner law. No new fluid simulation is mandated. M2 decides whether the current algorithm can supply B.

12. **“The ship never lands astride a channel.”** A: exclude a radius around the obsolete hatch. B: protect the actual reserved hull, unloading edge and bank access against calm and surge. Consequence: A strands the crew despite a passing centre-point check. Take B from ship-or-nothing plus the safe-arrival promise. Current generator explicitly searches RM_SeaDiveHatch; that is concrete evidence of the mismatch. Geometry export must include the real ship reservation.

13. **“Recoverable” / “indoors” / “rescued.”** A: any Roofed cell or any unspawned pawn. B: removed from dangerous liquid to safe shelter/carriage. Consequence: A heals beneath a roof over the sink or while a dead rescuer’s holder is still unsafe. Take B as delegated fairness inference from recoverability and readable consequences. Source already improves off-map recovery, but does not prove safe carriage. A blocked sink retains a findable victim and a placement failure rather than choosing an unchecked fallback. Failed rescue may kill and leaves readable remains.

14. **“Lost” / death / transfer.** A: a harsher setting may silently destroy a pawn or let the old map keep moving it. B: any loss still has a sign, and map/holder identity is authoritative. Consequence: A contradicts a standing ruling. Take B; default remains Recoverable and optional scar/loss variants move off. No setting authorises invisible loss. Source map-ownership/terminal-registration fixes are already visible and need regression evidence, not another stale bug report.

15. **“Waveglass is one organism” / gardener.** A: the lid is generic roof decoration and the keeper can be omitted. B: minimal persistent keeper/tending remains inseparable from the living roof. Consequence: A violates the frozen no-roof-without-gardener ban. Take B, but do not retain boss events, theatrical passing shadows or a lid-dark crisis on that basis. No local keeper implementation was found; first locate foreign work, then implement the minimal contract if genuinely absent. T43, not a guessed source path.

16. **“Temporary wells” / “years” / “week.”** A: source’s short lifetime or an inert freeze option defines the canon. B: finite years-scale light drift from the frozen sheet, with actual boundary behaviour proven separately. Consequence: A turns transient land into permanent sacred property or quietly changes its timescale. Take B; preserve lifecycle, warning and replacement without tenancy. Numeric lifetimes are checks/tuning under that source constraint. Near-expiry honest saves prove closure; they do not prove a new floor’s well naturally turned over during a short season.

17. **“Light is life.”** A: every cultivation tool, grazer, charge/wilt rule and seeding callback is indispensable. B: ordinary natural glow, eligible habitat, native growth and temporary wells suffice. Consequence: A uses code coupling as a scope argument. Take B from the cleanse deletion test. WellLedger.Opened/Closed directly call TwilightWellFlora; pressure calculations inspect player mobile glowers. Decouple those calls; preserve ordinary habitat placement and static glows for the same species. Removing the callbacks cannot remove the life roster. M5 checks that alternative.

18. **“A populated ark” / “settlement.”** A: keep an entire Compact town and its commerce; B: retain the frozen minimal inhabited dwellings/plots/moorings/lamplight through the campaign dependency, while deferring full town/tenancy systems. Consequence: A adds another economy; too-small scenery-only B erases the promised reveal. Take B from the_twilight_deep.md’s explicit distinction between initial inhabited holdings and the later full settlement. RM does not name the campaign or acquire hostile Compact raids. This necessary reveal is explicitly retained, not hidden as generic land clutter. T47.

19. **“Animal mechanic” / “ordinary life.”** A: all species-attached comps are automatically life. B: retain ordinary bodies, normal predation/feeding, native adaptation and moving glow; separately move a venom contact loop, weather graphic trick, explosion, wake event, sheet extrusion or special organ reward. Consequence: A launders independent mechanics into land/life. Take B from the cleanse deletion test. No roster entry is cut. Fictional flyers still use real flight; saving an off-state never deletes an animal to satisfy “absence.”

20. **“Off” / “present” / “already built.”** A: hide a checkbox or stop new assignments while old jobs, comps and reasserted glow continue. B: gate availability, dispatch, ongoing effects and reload; preserve saved bodies/holders. Consequence: A leaves deferred behaviour running in the default whole-bar proof. Take B from superb settings. “Absent” means no extra behaviour, special products, active offers, jobs/letters/incidents on the fresh default map; compatibility defs and ordinary roster bodies remain. The inert cross-biome promise has no on-state behaviour to demonstrate, which is why retirement is recommended rather than implementation.

21. **“Season” / “whole” / “cheapest proof.”** A: kernel fuzzing, def reads or a staged clear map proves the mod. B: calibrated state/geometry instruments with every kept system active, a full-map season, actual reload and reversed actions; final habitat read still needs a live look. Consequence: A misses ecological collapse and adapters that never tick. Take B from the cleanse brief. Models predict until they agree with real game records. Exactly one whole bar; engineering checks are not additional refusal bars. The old validation suite remains useful evidence with its scope labels.

22. **“Standalone.”** A: no dependency whatsoever; B: franchise-free content within supported composed packaging, ordinary engine dependencies allowed. Consequence: A duplicates engines; a careless B leaves RM_Eldspar harvesting a campaign-tier product. Take B from architecture Q16/Q17 and naming tiers; resolve that concrete binding under T48 without silently renaming saved defs. A biome with no world tiles remains expected and is never a defect here.

## Second attack: laziest implementations refused

A recoloured water map with a catch table fails B2/B3. A current that only runs when a debug worker registers the victim fails B5. A recoverable sink that heals under any roof fails B6. A static golden lamp and a “gardener exists” string fail B7. A whole-season recording with active feature flags but no completed jobs fails B8. These shortcuts changed the wording, starting states, controls and instrument tickets; they were not accepted as “good enough.”

Outstanding measurements are the eight unknown shared meetings in bars.json. They are assigned to named models. No unresolved choice needs an owner prediction of a running measurement, and no standing ruling is changed to make an implementation pass.
