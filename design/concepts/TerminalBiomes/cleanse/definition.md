## Say the mod

Visit alien sea floors by gravship: the Scald boils, the Grey grows salt, the Chill holds dark fuel, and the Twilight shelters gardens beneath living glass. Their native plants, prey and predators belong to those liquids, and the fish caught at the beach have living counterparts below. In the Twilight, harvestable banks tempt you beside a current that can carry a careless worker into a sink, so routes, warnings and rescue shape the visit.

Land: distinct material, floor, visibility, ordinary weather and building ground, with calm underwater presentation separated from beach weather.

Life: preserve the present rosters, normal native growth/harvest, movement, hunting, ordinary taming and environmental viability; no species eviction.

Signature: **the Twilight’s productive banks draw workers beside carrying water whose warned surges can take them to a findable, rescuable sink.** This is one chain: attraction, exposure, carry, consequence and recovery.

Runner-up: the cultivated-light/tenancy economy. It loses because it adds portable growers, culture, ownership, predators of light and ship maintenance alongside the bank/current chain. Minimal natural well drift and keeper support remain necessary land under the frozen lid laws; they are not a licence to retain that economy. Ordinary hot water, cold fuel and mineral pools use shared environmental rules rather than becoming additional bespoke progression systems.

## Why a player reaches for it

These are destinations for a supplied expedition that wants different resources, living encounters and a place worth navigating. The prediction is that planning a bank route and rescuing a worker makes Twilight visits memorable; enjoyment is not a bar. Vanilla water chiefly obstructs movement and supplies fishing; this kit must show floor life and a working, changing bank boundary. The sources’ GravTide comparison establishes a useful local-floor generation precedent, not proof of our own floor. We borrow no new engine from a donor and promise no alternative planet.

## Using it: first hour, routine, and a large colony

First arrival: the existing ship/layer dependency carries the actual crew and inventory down. A legal landing reserves its hull and unloading/return corridor outside current and surge reach. The crew sees floor material, native life and a reachable bank without consulting a hatch. Landing is refused or relocated safely when that geometry cannot fit; it never silently overrides the reservation. A broken ship from unrelated combat still needs vanilla repair.

First Twilight work: the bed reads as a silt trough with a distinguishable centre and margins; aligned bed detail and inspect text give downstream direction. Stakes mark the outside of surge reach, not the edge of calm safety. B5 requires a cold reader to distinguish the dry-looking bed and stakes from bank silt in unlabelled ordinary play views; a fired warning and correctly placed terrain alone cannot prove that visual boundary. The bank is visibly outside the trough and supports actual forage; both the look and field membership must agree. A new worker receives a readable entry warning before carry can become lethal. The centre is not a usable swimming crossing: ordinary pathing through it must be interrupted by carry. Marginal contact permits retreat, not immunity. Loose items drift through the same field; no cargo float is needed to demonstrate it.

Routine: forage and haul along safe ground; keep the sink approach reachable; use existing arrest works and a harness when deliberately working close. The harness slows the cost, never erases it. An undersurge announces and visibly widens the reach; the stakes and direction remain truthful. The player can withdraw beyond it without abandoning the ship. Banks may be depleted by harvest, but native growth is real and not scripted infinite food.

At 40 colonists: repeated work orders, moving animals, stockpiles and rescue jobs coexist on full maps. Existing field membership, light caches, native growth and pathfinding must remain coherent across crowds, multiple loaded floors, map transfers and save/load. No fixed wall-time target is an owner bar; a task or clock that stops progressing is a visible failure. The whole-system model measures work budgets to guide rules and tuning.

## What changes in the colony’s story

A sea visit has an arrival, a safe working edge, temptation beyond it, a bad choice and a possible recovery. Twilight’s animal-tended lid and temporary columns show a living garden. Campaign bottom dwellings, residents, plots, moorings and lamplight move to the next version: their removal leaves the land, native life and current signature intact. This is my scope decision under the later cleansing card, not a cancellation of the frozen sheet’s initial inhabited-reveal promise or its peaceful-holdings doctrine. RM passes independently of that campaign content. No tenancy, claim-buoy commerce, rent, special quest or new faction hostility is retained.

## Costs and ordinary materials

The ship carries ordinary food, water, power and environmental protection. This version does not add a machine to solve every environmental cost. Scald body water and its cooler margin never become potable; steam being clean means free of the lake’s fouling, not a separate poison or health currency. A condenser can produce a distinct distilled resource when its next-version feature is enabled, but default play does not depend on it.

Scald boiling contact is a cell-and-layer condition, not a penalty for being submerged. On a beach map, the ScaldWater boiling terrains charge contact injury; dry shore and the nonboiling, still nonpotable RM_ScaldMargin are retreat ground. The cool margin exists only on the beach. On a floor map, paid boiling contact occurs only in localized vent-fed pools explicitly authored with RM_ScaldWaterDeep, RM_ScaldWaterOceanDeep, RM_ScaldWaterMovingChestDeep, RM_ScaldWaterShallow, RM_ScaldWaterOceanShallow or RM_ScaldWaterMovingShallow, or their resolved equivalent boiling-liquid bindings. The rest of that map is ordinary hot mineral substrate, the sheet’s “mere heat”; being beneath the boiling sea does not itself apply a contact burn. A floor visitor retreats onto adjacent nonboiling substrate or into the hull. The hull-to-bank working route stays outside those pools, so it can be used without perpetual contact injury. Ordinary air/floor heat still requires preparation; retreat stops new contact burns without cooling the basin, instantly healing burns or guaranteeing a comfortable temperature. Native biology is the explicit environmental exception. The Chill is cold and oxygen-poor; hull atmosphere and ordinary protective gear come from the existing floor dependency. Native fuel organisms are not ordinary colonist food. Stonewater keeps ordinary harvest/cooking use; its custom extractor and refinery chains move. Ordinary material harvesting, including natural salt, remains; it is not a guaranteed market profit or a new trading system.

## When it goes wrong and the readable sign

A current victim retains identity and a location in the basin, with a visible Sunk condition and an actionable warning. The basin is a terminal destination, not another carrying cell that repeatedly re-registers arrivals. A blocked landing location never defaults to an unchecked occupied cell; report a placement failure and keep the victim findable at the last valid position until safe placement is available. It must not become a perpetual sink-teleport loop.

Rescue requires leaving dangerous liquid into reachable safe shelter or a safe carrier. Safe carriage means held by a living rescuer/ship outside the active hazard, or already transferred to a valid safe destination; merely being unspawned is not sufficient. A roof erected over live sinking water is not rescue. A dead/interrupted rescuer leaves the victim or corpse in a readable place. Neglect can kill; no pawn or animal disappears without remains, a location or another explicit readable transition. Existing off-map recovery code is evidence of an earlier fix, not proof this tighter predicate already holds.

The gardener is a living giant animal pawn spawned on each active Twilight floor map, moving through the overhead water beneath the waveglass. Its visible body can be selected and followed from the ordinary play view; it is not an off-map record, a roof icon or a story flag. The creature swims among ceiling gardens, visibly grazes growth from skylight rims and patches spent openings with secretion. A completed graze clears the rim’s visible occlusion and restores that opening’s existing light footprint; a completed patch seals an opening already spent under the natural well clock and removes its residual rim light. These are actual local rim/light changes recorded against that animal’s jobs, without a new roof-health, roof-growth hazard or repair-resource economy. Routine tending does not reset a live well’s age, force it closed or make it permanent; the theatrical NotifyGardenerPassAdvance callback stays off with its extra. This functional work is the minimum needed for “tends,” not a new encounter.

My delegated limit for this version: the gardener cannot be hurt, killed, hunted, slaughtered, captured, tamed, traded or transferred out of the floor in normal play, and needs no colony feeding. Ordinary health, aging and environmental paths cannot kill it either. Attempted attacks/removal give a readable protected-creature reason and leave the living animal in place; it has no loot or boss fight. Save/load and map unloading preserve the same individual and its tending state. This is protected gameplay for this version, not an assertion that the lore’s gardener is immortal. The lore’s last-gardener death would kill the roof; that destructive branch is unavailable here. A corpse with a replacement flag, silent respawn or a roof left standing after a reachable gardener death fails the definition. When explicitly opting out of the entire living-lid feature, freeze roof and tending together and preserve the saved animal; that sandbox is outside the default fiction.

Fading light columns have ordinary inspection and a visible waning stage. Replacements choose real eligible garden ground using authoritative current membership, hull exclusion and ordinary habitat. They never require rent, graze pressure, a cage, a sun-sphere or an event-spawned special plant. The sheet’s finite years-scale drift stands; the code’s week-scale defaults are a tunable rule to repair, not a new owner preference. An ordinary visit shows the gardener’s work and each column’s finite remaining lifetime, not the passage of years. Later-visit lifecycle evidence comes from the B7/M3 tick-aged branch, separate from the season: record a fresh floor’s source-save and configuration fingerprint, initial well ids/ages/lifespans, RNG state and game tick; keep that floor loaded and run every actual simulation tick with all kept systems active, skipping rendering only. The game itself saves near natural waning, then continues through closure and replacement, with checkpoint/event records and save digests chained to initialization and a real mid-branch reload. No age, stage, lifespan, deadline, tick jump, setting change or forced advance may prepare this evidence; missing provenance disqualifies a near-expiry save. M3 names the additional tick workload rather than hiding years in a season trace.

## Edges and deletion arguments

Ordinary roster bodies, static glow, ordinary harvests and viable native feeding/drinking remain. This is not a blanket exemption for unusual species mechanics. Contact venom, weather-retraction art, fuselight explosion, tarnn wake, hesuun extrusion, plant charge/wilt and well-triggered special spreading all move; the bodies remain. Replace event-only placement with ordinary habitat placement so deleting an extra does not empty the life roster. The death-drop organ and special loot move without removing the creature. Natural creature light must move with its body, which is why its registration adapter stays.

Scald steam presentation belongs to the beach; the floor has submerged bubbles and no weather-steam exposure clock. Grey salt fall is crystals in water, not cold snow or water rain. Mineral crowding is allowed while Grey fauna remain solitary. Twilight is allowed shoals below the lid; shore laws do not make it a sterile floor. Chill ice is floor bedrock/crust or a neighbouring beach, never a new location on the lake’s top. There is no native-land-predator invasion or new free immunity to boil traversal.

The explicit exceptions retained as needed support are current warnings/inspection, the float harness and existing arrest interfaces, findable sink recovery, natural light registration and the actual protected animal’s roof tending. Their deletion respectively destroys informed use, preparation, consequences, moving life or the roof’s creature-and-light law. Removing inhabitants does none of those things, so the inhabited reveal is deferred rather than treated as an exception. Settings, aliases and defensive guards are inherited/load support, not extra experiences. No bars demand a particular yield, density, duration, variant count, art path or numerical equilibrium.

## Settings

Ship master on, each sea on. Expose on/off per major kept mechanic: ordinary land presentation, normal life adaptations, natural well lifecycle, current, surge and warnings. Show effective parent state, shipped tuning/reset, and whether a change applies now or on the next local floor. All-off stops scripted behaviour without removing defs, destroying held things, leaving a launch restriction or blocking return travel. Do not switch off the world’s literal terrain or undo the fixed planet. An explicitly opted-out sandbox is not the default fiction.

Next-version options default false in both initialization and missing-value Scribe load. Each option is labelled next version, and gates construction, recipes, research, trade, incidents, map ticks, running jobs and reload reassertion wherever relevant. Natural counterparts remain available. Existing saved opt-ins need a readable migration, not a silent state purge. Existing unsupported cross-biome values stay off and labelled; recommend retiring the inert controls rather than implementing a capability the standing ruling dropped. No new worldgen control.

## First hour

The crew arrives beneath the Twilight lid. Gold columns pick out a garden; the bank path runs between the ship and forage. A worker starts toward a tempting patch across the bed, reads the warning and retreats. The scene already supplies the mod’s promise without rent, a pane strike or a custom farming station.

## An ordinary week

Foragers work the banks and loose dropped stock shows the downstream direction. A warned surge widens the reach; the crew brings workers beyond the stakes and resumes after calm returns. Native prey feed and predators hunt while harvest changes the accessible patches. The giant animal grazes a skylight rim overhead; that rim clears while its well clock continues normally. Campaign dwellings and residents remain absent at default settings.

## The worst day

A worker ignores the boundary and is carried into the sink. The warning selects a findable victim. A rescuer dies on the approach, leaving a body and an interrupted job rather than deleting either pawn. A second rescuer uses reachable safe ground, carries the worker into the hull and the condition improves. Reloading during that carriage preserves the outcome; roofing the sink would not have saved them.

## The season’s turn

The visit continues across natural current changes and ordinary native growth. A mid-way save survives reload, and hauling before foraging produces another viable route through the same obligations. A separate later visit to the same floor, aged from initialization by the game’s actual ticks under the B7/M3 provenance contract, shows a well wane and a replacement on safe ground. This is years-scale supplementary evidence, not a season event. The garden remains alive; the Grey stays a mineral murk, the Scald still boils and the Chill still demands heat. This is a prediction to test with the whole-mod recorder, not a claim that the current build has done it.
