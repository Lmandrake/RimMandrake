**The three most valuable things the first read missed**

1. **What the flood does to a parked ship has a rule in his own words.** On the shield sheet, 2026-09-02: *"Slow
   pressing things move through all shields is long-standing canon, including the crew. This is why nothing 'holds
   out water,' it just doesn't work that way."* The first read wrote that a hull on the floor *"can take it"* and
   offered an upstream wall. The cleanse's own rule (*"a roof shades but does not elevate"*) says the same thing
   about any roofed room on the bed. Read together: the river comes aboard. The same frame, built, already counts a
   sandstorm against an unscreened ship, and the campaign's weather for this biome has sandstorms; the first read
   said no source has them wear a hull.
2. **The witnessed flood is triggered by the ship.** His rulings of 2026-09-10 (`flood_witness_event_design.md`
   §5, a file the first read did not open): a Moisture Farmer's invitation, one fixed authored tile, and *"first
   gravship landing within range"* as the trigger, because *"timing rides the plot, never weather RNG."* It is the
   one thing that puts the biome's event inside a short visit, and the design now says so and asks whether it
   belongs in the first stop.
3. **The ship's part and the ship's instrument both come from here.** The fossil case (*"Bolted to a bulkhead it is
   the first thing a visitor asks about"*) is this biome's row on the ship list; the first read recommended
   dropping it. And the acoustic scanner every ship carries began as this biome's "Belly Sounder", which he picked
   from the GPT card of 2026-09-30. The same card records the three extensions he did not pick.

Also recovered: the muttavaq *"never stops: walls, crops, buildings and pawns on its feeding line are things it

**How the choices were made.** The owner's words of 2026-11-18: the biomes *"are going to be visited by a huge ship
on the run"*, crewed by Jawa *"way in over their head"*; *"anything that takes seasons to observe will simply be
missed"*; for 0.1.0 and 0.2.0 the *"OMG did you see that?"* of Jawa meeting alien creatures at close range is *"the
overriding priority"*. The cleanse proposal of 2026-11-17 took the experience line's last clause (*"farm the mud it
leaves"*) seriously and built 0.1.0 around a wet aftermath, while switching off salvage as a second reward loop.
His own answer a week earlier said *"in Utinni, there's no farming anyway"*, and the frozen sheet says what Jawa do
with a flood: *"a flood is a salvage strike."* So this design turns the cleanse's choice round: salvage on the mud
is in 0.1.0, farming is "later". The other large change is that the ship itself is on the map, and where it lands
(floor or bench) is now the first decision. Four things he ruled on today's card are stated per biome: the stop's
outer edge, cover from orbit, fuel, and what the place does to the hull.

**Words used in "what happened".** *kept*; *kept, reshaped* (changed as stated); *moved* (scope bucket changed, from
and to given; the cleanse used "first version", "next version, switched off" and "recommend retiring", and this
design uses 0.1.0, 0.2.0, later, dropped; a cleanse "next version" item that lands in "later" is not a move);
*folded into shared machinery*; *lost*.

## The ones I would look at

1. **"Farm the mud it leaves."** Four words of his experience line, picked by card on 2026-10-20, and I have moved
   farming to "later" on the strength of his later write-in. 0.1.0 still has the mud, its brief fertility, the
   visible growth and the gathering. What it does not have is a crew sowing a crop. If the line is to be honoured
   literally, a fast flood-week crop is the smallest way in.
2. **The roads.** *"Everyone travels the roads; only the Farmers stay."* Seven faction faces, each on the road for
   a different reason: the Hutt toll that stands *"on the correct side of it, smiling"*; the Geonosian column
   *"crossing the open flats in full sun, off-road, unbothered"*, *"the sight that empties a toll post."* It is the
   best faction writing in any of the five sheets in this run and none of it is biome content. Campaign layer;
   "later". For a hunted, stolen ship the road is the most interesting thing here, and this design can only point
   at it.
3. **The hidden water.** *"The water hides. After the flood the water seeps into the cracks; the Farmers' whole
   craft is knowing where it went."* Veqma that blushes green over a find; a survey of new water as *"an
   exceptionally valuable thing to sell."* The cleanse's runner-up signature. 0.2.0 here. Selling a map of somebody
   else's water is as Jawa as anything in the sheet.
4. **The Swale.** His own addition, ruled *"Utinni-locked until discovered in this biome"* and *"shaped for
   transient gravship players: the technique travels with the ship."* It is the only technology in these five
   biomes he designed around the ship leaving. 0.2.0, with the survey that unlocks it; but its use is farming,
   which Utinni does not do, so what the crew does with it after learning it is an open end.
5. **A fauna divided by time.** *"The fauna divides by when it is alive, and almost nothing is visible at all...
   You see nothing all day, and are watched the whole time."* Kept and quoted. The Weeping Stones comparison that
   frames it (*"a matched pair of instruments"*: vanes that sing in wind, chimes that ring in water) is lost from
   this design, because it is about two biomes.
6. **Smell.** *"The flood announces itself by nose first — wet clay on the wind off the peaks before any sound
   arrives."* No game channel carries it. Lost, except as the wind change that opens the warning.
7. **The herald sequence.** The cleanse switched off the wind, the ticking and the tarruq's hush as extras over the
   chimes. I moved them into 0.1.0: they are built, and the ground ticking under a salvage party is the first and
   best beat of the whole event.
8. **Fossils.** His addition: *"the canyon walls are FULL of the dead."* Built. 0.2.0 here as *"fossil cargo"*; the
   display furniture stays dropped. (The second reader kept the fossil case: it is the biome's part on the ship
   list.)

> **§8. Inhabited objects**
>
> - 🔴 **The Moisture Farmers — the only residents** (owner's ruling): homesteads,
>   vaporator fields, cistern heads, walled compounds, ruined farms — `MOISTURE_FARM_TEMPLATES_1`

>   things — or to threaten people; **envoys from the Hutts**; **Jawa**; and, rarely, only
>   when desperate, **the deep desert tribes** trade a little. **Mostly they all stay to the
>   roads.**
> - **Cisterns** — the Farmers' found water; and the dry cistern that nobody found in
>   time.
> - **Flood-marked ruins** — high-water lines on canyon walls; the farm that was built one
>   meter too low.
> - **The fliers' feeding ledges** — droppings, bones, the fish-heads near water.

> **§12. Faction faces — everyone on the roads, each for a different reason**
>
> §6's ban holds the frame: no resident but the Farmers, so every other face is a
> *road* face.
>
> - **The Moisture Farmers** — the only settled face: high-bench homesteads (never the
>   floor), vaporators AND banked cisterns — the one place their Manufacture doctrine
>   has a second source. Chime-lines, discovery surveys, walls facing sun and flood.
> - **The Jawa** — road traffic with a rhythm: **a flood is a salvage strike.** The
>   wall tears open ruins, washes buried tech from the banks, re-deals the board;
>   crawler crews follow flood-news the way prospectors follow gold. The best
>   scavenging on the dayside, for exactly as long as the mud is fresh.
> - **The Hutt Cartel** — the deep-desert posts include *Tolls*, and a slot canyon is
>   where a toll post actually works: one gate takes a cut of everything. Legal — the
>   one barbarism is charging for unsettled *water*; a road is commerce. The Hutts
>   know precisely where that line sits and stand on the correct side of it, smiling.
> - **The Deep Desert Tribes** — rare desperate trade (§8), the short sharp raid on
>   the road itself; the cisterns they never touch — settled water is legal water, and
>   they are the law's zealots.
> - **The Blackstar Company** — ambush country is contract country; hunts *end* here.
> - **The Empire** — convoys must thread the slots and every officer hates it: no
>   garrison (§6), just patrols through the best ambush terrain on the planet, where
>   the locals hold the high benches.
> - **The Geonosians** — they don't need the roads. A Foundry column crossing the open
>   flats in full sun, off-road, unbothered, is the sight that empties a toll post.
