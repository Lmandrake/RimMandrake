**How the choices were made.** The owner's words of 2026-11-18: the biomes *"are going to be visited by a huge ship
on the run"*, crewed by Jawa *"way in over their head"*; *"anything that takes seasons to observe will simply be
missed"*; for 0.1.0 and 0.2.0 the *"OMG did you see that?"* of Jawa meeting alien things at close range is *"the
overriding priority"*. And his later test: *"you should always look for solutions that make you say Very Jawa."*

The Leaning Scrub needed the least bending of any biome in this batch, for two reasons. Its definition sheet was
already written with Jawa in it (the vine a Jawa can thread, the scrap nests *"worth seeking for a Jawa"*), and
nearly everything it does happens in hours: a Gale, a Stall, a lash, a stampede. It also has the most finished
pipeline material: a locked Designer sheet and tree of 2026-11-16. That locked design promises *"a dependable
wind-powered home"*, and the one large change here is that the home is the landed ship. Three things do not
survive the move from a home to a stop, because they pay over seasons or belong to a wall: the years-long smother
claim, the sown sworn hedge, and the farmer's trade. One thing was added because his card of 2026-11-18 asked every
design for it: what the place does to the hull.

**Words used in "what happened".** *kept*; *kept, reshaped* (changed as stated); *moved* (scope bucket changed, from
and to given; the locked sheet has "does" (the current version), "later" (his next Design Version) and "cuts", and
this design uses 0.1.0, 0.2.0, later, dropped; his next Design Version is this design's 0.2.0, so an item going
from the sheet's "later" to 0.2.0 is not a move); *folded into shared machinery*; *lost*.

## The ones I would look at

1. **The years-long smother claim.** *"Locals kill a thicket by throwing blankets over it — starving it of light —
   then burn it after it desiccates for years... a blanketed thicket is a banked claim... 'my grandmother darkened
   this stand.'"* It is the sheet's best piece of invented culture, it is built, and his validated bar of
   2026-11-16 names it. No stop lasts years. I moved it to "later" and kept the cane, which a visitor now reaches
   through died-back stands, purchase, and somebody else's ripe claim. Asked as `NEEDS HIM`.
2. **The ship as the tallest thing on the plain.** A 2026-09-29 design idea: the hull casts a wind-shadow where
   fog-fat plants green and small animals move in, *"thornholds nesting in your hull's lee"*, and *"thunderstep
   herds converge on a launching ship to stamp the burn."* It is the only idea in any Scrub source written about
   the ship. *(Corrected by the second reader: he cut both halves at the sitting of 2026-09-28. They are in no
   scope bucket; his words are in the design's `NEEDS HIM` 1.)*
3. **The calling-pyre.** *"Very Pyrrhic; spoken of the way sailors speak of running aground on purpose."* He moved
   it to the next Design Version on 2026-11-16 and I left it there (0.2.0). Under the lens it is the best answer in
   the game to an Imperial siege camp, and a reader could fairly want it sooner.
4. **The quincunx and the two shadows.** *"An orchard nobody planted."* Every shrub casts a light-shadow one way
   and a fog-shadow the other, so the only stable arrangement is even, staggered spacing. It is the most beautiful
   reasoning in the sheet. Kept as how the land looks; lost as anything the player is asked to notice.
5. **The moisture farmer's trade.** *"Freedom and a meager living here; richness chained to a defended point
   there."* A whole economy stated in one paragraph, and it is a settler's choice. Later.
6. **The sweetline as a contour.** *"You can tell how far you are from the stormwall by the colour of the fuzz at
   your feet."* A fact about the planet that one landing cannot show. Later.
7. **The bark-wardens.** Cut on 2026-10-23, and they stay cut. They were the one thing between a Jawa and free
   wool, and a reader building the scratching-post moment may miss them.
8. **Sworn venomvine.** The one strain a player sows, with a sap-mark that lets its own walk through. Built, in
   the locked tree, recommended first in its own pitch. A hedge-fort is a home's wall, so: later. The other choice
   is 0.2.0 with the road-folk, whose hedge-forts are made of it.
