# Page one — what the Sump becomes

The Sump is a cold, flat black basin under permanent dry dusk, with solid glassy ground between pools of tar. Its waxy plants feed without sunlight, and its seated animals forage, hunt and can be tamed among the margins. Crossing raw tar slows and dirties workers; choosing routes and supplying solvent lets an established colony keep working without becoming clean everywhere.

Land: readable deep pools, shallow margins, cooled sheets, usable building ground and dusk; no rain or special sun heat.

Life: keep the seated plants and animals, ordinary food/products/taming, native tar movement, home-liquid hydration and real flight. No roster eviction. Plain mice and kethrel stay when their custom systems are off.

Signature: exposure to tar creates work and movement costs; routes and supplied worker cleanup answer them. I chose this from your experience sentence and your automatic-routine answer. Excavation was the runner-up: its archive, traps, clues and catastrophe require another system. The frozen sheet calls excavation core; this proposal sequences it under your later smaller-first-version instruction, preserving its future rulings.

# Page two — what to check

The consequential readings I took:

- “Every action”: recurring tar cost, not arbitrary failure of every command.
- “Black basin”: actual tar/sheet geography and usable connected ground, not darkened grassy sand.
- “Dusk”: dry visible weather under the fixed world's geometry; Direct Sun stays shared.
- “Chemotroph”: actual growth without a sunlamp, subject to ambient cold and the enabled growth engine.
- “Native life”: ordinary bodies and movement stay; independent shell, clue and trap systems move.
- “Automatic”: standing work policy, physical reachable solvent and no repeated individual medical bills.
- “Cleaned”: clear carried tar as well as the health label; otherwise the next scan reapplies it.
- “Never fouls”: glasswalk rejects floor deposits; it does not magically clean dirty boots.
- “Solvent supply”: current ordinary broodmother wax, not unbuilt mounds or an invented trader.
- “Same reaction”: chemistry can dissipate; bottled gas is gated with the deferred industry.
- “Off”: stop producers, jobs and delayed callbacks, preserving saved bodies and goods.
- “No moat”: special defence is off; ordinary FlowWorks tar/channel/fire/cooling physics still run.
- “Season”: normal coupled ticking through growth, work and reload; no clear sky or disabled-mechanic shortcut.

No new questions. The trap-reaction and evacuation answers remain future constraints; no waiver is reopened.

Models specified, first to build first:

- Situation walk: enumerate contact, dirty boots, recovery, reservations, death, disable and save boundaries; expose false cures and late callbacks.
- Whole map: forecast land, plants, animals, ordinary work and tar physics together, then compare against an actual complete run.
- Route geometry: sweep legal placements/bearings, connected margins, path cost and accepting cleanup surfaces.
- Solvent queue: locate supply and labour regimes where recovery helps or monopolizes the colony.

Models must agree with actual game records before their outputs are believed. A forecast cannot prove ordinary AI job selection or complete colony operation; those remain game readings. The other game requirements are terrain/sky legibility and actual native animal behaviour/flight state.

I could not settle growth/population persistence, solvent throughput, carried-tar recovery across glasswalk/reload or the extent of source-fed fire by argument. They are explicit unknown meetings with instruments, not guesses or questions for you to predict. Native hydration needs the shared tar binding; the growth classifier needs a resolved-profile read; the tracking binding currently lives in the campaign patch, and the cleaning recipe currently does not clear carried tar. Those are retained-version work, not reasons to keep the extras.
