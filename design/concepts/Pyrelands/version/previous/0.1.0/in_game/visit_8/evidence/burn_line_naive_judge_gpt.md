1. **Does it move? Yes.** Across A, the burning tips shift and leave longer, branching scars. B shows flames advancing into grass while earlier ground becomes dark and loses its flames.

2. **Does it flood the map? No.** Burning stays concentrated around scattered fronts and connected trails. Large grassy areas survive through all four days.

3. **Artificial-looking places:**
   - **B, bottom row, especially columns 3–6:** conspicuously straight, roughly one-tile-wide horizontal flame lines. Noticeable in these close-ups; at ordinary play they would likely read as a wind-aligned front on square tiles. Acceptable, though worth polishing.
   - **B, second row columns 5–6 and third row columns 1–4:** flames form compact blocks with flat sides and squared corners. Prominent close up, giving some moments a stamped appearance. Still acceptable as adjacent tiles burning together.
   - **A, central burned corridors in each panel:** long, narrow stretches suggest lanes, but they bend, branch, widen, and break. At map scale they read as fire paths, not ruler-drawn strips.

4. **Verdict: YES** — “a natural grass fire that moves, not a flood over the whole map and not a fire that stands still.”
