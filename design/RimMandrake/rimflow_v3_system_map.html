RimFlow v3: who plays what

Lukas brings the vision. Seven AI roles each own one question. A program, the Dispatcher, keeps time for all of them, the way a metronome does for an orchestra.

The roles, one by one

Each role owns one question and does not overlap the others. The Producer does not own the bar it is judged against, and the Arbiter's passes are themselves audited.

Lukas

the owner

Vision and taste; the final word on direction.

  • holds the vision and taste
  • rules on direction
  • decides trade-offs with the Conductor
  • sets the schedule file, switches roles on
The human; the final word

Conductor

the one he talks to

Quick mode on his phone, fresh per message; session mode when he sits down at the workshop.

  • answers him from the metrics pack
  • turns his words into Dispatcher instructions
  • brings replans and trade-offs to him
  • in session: design, hardest bugs, live testing
Helpers: design · builder · live-test (session mode)

Producer

what and when

Owns the plan: order of work, critical path, scenarios written before building.

  • plans the work and the critical path
  • writes scenarios before building
  • keeps the idea bank
  • drafts the replan when a forecast misses
Runs alone, no helpers

Arbiter

good enough?

Rules pass or fail when a feature claims done, on all the evidence: offline tests, live tests, code reviews and his feedback. The north star is one way of gathering it.

  • takes a feature's claim of done
  • weighs every kind of evidence
  • rules pass or fail, with the evidence
Helpers: judge helpers that never built it

Auditor

are you sure?

Checks the yeses: samples closed work and the Arbiter's passes, at most four investigations a day.

  • samples closed work
  • re-checks the Arbiter's passes
  • turns repeat errors into enforced rules
  • files tickets; never closes or accepts
Helpers: re-check helpers

Innovator

what could be much better?

Process experiments twice a week, game design review weekly; each experiment names its metric.

  • runs keep/kill process experiments
  • names the metric before starting
  • reviews what the game design lacks
  • proposes; vision changes go to him
Helpers: experiment helpers

Operations

keeps it running

Stuck processes, stale helpers, disk, git, reboots, and the only AI that analyses token burn.

  • reads the ops_watch digest, never re-gathers
  • unsticks processes, disk, git, reboots
  • analyses burn: rates, bleeds, trends
  • forecasts burn against the plan
Helpers: small fix helpers

Dispatcher

a program, not an AI

Every 2 minutes it feeds the builder and launches the roles. It measures; it never decides.

  • wakes a stalled builder, hands it the next ticket
  • checks it happened, retries, records
  • launches every role from one schedule file
  • enforces caps; picks the account for new work
  • runs ops_watch every 15 min (no AI)
Runs alone, no helpers

Foundry

the one autonomous builder

Builds continuously from the tickets the Dispatcher hands it.

  • takes the next ticket
  • builds, fixes, live-tests
  • claims done to the Arbiter
Helpers: builder · fixer · live-test

Rules that shape the map

Lukas's rulings that set who touches tokens, numbers and verdicts.

Token duties split by kind

The Dispatcher enforces caps and picks which account new work runs on before releasing it. Operations is the only AI that analyses burn. When a forecast misses, the Producer drafts the replan and the Conductor brings the trade-off to Lukas to decide together. Every other role is token-blind.

Measure once, analyse many

The Dispatcher measures with ops_watch every 15 minutes, no AI involved. Operations analyses its digest and alarms and never gathers the numbers again.

All the evidence

The Arbiter weighs offline tests, live tests, code reviews and user feedback. The north star is one way of gathering evidence, not the whole of it. The Auditor then checks the Arbiter's yeses.

No AI wakes itself

One schedule file names everything that runs: his message wakes the Conductor; the clock wakes Operations, Producer, Auditor and Innovator; events wake the Arbiter (a claim of done), Operations (an alarm) and the Producer (a failed plan step).
