Climate and light lines are the figures recorded in each biome's definition sheet, not live measurements. No tile counts are used to judge status: the world is repainted once at the end. the Abyss RM_Abyss

A country of obsidian-black stone twisted into shapes no wind carved, drowned in a darkness that behaves like a substance: it fills the air, it pools, it parts without warning into pockets of impossibly clear air, and it closes again. Light is eaten in transit here, not merely absent. The only reliable illumination is biological -- glowing grass, blue darklight gammas, yellow giant flora -- a land lit like a harbor at night, by its own lamps.

the Blue Desert RM_BlueDesert

From orbit the bluest thing on the planet; underfoot, colorless. A plateau of the oldest ice on Ash'karr, its "sand" ice grain, its "snow" that same sand blowing, scoured bare by cold wind draining off higher ground. Nothing here runs on water: what lives is a fuel charge stable only in the cold, and warmth is detonation. It is usually clear, silent, and almost empty — then the ablation line gives something up.

the Cauldron RM_Cauldron

A low, wet-black forest standing in permanent dusk over ground that breathes. The light never changes and casts nothing you could call a shadow; trunks weep a grey metallic sweat that hardens into scabs and crystal fans. Nearly everything alive here is either sealed against the vented metal-rich gas or has processed the load and become toxic itself -- and nothing is fast, because a chemosynthetic budget is thin.

The place where trees finally won. Great solid trunks, much more than one tile wide, have rooted the backswamp still and solid, tamed the flowing waters into shallow surface pools with deep, deep mud beneath. Their arches and branches have grown into byways well above the dark still pools -- roads made of living wood. In the crown: a whole arboreal world. Below: the black mirrors, unrippled. Nothing goes in the water here. Everything remembers why.

the Slime RM_GelatinousSlime

A country-sized living body lying open to the sky. It was made to be a weapon and it won: it ate everything it was sent against, read every genome it ate, and filed them all, and then there was nothing left to be sent against and nobody left to report to. It is still filing. Walking across it is being read — slowly at first, as a sheen you can wipe off, and then not slowly. Nothing on it is hostile. It does not need to be.\n\nThere is no rock here and no soil worth the name; there is slime, which will feed you, cure you of any poison you have ever swallowed, and then take its fee in the only currency it understands.

the Miasma RM_Miasma

Fuel Snows / Umbra: what is falling, and why the files disagree

Short answer. In the game as it stands, what falls on those tiles is ordinary vanilla snow (the stock SnowGentle and SnowHard weathers). There is no fuel-snow weather def, no fuel item, and no special terrain left behind: the ground is RM_PackedSnow / RM_PackedIce, copied from the old Alpha Biomes mod. "Fuel snow" is a label and a story laid over vanilla snow. The lore says it is hydrocarbon and ammonia crystals; on 2026-10-16 you ruled the opposite for the rules: "It doesn't snow fuel." So the lakes are propane, the sky is not, and the name Fuel Snows promises something the game does not do.

Ammonia
