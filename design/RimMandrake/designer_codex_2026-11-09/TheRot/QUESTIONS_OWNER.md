# TheRot — proposed direction

Your chosen experience sentence: “A cathedral of mushrooms in the dark, generous and hungry. The Rot shares everything, including you.”

Everything below is my proposal or guess, for your reaction.

The Rot is a cold forest of towering mushrooms, living light and milky ground that makes a colony want to stay. Its living mat warms shelter, and its fungi offer powerful medicines, but harvests decay quickly and the richest groves defend themselves. Fungal partners living in colonists bring useful gifts with lasting bodily costs; animal kin share wounds or heal better together. The giant hwelgrue gives back metal but swallows the fallen alive, making rescue part of living beside it. Its swallowed ship core offers another bargain: keep the giant alive to hear more of its log, or fight to extract the core and lose the unread entries. A brewing vat, a costly medicine for removing fungal partners, and an earned portable mat root let some of this life travel with the colony. Comfort, danger and belonging should feel like parts of the same hungry place. The risk is that daily life, medicine trips and the giant feel disconnected.

### 1. What makes the bargain feel right?

**Picture:** A cold colony settles on warm living mat and comes to depend on its medicines. Later, an exposed worker falls sick and a fallen companion is swallowed alive by a hwelgrue.

**Question:** Where does this connection between help and hunger feel right, and where does the proposed direction miss what you want from the Rot?

**Why this matters:** Useful rewards beside unrelated punishments could miss the feeling of joining a living network.

**My guess:** The comfort must be worth accepting, and its dangers should feel connected to the life providing that comfort.

### 2. What should a settled colony still have to manage?

**Picture:** Forty colonists have roofed stores, refrigerated living produce and rooms warmed by mat. They know the ordinary precautions; there is no special crisis today.

**Question:** What should still need daily attention, and what should a well-arranged base have made easy?

**Why this matters:** The Rot needs a distinctive routine without turning every day into repetitive chores.

**My guess:** Good shelter and storage should handle routine losses; exposed work and keeping harvests fresh should still shape plans.

### 3. How late can a rescue still work?

**Picture:** After a battle, a companion is discovered alive inside a hwelgrue with little time remaining. The fighters are injured, and the rest of the colony is busy. The companion’s name and remaining hours are available when inspecting the giant.

**Question:** What rescue should still be possible then, and when would a death feel earned rather than stolen?

**Why this matters:** A visible deadline only helps if it leaves a meaningful chance to act.

**My guess:** A prompt, committed response should have a real chance; delay should worsen the outcome.
