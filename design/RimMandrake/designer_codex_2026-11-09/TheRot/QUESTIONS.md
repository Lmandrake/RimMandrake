DRY RUN — TheRot, stages 0 and 1. Proposed wording and guesses are mine, not Mandrake's words. Nothing is approved or sent.

This biome bundles everyday ecology, guarded live medicines, kin behavior, the hwelgrue rescue and ship story, and portable rewards. Each built promise keeps its own proof.

PROPOSED ROOT

TheRot is a cold fungal cathedral whose generosity draws a colony into a living, hungry network. Its towering caps, biological light and milky ground make a place to live; the mat helps heat shelter, while the Sheen, fast decay and still-living harvest change how the colony works. Its best medicines demand a guarded expedition and fresh brewing. Symbionts offer real benefits with lasting bodily costs, and animal kin share wounds or mend in company. The hwelgrue makes the same bargain enormous: it returns metal, takes the fallen alive, and carries a ship core whose living log rewards patience while its extraction rewards a difficult fight. The vat, purge draught and earned mat root let the colony carry particular lessons away; teas and symbionts keep their existing limits. These connected promises need separate demonstrations, then a colony scene showing that comfort, danger and belonging feel like one place. The free mod stands on its own fungal cast; shared engines and campaign additions keep their own responsibilities.

08. Choosing to remain joined

Picture: Written thought experiment: the clan is preparing to leave. Some people carry useful symbionts. Keeping one means keeping its bodily price; the draught removes it at the cost of sickness and possible organ injury. The religious rite remains deferred.

Question: What should the player understand before deciding who remains joined on the next journey?

Why it matters: F1/F2. The bargains are settled, but benefits can be obvious while hunger, mental risk, sun intolerance or the purge cost arrive too late to choose knowingly.

My guess, not your answer: Each host should make the benefit and the continuing cost easy to compare, and the draught should disclose what it takes away and what it can hurt. I would use one departure decision, not another tutorial system.

What changes in the tree: If an ordinary health/inspect view suffices, bind that information there. If committing to a purge needs a deliberate warning, add the specific confirmation/cost preview he names. If uncertainty is part of the story, state which consequences may be uncertain rather than inventing hidden costs. No rite or launch siege is revived.

09. The cold stop after the reward

Picture: Written thought experiment: the clan earns the living mat root, installs it on the ship and later stops somewhere cold. It needs raw food there; fed roots can bud, and hungry mat stops warming and later retreats. The heat ceiling, raw-food diet and budding are already decided.

Question: At that stop, what difficulty should the mat have meaningfully eased, and what preparation should the clan still need?

Why it matters: F1/F3. The existing promise that it helps without trivialising cold needs a player situation; a room-temperature increment alone does not say the reward was worth earning.

My guess, not your answer: It should reduce the heating burden enough to make a cold stop attractive, while insulation, food and backup heat still matter. I would compare the same stop without it and with one and several roots.

What changes in the tree: If it primarily saves fuel, measure useful heating saved across that stop. If it enables emergency shelter, require a survivable preparation window and a hunger failure case. If it chiefly warms a travelling home, demonstrate actual ship/floor carry and repeated landings. Keep the settled cap and food/bud rules; tune only the experience numbers still provisional.
