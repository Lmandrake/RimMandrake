# LongShade — proposed direction

Only the experience quotation below is yours. Everything else is my proposal or guess.

> Shade is the currency, and every crossing is a gamble. Rest, dash, rest, and never trust a shadow you didn't cast yourself.

LongShade makes shade the ground you live by. In a perpetual sunset, people and animals rest in shelter, dash across exposed ground, and recover before going farther. Real shadows must protect them under the planet's ordinary sunlight and heat rules. Each stop has life, food or danger; a dark patch can be a trap. A wreck's shelter can be worth more than its metal, and taking it apart can break a route. Building shade makes new routes. A landed ship attracts wildlife while remaining free to launch; a great wandering gloomcast carries shelter and fertilises its stopping places. Dew, scavenging and trips to recover the dead give you reasons to venture out, while smoke haze briefly opens longer crossings. The pleasure is learning which shade to trust, reach, build and share, with understandable consequences for mistakes.

The risk in this proposal: attractive creatures and sunset scenery could leave ordinary travel unchanged. Shade must change daily decisions without making every worker need a babysitter.

### 1. How much supervision does ordinary work need?

**Written picture:** Forty colonists have connected workshops and shelters. Someone dismantles a wreck that sheltered the middle of a hauling route. The next worker receives an ordinary hauling job beyond the gap.

**Question:** When should that worker stop and hand the decision back to you, and what exposure may an ordinary job accept without asking?

**Why this matters:** Routine work should respect the shelter you built while leaving deliberate dangerous crossings possible.

**My guess:** The worker takes a safe detour if one exists; otherwise they wait with one clear reason. A deliberate order lets you accept the danger.

### 2. What makes a false shadow a fair trap?

**Written picture — mock needed before choosing its appearance:** A hot traveller approaches a mirrak, a creature resembling dark ground that offers no cooling. Real shelter is nearby. This describes the situation; it does not show or settle the visual clue.

**Question:** Before committing, what should a careful player be able to do to check that stop: observe the surroundings, inspect it, or try a cautious approach?

**Why this matters:** Distrusting borrowed shade needs a useful action, so a mistake can feel earned.

**My guess:** Observation should raise suspicion, with inspection or a cautious test available before full commitment. The exact visual clue needs a separate labelled comparison.

### 3. When a relied-on shadow leaves

**Written picture:** You have emptied a dead crawler. Its returning clan arrives to tow it away, but workers and animals now depend on its shade as a stopping place.

**Question:** What should the colony have time to accomplish between the clan's arrival and the crawler leaving?

**Why this matters:** Losing shelter should create a decision you can act on.

**My guess:** Clear the hull, bring exposed workers back, and put replacement shelter along the broken route, with clear departure notice.

Recommended demonstration order: a wreck crossing and its repair, then wildlife gathering beneath a landed ship, then a salvage trip to exposed remains.
