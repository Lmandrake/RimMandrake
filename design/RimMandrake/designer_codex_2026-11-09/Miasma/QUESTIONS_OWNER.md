# Miasma

The recorded experience sentence: “A stinking, beautiful nursery where the sea dumps everything. Rot is power, and every surge brings orphans.”

Everything below is my proposal or guess, not your words or an approved design.

## Proposed root

Miasma is a beautiful, stinking nursery where a river meets a dying salt sea. Irregular surges shift the salty shore, strand young in shrinking pools, and leave salvage among the roots. The colony must decide where to settle and how to divide its work between rescue, scavenging and survival. A mother confined to the water can hear young she cannot reach. Returning them earns her tolerance; selling them brings wealth and permanently breaks that relationship. Young that choose to stay can help in the water. When the visibly ancient mother dies, a smaller, weaker youngster can take over her watch. Rotting organic waste fuels electricity generators called decay cells. Their exhausted beds reduce corpses to bones and named skulls, and can grow cooking ingredients with the companion food mod. Silt and salt become attar, a luxury substance used to beautify artwork and treat old scars. Disease remains dangerous, but survivors can carry lasting changes. Predators, scavengers and harmless blooms make the delta feel alive throughout this work.

The central promise is that what the sea discards becomes both a responsibility and a resource. My concern: this could still feel like unrelated profitable chores. The surge creating rescue pools, the youngster actually guarding, and generators accepting rotting waste still need work.

## 1. What makes the place hold together?

**Written picture:** Lovely flowers surround a filthy channel. A surge leaves a stranded young animal; that evening, the colony has rescue, salvage and power work competing for its people.

**Question:** What makes that day feel like Miasma, and where does the proposed root tell the wrong story?

**Why this matters:** It determines which experience should connect the colony's daily choices.

**My guess:** The changing shore and the mother's dependence on the colony are the heart; beauty, rot power and salvage make living there worthwhile.

## 2. How demanding should rescue be?

**Written picture:** Several young are stranded in separate drying pools. A forty-person colony has sick colonists to tend, hauling underway, and one dangerous rescue route. The mother cannot cross land.

**Question:** What should a well-prepared rescue party be able to save, and what loss should remain possible despite a good effort?

**Why this matters:** This sets the rescue workload, urgency and room for meaningful failure.

**My guess:** A few prepared people should have a useful chance; difficult or delayed rescues can fail without every surge stopping the whole colony.

## 3. What should rot power earn?

**Written picture:** A base has batteries, other generators and a steady supply of rotting organic waste. Workers feed decay cells; eventually each cell becomes a corpse-processing bed and needs replacing.

**Question:** What role should these cells play in the base's electricity supply, and how much daily work should that contribution require?

**Why this matters:** Their usefulness must justify feeding and replacing them.

**My guess:** Useful power from discarded material, with manageable hauling and replacement costs, enough to plan around alongside other generators.

Recommended demonstration order: surge and rescue, an ordinary day's rot power and spent beds, then a salvage outing.
