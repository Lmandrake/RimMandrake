# Stillsand

The experience you selected:

> An ocean of nothing under a sun that never sets, crossed by giants. Rock islands are your harbours, and their caves are your fortune.

Everything below is my proposal, including the pictures and guesses.

## Proposed root

Stillsand is an apparently empty ocean of sand under a sun that never sets. Crossing it is an expedition: giants can carry moving shade across your route, rock islands offer harbours, and their caves hold opportunities worth the journey. Wind, tracks and buried creatures make the emptiness alive. A dune gale eases the sun's heat but can carry exposed travellers away. Learning to live here turns sunlight and sand into glass, lenses, cooked food and recovered water; giant remains and discoveries uncovered by wind hint at the desert's past. The standalone biome carries this experience; the campaign adds its creatures and a faith built around debt to the sun. Heat follows the shared sunlight rule: roofs and parasols block direct sunlight at every sun angle, while ordinary air heat remains. Danger should leave time for useful decisions, and a growing settlement should work without constant intervention.

My concern: leading with the expedition may understate settled life, while calling rocks harbours may promise safety before travellers have earned it.

## 1. What gives the place its meaning?

**Written picture:** A party crosses bare sand beneath a distant giant, reaches a rock island and finds something useful in its cave. Later, a settlement turns the same relentless sunlight into glass and water.

**Question:** Which part should give the whole experience its meaning, and where does the proposed paragraph miss that?

**Why this matters:** This sets how travel, refuge, discovery and settlement belong together.

**My guess:** The exposed crossing makes the harbour and cave matter; a working settlement is the reward for learning to live there.

## 2. When does a harbour let you rest?

**Written picture:** Travellers reach solid rock. Buried swimmers must surface there, but that does not make them harmless. The party is still outside the cave roof, exposed to sunlight, with a giant nearby.

**Question:** What danger should still make them hurry, and what must they secure before this harbour lets them rest?

**Why this matters:** A harbour needs a dependable use, even when arrival does not end every danger.

**My guess:** Rock gives a break from hidden swimming; rest needs real shade and a secured entrance. An above-ground attacker can still threaten them.

## 3. What makes a cave trip a fortune?

**Written picture:** A water-short colony finds eggs, material for glass and lenses, or a brine seep. Another cave has already been emptied.

**Question:** What should a successful discovery let the colony do next that it could not do before?

**Why this matters:** Valuable loot should change a practical choice, without promising treasure in every cave.

**My guess:** A find funds another expedition, supplies a new outpost or starts useful lens production.

**Recommended demonstration order:** Crossing to rock and a useful cave discovery; then a prepared gale crossing; then a settled workshop day.
