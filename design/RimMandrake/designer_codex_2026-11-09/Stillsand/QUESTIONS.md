PROPOSED ROOT

Stillsand is an apparently empty ocean of sand beneath an unsetting sun, where crossing the open ground is an expedition and a giant can be moving geography. Rock islands offer harbours; reaching their shade and exploring their caves can change what the colony can afford to do next. Wind, moving sand, tracks and buried life make that emptiness active: water and blood wake what was hidden, approaching creatures leave signs, and a dune gale offers relief from the sun while moving the ground and threatening exposed travellers. The same relentless sunlight powers a useful trade in fine sand, glass, lenses, cooking and recovered water. Giant remains and what the wind uncovers give the place a history. The franchise-free biome carries this experience itself; the campaign layer adds its canon creatures and Sun-Debt faith. Its heat must obey the shared geometry of Direct Sun, its losses must be readable, and its machinery must remain usable as a colony grows.

The starting point is the sentence the owner selected on October 20 in `design/RimMandrake/biome_experiences_2026-10-20.md:22`: the ocean of nothing, the unsetting sun, crossing giants, rock harbours and cave fortune. That is existing guidance; this expanded paragraph is my proposal. `record.json` leaves the approved `root` null.

My claim: the crossing, the harbour and the fortune join the biome's many built features into one experience.
The strongest objection: that account could make a substantial settlement and its glass/water industry sound secondary, or imply that a cave is completely safe. The code does not settle the right emphasis, and some of the proposed promise is still unproven or conflicts with later rulings.

These are eight drafts to choose from, not eight questions to serve together. **Ask Q01 and Q02 first**, then at most three at a time as the answers make later questions useful. A phrase, a different small story, or “this doesn't matter to me” is enough. No built capability is being offered for removal. Every picture below is a written thought experiment, not a capture, visual mock or staged game.

Picture: The party makes it off deep sand onto the rock. The swimmers must surface on hard ground. The travellers have not yet gone under the cave roof, and the mirror-crested giant is still nearby. A roof's protection from Direct Sun is already ruled; this question does not reopen it.

Question: Once they reach that rock, what danger should still make them hurry, and what should finally let them rest?

Why it matters: F2. “Harbour” can mean a place to stop and reorganise, or the beginning of a shorter dangerous approach to real shelter. Those give the player different decisions. Surfacing a swimmer is not the same as proving it cannot attack.

My guess, not your answer: Solid ground gives a reliable break from hidden swimming; an exposed crest is still exposed to the sun and a creature fighting above ground. Rest comes with real cover and an entrance the party has secured.

What changes in the tree: If the rock itself must offer a usable stopping place, require a protected foothold in the harbour scene and reject an outcrop with nowhere to gather. If refuge begins inside the cave, test the final approach and entrance as part of the crossing. If securing the cave is a fight, show that fight separately from the successful resting state. What changes in the tree: A travel payoff needs a carried-water expedition and a useful resulting range or reserve. An industry payoff needs a discovery-to-working-machine chain. A settlement payoff needs a usable cave layout with living/work space and access. A sale or treasure payoff needs the named exchange. Keep the different table outcomes, including the emptied row; do not invent a guarantee of treasure in every cave.

Add only the selected workload/throughput acceptance and necessary feedback; do not create a separate management screen from the wording alone.

Q08 — The walking harbour turns

Picture: Travellers have been using an oommok's moving shade across a local map. Their next rock island lies east. The giant turns north, taking its shade with it. The current engine has moving shade and animal followers; this picture does not claim a built automatic colonist escort order or world-map travel system.

Question: When the giant turns away from your destination, what choice should the travellers face?

Why it matters: F1/F2. “Crossed by giants” can become scenery unless their moving geography changes a real decision. Yet a claim about following one across the planet would exceed what the local shade machinery demonstrates.

My guess, not your answer: Stay with safe shade and lose time, or leave it for a prepared crossing to fixed rock. The giant has its own life; it is not a taxi. I recommend showing that fork on one local route first.

What changes in the tree: If choosing when to leave the shadow is the point, require useful moving cover, a reachable fixed refuge and an honest exposure transition. If waiting for another giant is the story, require a plausible waiting place and eventual opportunity before promising it. If you describe an actual follow order or a strategic caravan benefit, record that added scope explicitly and ground it separately; local shade alone cannot pass it. Existing moving-shade and commensal capabilities stay covered in every case.

Basis: the original crossing-with-the-giant proposal, the selected experience sentence, and current Oommok/shade-following source.
