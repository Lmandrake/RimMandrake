PROPOSED ROOT

CrackedLands makes a quiet, dry canyon worth learning before its rare flood arrives. Read the signs, leave the floor when the chimes sound, and return to work the mud: the same water that interrupts the visit makes its farming, sudden animal life, fresh fossil faces and salvage possible. The danger must give the player a meaningful reason to seek high ground while respecting the existing injury-and-knockdown ceiling for ordinary and witnessed floods. Cliff refuges protect travelers and trained animals; wind, ticking pans, the tarruq hush and advancing chimes make the warning a sequence the player can act on. Life belongs to that rhythm, from sleeping pan giants to the short-lived irqit carpet and plants of wet ground, shade and rock. Afterward, choosing what to farm, dig or recover is the payoff; rival crews trade and compete for the exposed salvage. A short stay can also yield a hidden-water survey to sell or use for a cistern, and the campaign's learned Swale technique can leave with the ship. CrackedLands owns the canyon and its flood rhythm; FlowWorks owns excavated water and the Swale, Explosive Growth owns the bloom, and the campaign owns the guaranteed invitation to witness it.

Listen for the chimes and farm the mud it leaves.”** The catalog says you picked it by card. That sentence is your words; the proposed paragraph above is mine. 05. Farming the mud

Picture: The water leaves useful soil. A small visiting crew can stay a few days; a settled forty-colonist base can commit many workers. Current code changes its ordinary flooded cells straight to permanent rich soil, while Explosive Growth separately controls the soak and growing plants.

Question: What would each group bring home from the first few days that makes farming the mud worth changing its plans for?

Why it matters: F2. Real soil is already promised. The unresolved part is the useful work and payoff, not whether the soil should exist. A fertility number can pass while a short stay gains nothing, or a large colony turns every flood into effortless wealth.

My guess, not your answer: Visitors get a useful harvest if they devote the stay to it; settlers gain valuable planting ground but must still do the work and live with the next warning. I recommend measuring both scales before choosing yield or growth settings.

What changes in the tree: A same-stay food harvest needs a timed sow/grow/harvest scene and a dry-ground comparison. If the visitor's practical reward is gathered materials or an existing bloom, test that specific harvest instead of inventing a guaranteed crop cycle. For settlers, test repeat use through a later flood. Your picture sets the payoff and relevant tuning; it does not reopen the ruled real-soil promise or turn this mod into a second growth engine.
