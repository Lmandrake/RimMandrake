# CrackedLands

Your selected experience sentence:

> Most days it is a quiet canyon, and one day it is a river that kills. Listen for the chimes and farm the mud it leaves.

Everything below is my proposal, including the guesses; it is not an account of your answers.

**Proposed root:** CrackedLands is a quiet, dry canyon whose rare flood changes how you live and work there. Learn the land during ordinary visits, heed the chimes, leave the floor for high ground, then return for what the water brings. The aftermath offers mud to farm, a brief burst of life, newly exposed fossils and salvage that other crews also want. Cliff refuges shelter travelers and trained animals. A short visit can also reward careful investigation with a hidden-water survey to sell or use for a cistern. The central choice is when to stop useful work and escape, then what to make of the flood's gifts. How deadly that escape must be remains unresolved below.

**Unresolved conflict:** The experience sentence describes a river that kills. An earlier decision limits both the first witnessed flood and later natural floods to injury and knockdown. These point in different directions; I cannot tell from the available record which should govern.

**My concern:** A brief splash followed by permanent rich soil could leave both the escape and the return to farming feeling trivial.

All pictures below are written situations to react to, not demonstrations of working features.

**1. What does the river threaten?**

**Picture:** A worker ignores the chimes and remains on the canyon floor. The flood catches them. One outcome leaves them injured or knocked down; another can kill them.

**Question:** How should the promise of a killing river and the earlier injury limit fit together?

**Why this matters:** This determines the consequences of missing the warning and the danger the root can honestly promise.

**Agent's guess:** Injury and knockdown may still create a costly evacuation, but they leave the killing-river promise unresolved. I need your interpretation before choosing either outcome.

**2. What must the warning let you save?**

**Picture:** Forty colonists are spread across work sites, animals are outside, and a hauler is carrying a valuable load. The first chime gives two game hours before water arrives, with earlier signs beforehand.

**Question:** What should a careful player still have time to save, and what should they have to leave unfinished?

**Why this matters:** The warning needs to create a fair, consequential interruption.

**Agent's guess:** A prompt recall gets people clear; finishing the haul is a gamble, and helping a straggler consumes the remaining margin.

**3. What makes the mud worth staying for?**

**Picture:** After the flood, a small visiting crew can stay a few days, while a settled colony can commit many workers. The flooded ground currently becomes permanent rich soil.

**Question:** What should each group gain from those first few days that makes changing its plans worthwhile?

**Why this matters:** Fertile ground needs a useful payoff at both scales.

**Agent's guess:** Visitors earn a useful harvest by committing their stay; settlers gain valuable planting ground that still requires labor and another evacuation when the chimes return.

**Recommended demonstration order:** A useful job interrupted by warning and escape, then a return to farming and salvage, then a dry visit investigating hidden water.
