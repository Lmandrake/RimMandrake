# Abyss

The experience, in your words:

> The dark is a palpable tide that protects you yet conceals terrors close by. Wonders emerge just as often for moments of transcendent beauty, only to twist into nightmares just as swiftly. You fear staying here too long will find the source of the nightmares themselves.

Everything below is my proposal, including the scenes and guesses; it is not an approved description.

**Proposed root**

The Abyss draws a fugitive crew into darkness that shelters their ship and hides dangers close by. Its wonders are as frequent as its terrors, and beauty can become nightmare before the crew feels safe. Heat clears small refuges; living lamps support food, work and travel. The rare Unveiling lifts the Dark into a moment of wonder before it returns. Keeping the colony alive means tending light, using brief opportunities and avoiding searching probes. Deeper expeditions tempt the crew with wreck fittings to restore their own ship, and an egg and great bone beside a sleeping giant whose waking means flight. Raising its young brings strength, hunger, aggression and ever-growing demands. Strange rings, exchanged offerings and dreams make familiar places unsettling, feeding the fear that staying will lead to the nightmares' source, without ever confirming the Nhaleth, the beings suspected behind the signs.

My concern: separate hazards and rewards could work while never making a welcome refuge turn frightening.

**1. The turn from shelter to dread**

Picture — written thought experiment: The crew welcomes the darkness around its ship. An Unveiling reveals wonders nearby. When darkness returns, a danger is much closer than anyone realised.

Question: What would make that turn fulfil the experience above, and where does my proposed root miss it?

Why this matters: This decides how protection, beauty and sudden terror belong together.

My guess: Shelter should feel worth choosing, and wonder should tempt the crew beyond its safety before familiar ground becomes frightening.

**2. A working colony in hiding**

Picture — written thought experiment: Forty colonists shelter around a landed ship. Living plants light their food and repair work while a probe searches nearby. Currently, concealment counts building lamps across the whole map, so a distant workshop can affect the ship's cover.

Question: What ordinary work should remain practical while hiding, and what activity should require a deliberate risk of discovery?

Why this matters: Hiding needs a believable cost that still lets the colony function.

My guess: Food, repairs and careful hauling should remain practical; conspicuous work near the ship or a passing probe should force a conscious gamble.

**3. The cost of staying**

Picture — written thought experiment: Travellers notice strange rings and leave. A settled crew encounters exchanged offerings and dreams in places it has come to trust, without learning who is responsible.

Question: After a season here, what should change in daily life to make leaving feel urgent?

Why this matters: A late dream alone may not deliver the fear of finding the nightmares' source.

My guess: Familiar places and routines should become harder to trust, without requiring a confirmed encounter or automatic punishment.

Recommended demonstration order: a working refuge through an Unveiling and probe search; the living-light farm under pressure; then salvage beside the sleeping mother.
