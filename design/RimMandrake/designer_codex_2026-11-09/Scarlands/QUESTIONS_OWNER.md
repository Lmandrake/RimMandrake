# Scarlands / Warscar

The selected experience sentence: “Poisoned forever, mad beasts, silent guns. Everyone calls it cursed, and everyone who scavenges here learns why.”

Everything below is an agent proposal, including the guesses.

**Proposed root:** Scavenging this old battlefield should make its curse understandable through what happens to the crew. Poisoned ground feeds strange life. Apparently quiet beasts can turn murderous, a living wall can rise, and an invisible hunter leaves traces. Broken guns track people without firing. When the wind stops, poisonous dust settles and holds footprints; returning wind wipes them away. The ruins also offer worthwhile prizes: protective projectors to carry home, useful chemicals, practical manuals, and abandoned machines whose names and histories survive repair or failure. Staying leaves a permanent mark that brings useful knowledge as well as a burden. Better protection and understanding help the next expedition, and recovered technology stays useful elsewhere. The standalone biome should deliver this experience; the campaign adds its own discoveries.

My claim: valuable salvage and understandable danger make the curse convincing. My concern: it could instead feel like profitable production with eerie decoration, or unrelated punishments.

Recommended demonstration order: shelter and salvage during a Settling—the windless period when poisonous dust falls—then machine repair, then deceptive wildlife.

**1. What comes home with the crew?**

**Written picture:** The crew returns with a working protective projector and a recoverable machine. One scavenger carries a permanent mark. They know more about the battlefield and have a reason to return.

**Question:** What consequence should make that successful trip feel cursed: the survivor's lasting burden, an unsettling discovery, or the temptation to return despite the cost—or something else?

**Why this matters:** This determines what a successful expedition must leave the player feeling, beyond having gained supplies.

**Agent's guess:** The temptation works because the cost is real and remembered; the trip should change somebody even when it succeeds.

**2. Who commits a machine to risk?**

**Written picture:** A named abandoned machine is having its limbs repaired in a repair cradle. Etchant, a chemical used to make this repair safer, is available, but hauling is delayed. After six in-game hours, the cradle starts a slower, riskier repair without it.

**Question:** Should that extra risk require your order, follow a standing repair policy, or begin automatically after the wait?

**Why this matters:** A hauling delay should commit a valued machine to danger only under a rule the player understands.

**Agent's guess:** Use a visible standing policy with an override for this machine; default to waiting when the chemical is reachable.

**3. Danger while you watch elsewhere**

**Written picture:** A lone night hauler crosses glower, the battlefield's black plant growth. An invisible hunter approaches while your camera stays at the workshop. Travelling with company offers protection.

**Question:** Should sending someone alone require accepting an ambush while you watch elsewhere, or must an approaching hunter interrupt you before attacking?

**Why this matters:** Local clues cannot provide advance warning when the player is elsewhere.

**Agent's guess:** Company should make routine travel manageable; lone travel may remain dangerous without an alert for every hunter. Mock needed before judging the actual footprint, shimmer, or sound cues; this question concerns the warning requirement.
