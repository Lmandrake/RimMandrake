# GelatinousSlime

Your chosen experience sentence: “Kind, generous and slowly eating you. It feeds you, heals you and reads you, and the price is who you are.”

Everything below is my proposal or guess, awaiting your response.

**Proposed root:** The colony lives on a generous body that slowly absorbs it. Its flesh provides food and building material, and eating it can purge poison. Those gifts stay useful even as unprotected people lose themselves and cultivated fields turn back into slime. Dry ground and a toxic antidote offer ways to escape. Seeking a chosen gene makes the bargain deliberate: someone crosses exposed slime for a dose that soon spoils, then needs a friend to treat them through a coma. They also receive an unwanted gene and a lasting social cost. Resistant animals live alongside outsiders already being absorbed; conversion does not make them hostile. The Titanoslime swallows prey, kills what it finishes digesting, and grows permanently by default. A harvested chunk becomes a weapon that soon spoils. Expensive technology can grow a dead person back as they were at their last recorded touch, while everyone else's life has moved on.

**My claim:** The gifts should remain tempting after the colony understands their price.

**My concern:** Floors, protection and prepared medicine could turn the body into an ordinary supply depot; constant treatment chores could make its gifts unappealing.

**Recommended demonstration order:** A gene expedition and treatment, an ordinary protected workday, then the returned person's reunion and a separate weapon encounter.

## 1. Saving several people

**Written picture:** One worker is heavily coated in slime, another is comatose after a gene injection, and a third is still outside. One friend can reach the antidote. Ordinary exposure worsens on slime; an early coating can dry away off it, but advanced absorption can persist after leaving. Injection starts a separate, faster countdown even away from slime. A similar-looking condition need not mean equal time left.

**Question:** What must the player know before choosing whom to help first, and how much hands-on work should rescue require?

**Why this matters:** Different routes to absorption need fair warning and practical treatment.

**My guess:** The player should be able to compare urgency without checking every patient separately, then assign a friend with medicine to treat them.

## 2. Returning to a changed family

**Written picture:** Someone last touched the slime while married. They later separated, their former partner remarried, and then they died. The colony grows them back from that earlier moment. They return expecting their marriage to continue.

**Question:** What should happen at their first meeting so the returned person's earlier life remains true without deciding their former partner's present life for them?

**Why this matters:** Restoring a person also brings back relationships that others may have left behind.

**My guess:** The returned person expects the old bond; the former partner's later life remains real, and neither is forced into a renewed commitment.

## 3. The weapon's consequence

**Written picture:** A harvested chunk bursts among armoured raiders, coating them in advanced slime. The slime-covered ground lasts a day. After they leave it, their advanced contamination persists, but they do not acquire the gene injection's faster countdown.

**Question:** What should change in that fight for this to feel like a terrible bioweapon?

**Why this matters:** Persistent contamination alone does not establish how the weapon threatens its victims.

**My guess:** Victims should immediately struggle to fight or escape, with equally clear danger to nearby allies.
