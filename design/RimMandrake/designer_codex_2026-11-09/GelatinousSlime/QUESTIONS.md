DRY RUN — GelatinousSlime, stages 0–1. This is an agent-authored proposal and question bank. Guesses are not answers; nothing is approved or sent.

PROPOSED ROOT

GelatinousSlime makes generosity dangerous: the colony can feed itself from a living landscape, turn its flesh into building material and safe food, and eat it to purge poison, while the same body slowly reads unprotected people and animals into itself. Fertile-looking ground takes cultivated fields back; resistant herds and partly absorbed arrivals show different ways of living on the body. Leaving early, taking the dry way home, or administering a deliberately toxic antidote can save a person; conversion itself never makes them hostile. The gene seeker makes the bargain deliberate: choose a gene, venture into an exposed current, bring home a perishable dose, and entrust a comatose recipient to someone who must administer the antidote. The chosen gene comes with an unchosen rider and a lasting social price. The Titanoslime gives the body a moving mouth: what it finishes swallowing is lost, feeding makes it permanently larger by default, and its harvested chunk becomes a short-lived bioweapon. With expensive technology, the archive can grow a dead person back from their last recorded touch, leaving a gap between that person and the life everyone remembers afterward. These connected promises need separate demonstrations, including the colony's ordinary work between the dramatic moments.

The foundation is the owner's chosen experience sentence, reproduced exactly from `design/RimMandrake/biome_experiences_2026-10-20.md`, GelatinousSlime row (picked by card): “Kind, generous and slowly eating you. It feeds you, heals you and reads you, and the price is who you are.” That sentence is his chosen wording. The proposed root and every other sentence here are mine. I found no corresponding approval of this proposed root in `OWNER.jsonl`; `record.json` therefore keeps `root` null.

My claim: the gifts should keep tempting the player even after the player understands their price.
The strongest objection: a routine of immunity, floors and prepared antidotes could make the body feel like an ordinary resource shop; constant medical chores could make its generosity irrelevant. Neither a passing rate calculation nor an attractive landscape decides whether the bargain works.

These are eight drafts to choose from, served at most three at a time after independent review. **Ask 01 and 02 first.** A short phrase, another scene, or “this doesn't matter to me” is enough. Silence changes nothing. All pictures below are written thought experiments, not captures, visual mockups or staged acceptance scenes.

01. **ASK FIRST — What makes its kindness dangerous?**

Picture: A poisoned worker eats raw slime and recovers, then carries its film home. Later, another colonist knowingly brings back a gene dose and asks a friend to see them through the coma. Both took a gift from the same body.

Question: Which moment best carries “the price is who you are,” and what in the proposed root misses your picture?

Why it matters: F1/F2. Bodily loss, chosen alteration and the later return of an earlier self are different consequences. The root needs your meaning, while preserving the built features.

My guess, not your answer: The early kindness should be real; the horror is that accepting it gradually changes what the colony regards as an ordinary, acceptable price. I recommend leading with the bargain rather than the giant.

What changes in the tree: If slow loss leads, the root centres routine exposure and opportunities to retreat. If chosen alteration leads, it centres the seeker and lasting costs. If the returned person leads, it centres the archive's identity gap. All retain the other built capabilities and their coverage. A correction rewrites dependent claims; no reply approves nothing.

03. A season of taking its gifts

Picture: A forty-person settlement has floors, a compressor, a rendering pit and antidotes. Its first fields have started reverting. The colony knows the risk now; this is a working Tuesday, not its first disaster.

Question: What should still tempt this colony to send people onto the body, and what part of that day's work should still feel costly?

Why it matters: F1. Scarcity, exposure and medical work can create interesting choices or repetitive chores. Existing buildings and protection routes stay; their ordinary use needs an experience bar.

My guess, not your answer: Harvesting and chosen genes remain worth leaving safety for. The cost is planning who goes, how long they stay and who can treat them afterward, rather than checking every pawn constantly.

What changes in the tree: If supply trips carry the cost, require a useful harvest/manufacturing cycle with protected and exposed workers. If medical staffing carries it, require a normal workday where staffing choices matter and treatment remains practical. If the bargain should become routine once mastered, the scene instead proves useful production and reliable protection without inventing a permanent emergency. The ruled field conversion and full colonist ladder remain.

Basis: `SlimeWorks.xml`, `SlimeExposure.cs`, `Slimification.cs`; inventory `compressor`, `pit_food`, `field_conversion`, `exposure`.

04. A fair chance to save several people

Why it matters: F2. The friend-and-antidote rule is settled. What counts as a fair, readable rescue when several clocks differ is not settled by the alert existing.

My guess, not your answer: I should identify who will be lost first and issue a treatment order without opening three separate health panels; I should still have to choose and provide the friend and medicine.

What changes in the tree: If individual urgency must be visible together, add a readable ordering/deadline requirement exercised with ordinary and injected clocks. If stages plus deliberate inspection are enough, require accurate stage and patient information, with no new overview requirement. If routine treatment should use normal medical work, acceptance must follow that actual order and queue rather than calling the cure effect directly. No answer changes the antidote's toxicity or invents an automatic rescue.

05. Reaching a current just as it moves

Picture: A worker carries a primed seeker toward a marked liquid channel. The current's window ends while they are walking. There is another usable channel farther across the body. The present scanner can choose a fresh mark, but the extraction job does not enforce that expiry.

Question: At that moment, what choice should the worker's journey leave you with: finish what was started, follow the new current, or bring the seeker home—and what should the failed timing have cost?

Why it matters: F2. A circulating archive can be a meaningful expedition or an arbitrary moving destination. Its correct behaviour at the transition is a real fork; the existing handheld seeker and exposed extraction stay.

My guess, not your answer: A clear warning leaves me the choice to follow a fresh current or retreat with the unspent seeker. Lost time and exposure are enough; I would not spend the device without a sample.

What changes in the tree: If starting commits the window, bind a mark to the ordered journey and test expiry during travel and work. If the current can outrun the worker, require visible expiry, an honest stop/reorder and a no-expiry control. If a missed window costs the device, require that cost to be stated before departure and prove no gene sample is also produced. None authorises remote extraction or a permanent gene stockpile.

Basis: `GeneSeeker.cs:151`, `GeneSeeker.cs:361`; inventory `scanner_mark`, `sample_extraction`.

07. What the terrible weapon does to a fight

Picture: A chunk bursts among armoured raiders on ordinary ground. They are drenched into late-stage slimification. The patch of slime lasts a day. In the current implementation, ordinary late-stage film holds once a victim leaves the slime; it does not use the injected fast clock. No new hostility is caused by conversion.

Question: After that burst, what change in the fight would make this feel like the terrible bioweapon you described?

Why it matters: F1/F2. Armour bypass and a severity number do not alone define the weapon's consequence. The weapon is already requested and built; the question is what its victims and the player experience next.

My guess, not your answer: The group immediately becomes much less able to fight or escape, and surviving the contamination becomes an urgent problem. Its danger to nearby allies should be equally legible.

What changes in the tree: If the point is immediate incapacitation, bind the concrete loss of fighting ability, with an unaffected control outside the radius. If it is a continuing death clock, explicitly define its progression after leaving the burst ground and the antidote/drying controls. If the point is dangerous ground and lasting impairment, prove movement and later consequences without pretending every hit starts a lethal timer. Each answer keeps the harvested weapon, short shelf life and ruled resistance/cure routes; none gives the body intent or new aggression.

Basis: `OWNER:1790977575993603898`; `ChunkBomb.cs:63`, `Kernel/RM_SlimeLadder.cs:35`; inventory `chunk_weapon`, `chunk_ground`, `chunk_shelf`.

Picture: A partly absorbed animal from a neighbouring biome wanders past the colony. A resistant native grazes nearby. The visitor is quiet and slowing; saving it would cost time or an antidote. Nobody has attacked anyone.

Question: What should that encounter make a player consider doing, and what would make it feel wrong?

Why it matters: F1/F3. The visitor mechanism is built and stays. It could read as the body's indifferent ecology, an optional rescue, or a source of unsettling opportunities; the chosen emphasis changes its presentation and acceptance.

My guess, not your answer: It first shows what the body is doing to outsiders. Helping or exploiting it is the player's choice, with no implication that the body sent it or that every arrival is a rescue quest.

What changes in the tree: If the encounter is ecological, require native/visitor differences to be recognisable and an arrival-to-dissolution story, without quest machinery. If rescue is central, require an actual treatment/escape route and readable urgency. If exploitation is central, identify and prove the particular reward and its cost rather than inventing a general harvesting system. The ruled neighbour-derived arrivals remain under every answer.
