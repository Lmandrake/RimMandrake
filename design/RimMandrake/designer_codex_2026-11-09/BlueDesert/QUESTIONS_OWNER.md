# BlueDesert

Your recorded experience sentence:

> An alien expanse of beautiful blue locking ancient secrets from the depths of time. Mine slowly and carefully or risk your own passion's heat turning everything into a bomb.

Everything below is my proposal, including the guesses.

**Proposed root:** BlueDesert draws a colony into a beautiful blue wilderness where ancient secrets wait beneath the ice. Finding something precious tempts the crew to disturb more than it understands. Careful excavation and knowledge should help them bring discoveries home; the heat of their own work can turn the surrounding cold-loving life into an explosive chain. That danger follows harvested fuel and native produce into storage, making the journey home and keeping cargo cold part of the adventure. Slowly emerging finds, traces of earlier expeditions, storms and strange animals give the colony reasons to explore and reasons to hesitate. The heart of the experience is choosing how far to pursue a discovery when that pursuit can destroy it—and the crew.

**Main concern:** Careful mining does not yet have a working connection between pace, heat and danger. Random explosions alone would miss the experience above.

### 1. What does careful work change?

**Written picture:** Two equally skilled crews uncover the same amount of ancient ice. One works in short visits and takes precautions; the other pushes through in one expedition. Neither has learned the special cold-cutting technique yet. Slower work currently does not accumulate less quarry heat.

**Question:** Across the whole expedition, what should the careful crew do differently, and what danger should that help them avoid?

**Why this matters:** This gives caution a useful consequence without assuming that waiting makes the next block safer.

**Agent's guess:** Read the site, limit how much is disturbed at once, and withdraw when conditions worsen; preparation and restraint should reduce the chance of losing both crew and discovery.

### 2. What time should stored ice buy?

**Written picture:** A busy colony stores reactive fuel and native feed in a cold hold. Power fails while haulers keep opening the door. Its ice-fed cooling rack still works, but the same blue ice is also needed for drinking water.

**Question:** What jobs would you change first, and what should the ice reserve give the colony time to finish?

**Why this matters:** Emergency cooling needs to support a useful response under ordinary colony traffic.

**Agent's guess:** Stop unnecessary traffic, replenish the ice and repair powered cooling, with enough time for a deliberate response if reserves were prepared.

### 3. What does a passing giant demand?

**Written picture:** HugeThings, the existing system that brings passing giants through biomes, needs a suitable native or visitor here. Imagine one crossing beside an active excavation. It would be separate from the existing vhaulk, the great walking cistern.

**Question:** What decision should its passage force on the crew?

**Why this matters:** The encounter needs a purpose beyond adding another large explosive animal.

**Agent's guess:** Clear its path and decide whether to abandon exposed work; its indifferent passage threatens the expedition without becoming an ordinary hunt.

**Recommended demonstration order:** Careful quarry expedition, a slowly emerging find and its recovery, then a cold-hold power failure.
