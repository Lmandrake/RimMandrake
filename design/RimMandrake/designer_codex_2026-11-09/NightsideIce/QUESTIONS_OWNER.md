# NightsideIce / The Sleeping Ice

Your chosen experience sentence: “A frozen highway that nobody stays on. You cross it, dig into it, or go under it.”

Everything below is an agent proposal, including the pictures and guesses; it is not approved wording.

**Proposed root:** The Sleeping Ice is first a place to cross: a sparse, still expanse of dirty white ice, with reasons to stop and reasons to leave. Frozen wrecks offer useful salvage, but the warmth that makes a camp habitable attracts blind shivven beneath the ice. Their approach brings moving cracks and rumbling; the first breach gives a precise warning, while later ones leave more uncertainty. Cold and constructed floors offer real protection. Digging can uncover what the ice has preserved, and going below should be a deliberate expedition with danger and a way home. Staying longer exposes stranger life and larger threats. The central choice is what is worth stopping for, and how much trouble to accept before continuing the journey.

The main risk in this proposal: the warmth-and-breaches system could overshadow the journey. Preserved victims, thawing ice and much of the strange life still need building; Lantern Deeps already offers separate caves, but does not yet provide a rescue route into a shivven's store of victims.

**1. What should staying mean?**

**Picture:** A travelling party stops at a frozen expedition rig, salvages useful cargo and leaves. Elsewhere, forty colonists have built a warm refuge with protective floors, but must venture onto open ice to recover supplies.

**Question:** Should a successful long stay still feel like an expedition preparing to move on, or can a hard-won permanent refuge fulfil this experience too?

**Why this matters:** This decides whether ordinary settled life belongs at the centre of the biome alongside crossing and recovery.

**Agent's guess:** Crossing leads; a lasting refuge can work, but should remain an exceptional commitment with ongoing costs.

**2. Can protected warmth hold a predator's attention?**

**Picture — mock needed:** A stronger heater sits behind protective flooring; a weaker heater is exposed nearby. Both are within the shivven's sensing range. A future comparison should show it lingering at the protected warmth versus turning toward the exposed source; no movement mock exists yet.

**Question:** Should a protected heater let players hold the shivven's attention as a decoy, or should an inaccessible source lose out to reachable warmth?

**Why this matters:** This determines whether safe decoys are a dependable strategy; flooring already prevents emergence through it.

**Agent's guess:** Protected warmth can hold attention while powered, giving decoys a useful role without weakening floor protection.

**3. What does a rescue below involve?**

**Picture:** A colonist has been dragged beneath the ice, leaving a trail and warning. The rescue party reaches the crack. Recovering that person is already intended; the unresolved part is where the rescuers operate.

**Question:** Must rescuers enter a small underground rescue site and return, or should they cut down from the surface and retrieve the captive there?

**Why this matters:** This settles the descent model without asking you to design the whole rescue.

**Agent's guess:** Enter a bounded rescue site with a return route, separate from Lantern Deeps' persistent caves.

**Recommended demonstration order:** Crossing with a worthwhile salvage stop, then a warm camp's warning and defence, then descent and return once built.
