# TheForge

Your experience sentence:

> Where the gods came to forge a world is now the ruins that seek to unmake you. Even the beasts challenge your presence.

Everything below is my proposal or guess.

**Proposed root:** Living in TheForge should feel like trespassing in an ancient forge whose workings can still destroy you. Gas fires, boiling rain and returning lava dictate when the colony shelters, works and retreats. For a short time, lava cools into a steaming crust where crews can gather floatstone for strong, light construction. Animals make those trips harder: apparent outcrops wake in rain, hunters gather around vapour columns, and an attacked dhoggur becomes a fire-spitting danger. Survival means learning the place and choosing when its rewards justify the risk. One central part remains unresolved: what makes the ruined forge present on an ordinary map in the standalone biome. The promised towers and machines belong to the campaign addition; they do not yet answer what a colony encounters before visiting them.

**1. Encountering the old works**

**Picture:** A crew shelters through the fire and rain, crosses the cooled lava and brings back floatstone. They have met dangerous wildlife, but have never entered a campaign tower. So far, their day could belong to a hostile volcano. This is a written scene; mock needed before choosing the ruins' appearance.

**Question:** What encounter, if any, must belong to that ordinary day for it to feel like living among the gods' ruined works?

**Why this matters:** The central promise includes ruins, and the standalone biome's part in that promise is still open.

**My guess:** Recognisable remains of manufacture should affect an ordinary trip; the campaign towers and machines should deepen that experience. That is a proposed addition to clarify, not an established feature.

**2. Time to act**

**Picture:** Forty colonists are scattered between workshops and outdoor jobs. Vent hissing warns of a gas wash; later, glowing cracks warn that the crust will melt. A separate boiling-rain burst can interrupt otherwise quiet heat. The existing rain rule allows a first burst to leave someone downed and scarred, never instantly dead.

**Question:** What useful action should an attentive colony have time to complete before the gas wash and before the melt: recalling workers, rescuing someone, or recovering the last load?

**Why this matters:** A warning only helps if it leaves time for a meaningful response; each danger needs its own allowance.

**My guess:** Gas warnings should allow a prompt recall, and cracks an orderly retreat; a rescue after delay should remain risky.

**3. Shelter against steam**

**Picture:** A steam front passes an open work site, an open-sided roofed shelter and a closed stone workshop. Roofs already stop scalding rain; the moving steam currently reaches across walls and roofs.

**Question:** What protection should workers in each place receive from the passing steam?

**Why this matters:** Players need to understand which shelter actually protects them.

**My guess:** An open roof stops rain but offers little protection from sideways steam; a closed room blocks the immediate front, while ordinary room temperature still matters over time.

**Recommended demonstration order:** A complete floatstone trip through shelter, harvest and retreat; then an animal encounter; then the campaign tower.
