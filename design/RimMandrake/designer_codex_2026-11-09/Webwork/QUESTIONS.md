PROPOSED ROOT

The Webwork is a quiet jungle already owned by something that regards everything in it as food. Walking into its hush feels like trespassing; landing a ship draws an answer. Thick silk cables run through living trees, with sticky threads between them and fine carpet at the spiders' sites. Touching the web or carrying stolen eggs gives its hunters a way to find you. Sunlit clearings and fire give the colony ground to defend, while the growing canopy threatens that refuge. Its treasure makes returning tempting: thrixweave for fine equipment and strong tethers, and inert eggs worth smuggling. Rare traders can resell the silk dearly. A gutter specimen teaches the crewed traction lance, sharing its pull with the Sump's capstan and turning a spider's dependence on shade against it. The wrapped bones of an urraveth show what the owners can consume; reading them earns silk, and loading them risks a collapse that warns first. Beyond the jungle, its spiders can besiege shaded neighbours by visibly spinning their own approach, binding doors and cocooning fallen colonists. The player should learn to read the place and make dangerous trips into it, rather than mistake silence for safety.

Foundation: the owner's actual experience sentence is “So quiet that you know you're in terrible danger. It makes treasure, it eats all, and it's coming for you.” Source: `design/RimMandrake/biome_experiences_2026-10-20.md:30`, under “The experience, in the owner's words.” This is an existing quotation, not approval of the paragraph above. His October 22 answers already say quiet is mostly sound, applies to arrival on foot, and a ship landing always gets a response (`design/RimMandrake/webworks_open_questions_2026-10-22.md`, Q7).

My claim: the common idea is trespassing in a predator's working territory, with treasure making the risk worthwhile.

05. A trip worth making again

Picture: A modest colony sends a guarded party for silk and eggs. Later, a forty-colonist settlement sends a much stronger expedition. Both have rare, expensive trader resale as the already-ruled alternative; neither can turn ordinary eggs into farmed spiders.

Question: After a successful trip, what should each party be able to make or afford, and what should still tempt them back?

Why it matters: F2/F3. The current yields and detection chances are engineering defaults, and the terrain/weather item explicitly leaves their balance for your review. A huge haul can empty the reason to return; a tiny haul can make the danger pointless.

My guess, not your answer: The small party earns a noticeable equipment improvement or a valuable sale; the large expedition equips several people but still has reasons to return. Rare resale should be a costly fallback.

What changes in the tree: A few exceptional pieces gives a small-party reward scene; colony-wide supply gives a throughput and repeated-trip scene. Eggs as the main payout needs a demonstrated selling route, with campaign outlets proved separately. Silk as the main payoff needs crafting or tether use after extraction. Your picture sets yield, time and risk targets together; it does not reopen inert eggs or rare resale.
