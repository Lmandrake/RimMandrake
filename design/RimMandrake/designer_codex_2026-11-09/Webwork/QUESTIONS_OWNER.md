# Webwork

Your experience sentence:

> So quiet that you know you're in terrible danger. It makes treasure, it eats all, and it's coming for you.

Everything below is my proposal or guess, for your correction.

**Proposed root:** The Webwork is a quiet jungle that belongs to hungry spiders. Entering on foot feels like trespassing; landing a ship always draws a response. Silk cables join living trees, with sticky threads between them and fine web carpets at spider sites. Touching the web or carrying stolen eggs lets the hunters find you. Valuable silk and eggs make the danger worth returning to, while sunlight and fire give you ground to defend as the canopy grows back. The silk makes fine equipment and strong tethers; the eggs are valuable cargo but never hatch into farmed spiders. A discovered specimen teaches a crewed lance that can drag a spider out of shade. Wrapped giant bones reveal what these hunters can consume, rewarding examination with silk and warning before an overloaded bone collapses. Their threat also reaches shaded neighbouring settlements, where spiders spin toward buildings, bind doors and cocoon fallen people. The experience should connect temptation, trespass and pursuit: silence offers no safety.

**The weak point:** Valuable loot and a quiet jungle will not carry this experience unless being noticed leads to a hunt the player can respond to.

### 1. The chance to escape

**Written picture:** A silk cutter realises the web has noticed the party. One hunter is close; others are farther away. The party can retreat toward sunlight, rescue a bound companion or stay to finish harvesting. Dropping a stolen egg does not erase a pursuit already under way.

**Question:** What chance should careful players still have at that point, and what mistake should turn it into an ambush they cannot easily escape?

**Why this matters:** The warning needs to leave a meaningful decision between discovery and capture.

**My guess:** Prompt retreat should offer a real escape; lingering for more silk or going deeper under canopy should let the hunters close.

### 2. A trip worth repeating

**Written picture:** A small colony sends a guarded party for silk and eggs. Later, a forty-person colony sends a stronger expedition. Buying silk from a rare trader remains an expensive alternative.

**Question:** What should each successful party be able to make or afford, and what should still tempt it back?

**Why this matters:** Too little reward makes the danger pointless; too much removes the reason to return.

**My guess:** The small party earns a noticeable equipment improvement or valuable sale; the larger expedition equips several people without supplying the whole colony forever.

### 3. The giant's attackers

**Written picture:** A giant from a neighbouring biome passes through the jungle. Ordinary ollathrix, already elephant-wide spiders, attack together. Their size and coordination can both contribute to the battle.

**Question:** Does this encounter require another kind of creature beyond those ordinary spiders hunting together?

**Why this matters:** Another creature would add a separate design decision to the existing single spider kind.

**My guess:** The ordinary spiders hunting as a pack are enough. Any later choice of their appearance or movement needs a mock first: **mock needed**.

**Recommended order:** Start with entry, theft and escape; then the neighbouring settlement siege, the giant remains and the lance.
