PROPOSED ROOT

RustCathedral turns the owner's maze, guarded machines and salvage paradise with a temper into a place the colony learns to work within. Intentional metal passages make the walls both the route and the treasure: ordinary plate is free salvage, richer remains tempt you farther, and sacred structures and deep extraction carry consequences. The hum and the small bolts' changing movements let you notice those consequences without explaining the place's secrets; coolant eels and land-cleaning machines make its sparse life feel maintained, and the rare machinery cycle makes the whole plateau pause. A peaceful, worn-out borehulk offers a longer commitment: remove and transport its heavy drill head for refurbishment, bring back unusual parts, then restore it and choose between an expensive mining servant and a free machine that answers favours elsewhere. Bolts that ride the ship stay lively companions, show unsettling reactions to what the colony does, and eventually present their already-ruled dilemma. Each promise needs a demonstration in ordinary colony play. The free biome owns these mechanics; the campaign owns its hidden relationship and revelations, while shared systems own sun, water confinement and generic creature behaviour.

The root starts from the owner's chosen sentence: “A maze built by minds you'll never meet, still guarded by their machines. Salvage paradise with a temper.” Source: `design/RimMandrake/biome_experiences_2026-10-20.md:21` (picked by card, 2026-10-20). That sentence is his; the proposed paragraph above is mine.

My claim: the ordinary salvage expedition is the centre, and the longer repair and ship stories grow from learning how to behave here.
The strongest objection: a collection of working parts can still miss the intentional maze and its readable temper. The current stage ceiling and sacred-wall connection are concrete doubts; this paragraph is a proposed promise, not a report that the code passes.

03. A maze that changes the route

Why it matters: F1. 'Intentional maze' must change a real expedition, not merely resemble a circuit board when zoomed out. Guardian purpose is already defence, never a hunting force.

06. The owned borehulk earns its cost

Picture: The colony has transported the head, paid for refurbishment and spent the unusual parts. Directed surface mining is already promised; deep drilling is outside this payoff.

Question: What job should that first day finish that would make you say the repair was worth the trouble?

Why it matters: F3. A ten-times-speed setting can pass arithmetic while yielding an awkward machine that needs constant attention or cannot reach useful work.

My guess, not your answer: A substantial excavation that frees several colonists for other work, with one area order and a clear service warning. Its power should be obvious through work completed.

What changes in the tree: A whole room/quarry task gives a completion and material-yield acceptance scene. A continuous industrial role needs a longer run with hauling and a service cycle. If the delight is watching the machine work, add a separate movement/sound judgment as well as completed work. The answer sets the payoff bar, not permission to weaken mining wherever surface cells are reachable.

07. A free machine doing you a favour

Picture: You freed the restored borehulk. Later, away from the Cathedral, you ask for help with a large marked excavation. It arrives as a friend, works for a while and returns home. There are more marked cells than it can finish during the provisional stay. It still takes no direct orders and never mines the Cathedral for you.

Question: What should you be able to count on getting done before it leaves?

Why it matters: F3. The free branch's favour is already settled. Its practical commitment is not settled by a provisional visit timer or an instantly appearing helper.

My guess, not your answer: A clearly useful share of the work, with a readable departure time and no disappearance in the middle of an unclear promise. It can leave unfinished work when that was clear from the start.

What changes in the tree: A timed contribution needs useful output and honest arrival/departure cues, including inaccessible work. A promised finite job needs a defined accepted job boundary and an ending condition. A task chosen by the machine needs its selection made legible. Every answer retains favours, no orders, cooldown and the Cathedral exclusion.
