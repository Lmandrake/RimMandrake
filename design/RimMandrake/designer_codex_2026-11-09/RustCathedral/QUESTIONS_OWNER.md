# RustCathedral

Your selected experience sentence: “A maze built by minds you'll never meet, still guarded by their machines. Salvage paradise with a temper.”

Everything below is my proposal or guess, not your words or an approved design.

RustCathedral is a place the colony learns to work within. Deliberate metal passages make the walls both routes and treasure: ordinary plate is free salvage, richer remains tempt you farther, and sacred structures and deep extraction carry consequences. Machines defend their ground rather than hunt visitors. The hum and the small living bolts' movements warn of the place's temper without explaining its secrets. Coolant eels and cleaning machines sustain its sparse mechanical life; a rare machinery cycle makes the plateau pause. A peaceful, worn-out borehulk offers a longer commitment: transport its heavy drill head for refurbishment, find unusual parts, and restore it. Keeping it earns powerful directed surface mining at an ongoing cost; freeing it earns favours elsewhere. Bolts aboard the ship remain hardy companions, secretly witness the colony's actions, and eventually force the established choice between keeping them and prying them off. The campaign reveals the hidden relationship at its proper time.

My main concern: these parts could work individually while the maze still feels accidental and its temper gives too little warning.

Recommended demonstration order: an ordinary salvage expedition, the borehulk's repair and useful work, then the ship bolts and an uneasy return.

## 1. A passage worth following

**Written picture:** Rich metal lies beyond a wall. Following the passage takes longer; cutting through ordinary plate saves time but may open directly into guarded ground. Ordinary plate remains freely mineable.

**Question:** What should make following the passage a worthwhile choice instead of always mining straight through?

**Why this matters:** The maze needs to shape an expedition, beyond looking deliberate from above.

**My guess:** Useful routes and recognisable protected places make shortcuts tempting but consequential, without charging a penalty for ordinary salvage itself.

## 2. A fair chance to stop

**Written picture:** Forty colonists are busy. Fishing and salvage overlap. The Cathedral becomes irritated while several jobs continue, and the player needs a chance to pause the offending work before things worsen.

**Mock needed:** A labelled audible and visual comparison of calm and irritated hum and bolt behaviour is needed before choosing their sound, movement or prominence. This written scene cannot settle those choices.

**Question:** Before consequences escalate, should a careful player be able to identify the particular work to stop, or only recognise that the place is upset and investigate?

**Why this matters:** A warning must support a useful response while preserving the mystery.

**My guess:** Noticeable unease should prompt investigation; a refusal on the particular order can identify the immediate problem without explaining the intelligence behind it.

## 3. Returning after an offence

**Written picture:** One crew took a few eels and left as irritation rose. Another damaged a sacred structure. Both return tomorrow for ordinary plate salvage. The campaign's deliberate betrayal remains a separate permanent loss.

**Question:** What should each crew have to do before working comfortably here again?

**Why this matters:** Minor disturbance and sacred damage need different, understandable consequences without making desecration impossible to recover from.

**My guess:** Time and careful work ease minor irritation; sacred damage requires costly, concrete repayment, without adding a new ritual.
