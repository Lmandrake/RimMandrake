PROPOSED ROOT

LeaningScrub is an endless moving fuzz plain where steady wind makes power plentiful and tall, dangerous venomvine makes footing costly. One wind shapes the plants, carries scent and drives fire, through a rich soundscape of small life. Beneath the canopy, a passing body sets off a wave of scattering, swaying and surfacing creatures; some burst into the air, others shed a twitching arm or retreat toward their home. The Stall exposes movement, while the Gale brings noise, turbine surges and breakdown risk, and a busier plain. Venomvine forms make islands with different dangers and rewards, creeping slowly downwind while the colony works its ways through. Fuzz and sweetline felt become blankets that bank premium fuel for future seasons; rare named sweetline trees gather coats, shed felt and keep a history. Dead farmsteads teach powered fog-water harvesting, and eligible scavenger clans can inhabit the land. Open fires bring giants down on the blaze and its maker; the calling-pyre makes that danger a deliberate, costly act. A viable home is possible here, but its people must earn their routes, read the wind and respect the life concealed beneath their feet.

My claim: the wind gives the colony dependable resources while the living ground makes every foothold something earned.
The strongest objection: this could become a collection of individually working effects with no satisfying ordinary day. The existing island walk even recorded a colonist being downed. The paragraph proposes the experience to prove, not a report that it already passes.
