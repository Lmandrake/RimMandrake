# LeaningScrub

The chosen experience sentence:

> Endless leaning grass, endless motion, endless venomvine. Power is free here, but footing is not.

Everything below is my proposal or guess, awaiting your response.

**Proposed root:** LeaningScrub is a vast, restless grassland where steady wind supplies plentiful power, but tall venomvine makes travel dangerous. The same wind bends plants, carries scent and drives fire. Small creatures scatter, surface and take flight beneath the moving cover. When the wind stalls, movement gives hidden animals away; a gale brings noise, extra turbine power and repairs. Vine islands offer different dangers and rewards as they slowly spread downwind. Rare sweetline trees collect animal coats and shed useful felt; blankets made with felt and fuzz turn covered vine stands into reserves of long-burning fuel for later seasons. Abandoned farmsteads teach powered fog-water collection. Giants descend on open fires and threaten their makers; a calling-pyre deliberately invites that danger. A colony can make a home here by learning the wind and earning its routes through living ground.

**Main concern:** Plenty of lively effects could still leave ordinary work exhausting rather than satisfying. A home must feel worth establishing without making the surrounding plain harmless.

**Recommended demonstration order:** A working colony through ordinary wind, a lull and a gale; then a purposeful vine crossing; then a sweetline harvest and seasonal fuel reserve.

## 1. What becomes familiar?

**Written picture:** By the end of the first season, a small colony has turbine power, modest fog-water collection and a frequently used route between vine islands. Animals still move under the grass, and work beyond that route takes planning.

**Question:** What should this colony now do comfortably, and what should still make stepping outside a considered choice?

**Why this matters:** This sets the balance between a dependable home and the difficult footing at the heart of the experience.

**My guess:** Home and a few maintained routes become dependable; journeys farther out still demand preparation and attention.

## 2. Who commits to the dangerous shortcut?

**Written picture:** A hauling job has two possible routes: a long clear detour around a vine island, or a short passage through venomous thorns. Separately, the player deliberately orders a prepared expedition through the island.

**Question:** What should let a colonist take the thorny passage during routine work, compared with an explicitly ordered expedition?

**Why this matters:** The vines' danger is established; the open choice is how much control the player has over accepting it.

**My guess:** Routine hauling uses a usable clear detour; an explicit expedition can accept the dangerous crossing.

## 3. How do giants identify the fire's maker?

**Written picture:** A colonist lights a patch and retreats behind a wall. One giant saw the act; another arrives after the colonist vanished. An innocent worker is still beside the spreading fire.

**Question:** How should the giants connect the fire to its maker, and what could that person do to escape?

**Why this matters:** Threatening the arsonist needs a believable connection to the act and a way to distinguish nearby bystanders.

**My guess:** Witnessing the act or following an immediate physical trail identifies the maker; breaking contact offers a chance to escape.
