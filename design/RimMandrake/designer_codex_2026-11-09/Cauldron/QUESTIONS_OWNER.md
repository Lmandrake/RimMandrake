# Cauldron — proposed direction

Your experience sentence:

> The sky roils with eternal anger and lightning yet no rain ever falls. Water is acid, animals are minerals, and life twists under the load… including you.

Everything below is my proposal or guess, pending your answers.

**Proposed root:** The Cauldron is a mineral forest beneath an angry sky of lightning without rain. Acid water and poisonous chemistry shape its plants, animals and the people who work there. Cold vents breathe flammable gas; a lull in their breathing and the stillness of nearby animals warn of a surge that burdens exposed bodies with metal. Thornwood yields wood and metal through slow, exposed work. Mineral creatures offer food, danger and protective material; some renew the forest, while the giant vexxiss competes for the vents the colony taps. Shelter, sealed clothing and filters let colonists make drinking water and useful chemicals from dangerous liquids. Learning to live here should bring a worthwhile livelihood within a strange living world. What happens to a person under that load remains unresolved: temporary poisoning, lasting injury and becoming something different are distinct promises, and this proposal does not yet settle yours.

**1. What does living here do to a person?**

**Written picture:** Two colonists work here for weeks. One shelters and wears sealed gear; the other repeatedly works exposed. They leave together. The current effects include metal poisoning that fades and acid wounds; the record establishes no lasting Cauldron-specific adaptation.

**Question:** After leaving, what should remain different about the exposed colonist to fulfil the final part of your sentence?

**Why this matters:** Recovery, permanent damage and bodily transformation lead to different experiences of living here.

**Agent’s guess:** Repeated exposure should have a cumulative bodily consequence that matters beyond a routine poison meter; its form and persistence still need your direction.

**2. What stops burning gas?**

**Agent’s guess and physical argument:** Fire should follow a continuous flammable cloud across water because the gas is the fuel. Water prevents a lasting ground fire; a break in the cloud or a sealed barrier stops the gas flame. Burning gas still produces toxic smoke.

**Written picture:** A cloud spans a pool and both banks. A spark on the near bank carries through the gas to the far bank. Nearby, another cloud is separated by a clear gap and stays unlit. This is a proposed rule, not a demonstrated scene.

**Question:** Does that fit the Cauldron, or should water itself provide a dependable boundary even beneath connected gas?

**Why this matters:** This determines whether colonists can rely on a pool for protection and how they contain a gas fire.

**3. What makes a successful day worth it?**

**Written picture:** A forty-colonist base has reliable roofs, sealed clothes, filters and protected ships. Machines run, workers return safely, and useful materials accumulate.

**Question:** Once that routine works, what should still shape the colony’s day, and what reward makes staying worthwhile?

**Why this matters:** Successful settlement needs a satisfying purpose beyond replenishing protection.

**Agent’s guess:** Competence buys dependable protection, manageable work and valuable output, while wildlife and exposed expeditions still create meaningful choices.

**Recommended demonstration order:** Survive a vent surge, turn local fluids into useful supplies, then handle a vexxiss competing for a tap.
