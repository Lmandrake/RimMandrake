# AtmosphericBase

Proposed root: AtmosphericBase lets other mods speak through a colony's existing lamps and ambient sound. You choose which lamps to hand over and can reclaim them with your own colours restored; an outside colour edit releases that lamp and preserves the edit. Animals, plants, worn gear and holograms keep their own light. In ordinary colonies, you choose beautiful, gentle patterns. Aboard an Utinni gravship, the gods choose: two presences hold separate, slowly moving areas while quieter gods briefly intrude in their own colours. Colour and movement express identity, and the light still affects colony life. The gods inhabit the wiring; they are not the ship's feelings. The Narrator interrupts everything, including alarms, with reserved white, then returns the lamps to whatever should now be showing. Chosen darkness can carry movement; loss of power silences the powered effects. Ambient sound follows the territory beneath the camera. AtmosphericBase supplies the shared effects; the campaign decides what they mean and what happens because of them. Both light and sound belong in the finished mod.

All wording here is my proposal, not your words. Light ownership, patterns, territory and local pauses exist; sound, general interruptions and the campaign connections that give these effects meaning are not established as built. The main risk is beautiful effects that convey no understandable presence.

1. **What joins the two experiences?**

   Picture: In an ordinary workshop, you choose a warm pattern. Aboard the Utinni, gods use those same kinds of lamps to address you, then yield to the Narrator.

   Question: What should make these feel like one mod, and where does the proposed root misstate that relationship?

   Why this matters: The shared purpose must explain both everyday beauty and presences acting for themselves.

   My guess: The lamps are a medium others can speak through; the Utinni is its richest use, while ordinary colony patterns are worthwhile on their own.

2. **Power changing versus a memory lingering**

   Picture — mock needed: Compare labelled light scenes: A, the same two gods' border drifts normally; B, a deed changes which god leads; C, a new occupant holds a lamp while traces of the previous occupant appear in its quieter moments. These are descriptions, not rendered comparisons.

   Question: What should a player understand about the gods' relationship after B that they should not infer from A or C? Choosing the visual cues needs the comparative mocks first.

   Why this matters: Ordinary movement, a change of power and a lingering memory could otherwise tell the same misleading story.

   My guess: B communicates a real change of leadership; C preserves a recognisable memory without suggesting that the former occupant still controls the lamp.

3. **What the predator's silence means**

   Picture — mock needed: Prepare a listening comparison beside a predator that silences biome ambience: A, the gods' sound briefly yields; B, their distinct voices continue. Keep the cathedral's existing hum behaviour fixed. No audio comparison exists here.

   Question: What must that sudden quiet still tell the player about the predator, even with gods present? The audible treatment should be chosen only after listening.

   Why this matters: Added atmosphere could obscure a survival warning.

   My guess: The predator must remain recognisable; any god's response should have a deliberate campaign meaning.

Recommended demonstration order: contested rooms with clearly labelled simulated gods, lamp handover and restoration, then the Narrator's interruption once light and sound can both support it.
