DRY RUN — AtmosphericBase, stages 0–1. All wording, pictures, recommendations and guesses below are agent text, not Mandrake's words. Nothing is approved or sent.

PROPOSED ROOT

AtmosphericBase lets other mods speak through a colony's existing lights and ambient sound. The player hands lamps over and can take them back with their own colour restored; animals, plants, worn gear and holograms keep their own light. Away from the Utinni, the handed-over lamps offer beautiful, gentle patterns the player chooses. Aboard an Utinni gravship, the gods choose: two presences hold separate, slowly moving areas, and quieter presences intrude briefly in their own colours. Hue and movement carry identity; real illumination makes their choices matter to colony life. The Narrator commands a reserved white above everything, even an alarm, then returns the lamps to what should be showing underneath. Chosen darkness carries motion; a power failure silences the powered channels. The gods are tenants using the wiring, never feelings of the ship itself. The framework supplies the common patterns, claims and restoration; the campaign supplies their meaning and consequences. Sound shares that language, with the camera as listener. Both channels belong to the finished mod.

This proposes the whole intended mod. Light ownership, patterns, territory and tremor are built; general gestures, sound, the witness ledger and gameplay consumers are not established as built in the current source. The inventory in record.json keeps those differences explicit.

My claim: the common idea is meaningful presences using familiar fixtures, with a useful neutral life outside the campaign.
The strongest objection: an attractive animation can pass every colour check while conveying no presence at all; the missing consumers and sound can also make a framework demonstration look more complete than the game actually is.

These are eight drafts for a question bank, not one eight-question turn. Begin with 01–02 and serve at most three at a time after independent review. A different picture or “this doesn't matter to me” is a usable answer. Silence settles nothing. The existing north-star section stays DRAFT and stopped; these drafts do not amend its bars or restart its automation. Later taste questions belong beside a labelled live exploration when that is available.

Aboard the Utinni, the same kinds of lamps become a conversation between occupants who choose for themselves. Later the Narrator interrupts them and gives the room back.

Question: What would make those feel like the same mod to you, and which sentence in the proposed root gets the relationship wrong?

Why it matters: F1/F2. A general lighting framework and a campaign's expressive language can coexist, but a root led entirely by either can obscure why the other belongs. Their built capabilities are already accounted for.

My guess, not your answer: The lamps are a medium others can use; the Utinni is its richest use. I recommend that emphasis, with the neutral patterns a complete experience of their own.

What changes in the tree: If expression leads, organise the root around who is speaking and what changes in play. If the reusable medium leads, organise it around ownership, claims and consumers. If ordinary colony beauty is equally central, give it an independent promise and scene. Every answer retains built coverage; correcting the paragraph changes dependent draft claims. No answer means root remains unapproved.

03. What a warning is allowed to tell you

Picture to react to — text mock of meaning, not lamp appearance: The normal pattern continues, then an unfamiliar pulse travels along the corridor toward the engine. The colony really has trouble, but a test author could point that pulse anywhere without consulting the trouble's location.

Question: What should an experienced player be entitled to conclude from that movement: who is demanding attention, what sort of trouble it is, where to act, or something else?

Why it matters: F1. An unmistakably different warning is already intended. A direction that looks useful but is merely decorative would promise information the framework does not possess.

My guess, not your answer: Identity and a recognisable warning are the common promise; a useful direction is earned only when a consumer supplies a real target. I recommend avoiding a false instruction to visit the engine.

What changes in the tree: Identity alone gives distinct warning vocabulary without a location promise. A trouble category needs authored, distinguishable categories. A location promise needs a real consumer target and an exploration where the trouble moves but the old location remains tempting. A deliberately deceptive god needs a specifically authored deception, not arbitrary wrong pointers. Lights still cause no raid or other unrelated world effect.

Basis: definition L1/L2 and §4; walk's alarm/omen intent; catalogue I-1. This does not reopen learning rules or propose an alarm exemption from the Narrator.

04. The moment a new god takes the front

Picture to react to — symbolic timing mock, not an in-game render. A and B mean occupants, not RGB values; each position stands for a room, and actual lamps are irregular:

```text
Before                  A A A | B B
Ordinary drifting       A A B | B A     continues on the usual slow clock
A distinct handover     B A A | B B  -> B B A | B B
```

Question: After a deed really changes who is in front, what should let you notice that this was a change of power rather than the border's ordinary wandering?

Why it matters: F1. The source can reorder claims and the neighbour already owns the front decision. Neither fact settles how the player recognises the transition. A pretty moving border can conceal the event.

My guess, not your answer: A brief, recognisable handover in the incoming presence's own motion, then ordinary contested territory resumes. I recommend exploring that against a control with the same two occupants but no change of front.

What changes in the tree: If a distinct handover matters, add a bounded transition promise tied to the real front event. If changed territory and the occupants' usual motions suffice, require correspondence to the event without inventing another flourish. If a spoken acknowledgement carries the event, the campaign owns that acknowledgement and its witness rule. The framework still reads rank from outside; this asks no new ranking algorithm.

05. A trace of the previous occupant

Picture to react to — symbolic sequence, not a colour mock. A fixture changes to B's territory. During B's low moments it briefly shows A again: `B B A B B A B`. The built scar mechanism does this; Rekko's authored readings already request a four-minute memory, marked provisional. A viewer could instead read it as ownership changing back and forth.

Question: What would make that old rhythm feel like a memory of A rather than A still controlling the lamp?

Why it matters: F1. Scar is built, so its presence is not up for keeping. Its meaning has to survive beside both an actual moving border and a third occupant's brief intrusion.

My guess, not your answer: B's continuing motion and peaks should make present ownership clear while A remains recognisable in the low moments. I recommend showing it beside a genuine return of A and a third-presence flicker.

What changes in the tree: If the new occupant's motion carries ownership, require it to remain recognisable through the trace. If the surrounding border provides the explanation, stage the trace with enough territory in view and check that context. If an inspect explanation is essential, add it as a supporting readout while retaining the intended visual distinction. If he delegates the exact cue, record that delegation and compare labelled explorations. The built memory and its authored exception stay accounted for; no answer licenses a third territorial owner or interpolated colours.

Basis: RM_Scar.Decide, the compositor's scar path and RUT_NineSignatures.xml's Rekko readings; god-patterns M3. Existing provisional timing is reported as built data, not attributed to him.

06. What a listening room says

Picture to react to — written thought experiment, not a capture: A colonist performs a deed in a room. A consumer has honestly established a witness before requesting the built hush. That room's moving lamps settle to a held colour, then recover; the next room continues normally.

Question: What should that pause mean to the player: someone listened, someone approved, someone is about to answer, or another meaning?

Why it matters: F1/F2. A local hush is built, but the witness ledger and its consumers are not. Reusing the same pause for approval and menace could turn an intended acknowledgement into a false promise.

My guess, not your answer: It means attention was received, with approval or displeasure carried by the occupant's other behaviour. I recommend keeping the hush distinct from the Narrator's ship-wide white hold.

What changes in the tree: Attention alone needs a witness-gated acknowledgement with no guaranteed reward. Approval needs a matching campaign state and a control where the deed is seen but disapproved. A coming answer needs a defined follow-up or an honest expiry. If the meaning varies by occupant, each authored use must establish its meaning; the framework does not infer a witness from the effect it just produced.

Basis: RM_AtmosphericBaseAPI.Hush; RM_Hush; definition L14; god-patterns M4.

07. Two letters before the lights have returned

Picture to react to — written transition mock, not built gameplay: The Narrator's first letter is open and the lamps hold white. A second authored Narrator letter arrives. Underneath, an alarm and the two occupants' patterns continue to have their own current state.

Question: What should you see as you read and dismiss those two letters: one continuous address, a fresh rise for each, or a deliberate sequence of separate performances?

Why it matters: F2/F3. White priority, real time through pause and returning to the current underlying pattern are settled. Overlapping addresses still need an identity and end condition; a timer-only gesture can finish while someone is still reading or strand white forever.

My guess, not your answer: One continuous white address while any Narrator letter being addressed remains open, then one return. I recommend it because a second white rise from white carries little new information.

What changes in the tree: A continuous address needs overlapping lifetime ownership and a last-close rule. Separate rises need an observable restart without losing the underlying alarm. A queue needs cancellation and close-order rules. All variants retain the Narrator above the alarm and restore the current underlying state, including any intervening external lamp edit. Reload still restarts gently; it does not acquire mid-gesture persistence through this question.

Basis: definition L10–L13; review package §9.10; current ProofSample explicitly reports gesture/narrator scenarios unbuilt.

08. When another creature makes the world go quiet

Picture to react to — listening scene description, not generated audio: You move the camera from one occupant's rooms to another's. AtmosphericBase's intended bed follows that territory. Nearby, a silence-aura predator triggers CreatureBehaviors' hush of biome ambience; RustCathedral's independent hum may also be present.

Question: During that predator's silence, what should remain audible from the gods, and what should the sudden quiet mean?

Why it matters: F1/F2. Camera listening, both channels shipping together and leaving the cathedral hum's system alone are settled. The predator currently ends biome ambient sounds; it has no agreement with the future AtmosphericBase beds. Simply adding another loop could erase a meaningful survival cue.

My guess, not your answer: The predator cue should stay legible, while the occupants' response has an authored meaning of its own. I recommend comparing a bed that yields briefly with one that continues as an unmistakably separate voice, using existing cathedral behaviour as a fixed neighbour.

What changes in the tree: Yielding adds a silence-event interaction and exact recovery of AtmosphericBase's own bed. Continuing requires the predator cue to remain understandable alongside it, judged by listening. A god-specific response belongs in the campaign's authored mapping. Nothing here authorises editing the cathedral hum, inferring a current third-party mod list, or turning sound playtest notes into an automated gate.

Basis: definition §3; hook ecosystem §5; current CreatureBehaviors silence cue and RustCathedral hum source. The audio itself is unbuilt here, so this is a future interaction decision.
