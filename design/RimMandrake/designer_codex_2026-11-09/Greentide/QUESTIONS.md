PROPOSED ROOT

Greentide is a steaming river jungle whose abundance makes entering it worthwhile and whose living pressure makes holding a home there a daily struggle. Food, timber, strange catches and useful medicines come from the same place that hides predators, swarms with stinging insects, exhausts its workers with fever and swallows people and supplies in churnmud. A colony earns working space: it clears routes, protects its doorways with dry-air blowers, makes firm ground, pulls people free and digs its belongings back out. A greatbole offers another bargain: carve shelter and valuable heartwood from a living trunk, seal the chambers you mean to keep, and face increasingly violent consequences if you take too much. Learned tools and fever survivors make the colony more capable; the jungle's danger does not rise to cancel that learning. Success is an abundant, hard-won foothold that still needs care, with understandable warnings and a chance to act when its defenses fail.

The root grows from the owner-chosen Greentide experience in `design/RimMandrake/biome_experiences_2026-10-20.md`, row Greentide. The paragraph above is my proposed wording, not his quotation or approval. It describes the promise to establish, not a claim that the current build passes it.

My claim: the through-line is earning and maintaining space in an overwhelmingly productive place.
The strongest objection: the implementation can supply many dangers and rewards without ever making the jungle contest an ordinary base from day to day. Faster plant growth, a successful mud probe and a spawned predator would not establish that experience.

These eight drafts are a bank. Ask **01 and 02 first**, then at most three in a turn as the answers make the others useful. Each picture is a written thought experiment, not a capture, mock image or staged game. A correction, a different picture, or “this doesn't matter to me” is a useful answer. Silence settles nothing.

01. ASK FIRST — What must the root get right?

Picture: A small colony has plenty to eat, a workshop and a safe route to the river. Its people spend part of every day holding back the green. When they neglect that work, they start losing access to what they built.

Question: What in that day makes it Greentide to you, and where does the proposed paragraph tell the wrong story?

Why it matters: F1/F2. The chosen experience names both abundance and a base that can be lost. The root needs the right relationship between them; a jungle that merely slows walking could pass weaker words.

My guess, not your answer: The jungle should feel worth living with, and capable of taking back an unattended foothold. I would make that daily bargain the lead.

What changes in the tree: If defending inhabited space is central, the root gets a colony-upkeep acceptance scene with visible loss after neglect. If expeditions are the stronger picture, lead with reaching, collecting and getting home while retaining the built settlement systems and their coverage. If another story leads, rewrite the root and its dependent claims. No answer leaves `root` unapproved.

02. ASK FIRST — The first demonstration

Picture: A, an ordinary working settlement whose doorway defense fails and is recovered; B, a team crossing the jungle, rescuing a mired companion and returning with a useful harvest; C, a colony carving and sealing a greatbole chamber, then taking enough wood to provoke the tree.

Question: Which would you watch first to decide whether this mod has understood Greentide, and what failure in it would make you stop the demonstration?

Why it matters: F1/F2/F3. The current script mainly isolates mud and settings. It does not establish the biome's face. Root approval and choosing the first showcase are separate decisions.

My guess, not your answer: A, because it most directly tests the daily struggle in the chosen experience. B and C deserve separate scenes with their own controls.

What changes in the tree: His recorded choice names showcase 1 and its rejection case. A prioritizes the interaction of growth, defense and access; B prioritizes travel, rescue and reward; C prioritizes shelter, sealing and the harvest ladder. The other built capabilities remain covered. Further showcases must add substantially different play; none replaces individual feature proof.

03. A doorway loses its dry air

Picture: A blower runs out of chemfuel while the colony is busy elsewhere. The current field has a provisional six-hour hold after its last refresh. Later the doorway is overgrown, and a colonist is on the other side. This is a proposed failure scene; that whole sequence has not been established by the existing script.

Question: What should be the first sign that makes you send someone, and what should still be recoverable when they finally get there?

Why it matters: F1/F3. “Within hours” and “lose your base” need an actual sequence of neglect, warning and recovery. An immediate silent lockout and an endlessly harmless outage tell very different stories.

My guess, not your answer: The failed machine is obvious before access is lost; arriving late costs clearing and hauling work, with a practical way to reach anyone stranded. I would test the first warning and a deliberately late response separately.

What changes in the tree: A machine warning makes the failure cue and its timing essential. A visible advancing growth front makes that front and its relation to access essential. A recoverable blockage needs an actual clearing/restart scene; lasting damage needs the precise damage and warning established. An expectation of eventual evacuation needs a bounded loss scene. None changes the already-built blower's two jobs or the later light-gated growth ruling.

04. Pulled free, still in the mud

Picture: One colonist is stuck in churnmud. A companion reaches them and finishes pulling them free. The current job clears the mire condition but leaves both people where they stood; the mud can start affecting the rescued person again.

Question: What should you see them do next for that rescue to feel finished?

Why it matters: F3. A health-tab change can pass while the same person immediately needs rescuing again. The question is the end of the rescue, not whether the already-built job belongs.

My guess, not your answer: A conscious person should have enough of a chance to get onto the nearby causeway, with the helper able to finish the rescue rather than become the second casualty. I would show the actual walk afterwards.

What changes in the tree: If freedom to walk is enough, prove an ordinary move onto safe ground after the pull. If the helper must deliver them to firm footing, add that specific transport behavior and a trapped-helper control. If a short recovery window is needed, define its end and re-entry behavior. If repeated rescue on hazardous ground is intended, state that boundary plainly and require a usable escape order rather than calling a momentary cleared hediff success.

05. A crossing you have paid to establish

Picture: The colony has learned about false banks, cleared an illisk shoal with explosives and built a bridge onto a maintained causeway. A bridge avoids immersion; it does not by itself secure the approach. The current vurrak can lie on standable ground near water, and a dhollock can reach prey beside the water. These creatures can still threaten this route.

Question: What would make you trust this particular route to unescorted haulers, and what would make you send an escort or repair crew back?

Why it matters: F1/F3. The player must gain something by learning and building. “No safe standing water” does not by itself settle whether a completed route needs ordinary upkeep, active defense or both.

My guess, not your answer: The bridge earns a dry crossing; surveying and guarding the approaches earn a route. I would test that practical preparation before assuming ordinary bridges or causeways are safe from the creatures already built.

What changes in the tree: If a surveyed, separated approach earns reliable hauling, demonstrate that geometry against an exposed crossing. If patrols or escorts are essential, show their work and a route left unguarded. If no route can be trusted unattended, define the observable reason and a useful player response. Gradual obstruction needs maintenance and recovery; structural damage needs its named existing trigger or an explicitly new interaction. No answer silently grants safe water, deletes the river creatures or scales danger with colony progress.

06. Smoke solves the animal encounter; what is the trip still costing?

Picture: An experienced team carries stench smoke, uses it to drive animals away and gathers food and useful plant materials. The smoke must work on every animal, including the colony's own livestock; that is already settled. They still have to walk through the jungle, work, carry their haul and return.

Question: Which part of that well-prepared outing should still make you decide whether today's harvest is worth the trip?

Why it matters: F2. Preparation should pay without turning the generous jungle into a meaningless collection run. This asks what the remaining bargain is, not for permission to weaken the smoke counter.

My guess, not your answer: Time, carrying capacity, heat, footing and the risk of overstaying for another load. I would start by comparing one short haul with a team that reaches for a second.

What changes in the tree: If travel and hauling lead, test a useful load brought home under those costs. If exposure and recovery lead, test time spent working plus the return journey, with equipped controls. If maintaining access leads, tie the outing to a route that can actually be maintained. If a prepared outing should be comfortably profitable, that earned payoff becomes the bar; no new tax or stronger swarm is inferred. All existing rewards stay in the inventory.

07. Forty colonists, a familiar jungle

Picture: A forty-person colony has sealed its storerooms, maintains blower doors and has assigned people to clear routes. The jungle has not become stronger. The player is trying to plan the next day's work without watching every worker's feet.

Question: What would you expect to delegate to those colonists, and what event should still make you take over personally?

Why it matters: F1/F3. Daily resistance can become either satisfying colony work or repeated manual orders. The core experience has to survive ordinary work priorities and a larger settlement.

My guess, not your answer: Routine refueling, clearing, hauling and digging should follow assignments; a failed defense, a person stuck out of reach or a badly timed herd deserves attention. I would test unattended routine work before judging its dramatic interruption.

What changes in the tree: Work assignments as the normal answer require autonomous completion and recovery from interruption. Deliberate direct orders as the normal answer require an explicit, tolerable repeated task and immediate feedback. Attention only on exceptional events requires honest warnings and a routine interval that needs no intervention. His answer changes the upkeep and scale leaves; it does not add difficulty scaling or remove hazards.

08. Deliberately giving someone the Frenzy

Picture: A deadline is close. A colonist is given a harvested dose, works faster, collapses and is nursed through the coma. The dose, dangerous illness and lasting survivor mark are already built; this is not a question about those belonging.

Question: What should the colonist and the people around them make of the decision to give that dose?

Why it matters: F2/F3. The disease item's own Watch out leaves the social response unresolved. A planned sacrifice, a desperate volunteer and routine drug use produce different colony stories even if the same work and coma occur.

My guess, not your answer: Treat it as an acknowledged risky medical choice, with the illness and nursing burden doing most of the work; I would add no automatic colony-wide moral verdict without his direction.

What changes in the tree: If the existing health and treatment consequences tell the whole story, add no social mechanic. A volunteer distinction needs an explicit player action and a consequence tied to that distinction. Resentment, gratitude or an ideological judgment needs the particular memory/precept response he names and a control where the choice differs. None reopens the harvestable route, deliberate dosing, coma, survivor immunity or settled removal of the mild illnesses.
