# Greentide

Chosen experience:
> Abundance with teeth: everything grows, and everything sinks. Fight the jungle every day or lose your base to it.

Everything below is my proposal, not an approved description or your wording.

## Proposed root

Greentide is a steaming river jungle full of food, timber, strange catches and useful medicines. That abundance comes with predators, stinging insects, fever and mud that swallows people and supplies. Keeping a home means fighting back every day: clearing routes, protecting doorways with dry-air blowers, making firm ground, rescuing people and digging belongings out. Neglect lets the jungle reclaim the base. A greatbole, a giant living trunk, offers shelter and valuable heartwood, but taking too much provokes increasingly violent consequences. Preparation and experience should make life easier without the jungle growing stronger to cancel those gains. Success is a rich, hard-won home that still needs care, with warnings and a chance to respond when its defenses fail.

My concern: plentiful resources and isolated dangers could still leave ordinary colony life untouched by the jungle.

## 1. The daily bargain

**Written picture:** A small colony has full food stores, a workshop and a cleared river route. Its people spend part of each day keeping the green back. Neglect starts costing them access to what they built.

**Question:** What essential part of your chosen experience does this day, or the proposed paragraph, still miss?

**Why this matters:** The everyday struggle must make the abundance worth defending and the threat of losing the base real.

**My guess:** Maintaining an inhabited foothold should lead, with dangerous harvesting trips feeding that struggle.

## 2. An unattended doorway

**Written picture:** A doorway's dry-air blower runs out of fuel while everyone is busy elsewhere. Growth eventually blocks the entrance, leaving someone outside. This is a proposed situation, not a demonstrated sequence.

**Question:** If the colony responds late, what should it still be able to recover, and what should prolonged neglect put at lasting risk?

**Why this matters:** Losing a base needs understandable consequences and a workable chance to intervene.

**My guess:** An early warning buys time; a late response costs clearing and hauling work, while continued neglect can force evacuation and leave supplies behind. Reaching a stranded person should remain practical.

## 3. When is a mud rescue finished?

**Written picture — mock needed for movement:** A helper pulls a colonist free of churnmud. Currently both remain in the mud, so the rescued person can become stuck again. No movement mock is supplied here; this question concerns the rescue's responsibility.

**Question:** Should the rescue task end once a conscious person can escape under their own orders, or only once the helper has delivered them to firm ground?

**Why this matters:** A rescue should give the player a usable escape rather than an immediate repeat emergency.

**My guess:** The helper should get them to nearby firm ground, with a practical escape for the helper too; the movement needs a mock before judging how it looks.

Recommended demonstration order: a working settlement losing and recovering a doorway, then a harvest trip with a mud rescue, then greatbole shelter and overharvesting.
