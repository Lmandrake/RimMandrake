# TheSump

Your experience sentence:

> Sticky, stinking, slow to move or burn or surrender. The sump hides all and makes you fight it for every action.

Everything below is an agent proposal or guess, for your reaction.

**Proposed root**

TheSump makes life in a cold tar basin a struggle over ordinary actions: moving, working, staying clean and getting back what the black has kept. The colony learns from mice and plants, picks routes through sticky ground, and turns troublesome tar into useful materials, beautiful gaslight and storage that preserves things but needs solvent to release them. Digging can uncover ancient finds, preserved traps or something much larger below. Channels and gates put tar to work, while a belch or careless fire can change familiar ground. A normally disturbed tar beast threatens to eat the station, giving people reason to evacuate; deliberately pouring solvent instantly wakes a beast that hunts them. Tools make settlement possible, but the Sump keeps resisting.

The risk in this proposal: coping could become repeated chores, or become so easy that the place loses its resistance.

**1. A colony that knows the place**

**Written picture:** Forty colonists have glass walkways, stocked solvent, food storage and dependable lamps. Outside work still brings tar home.

**Question:** Once the colony is equipped, what should still need your attention each day, and what should its workers handle routinely?

**Why this matters:** The colony should keep making meaningful choices without needing the same cleaning and food-retrieval clicks all day.

**Agent's guess:** Route planning, work sites and spending scarce effort stay with the player; supplied workers handle routine cleaning and food retrieval. A capable colony remains dirty.

**2. Time to react to a trap**

Skilled disarm is already settled. Automatically assigning an unqualified miner is a defect to fix, not a decision for you.

**Written picture:** Digging arms a trap. Workers are nearby; the qualified miner is elsewhere. Assume the player has noticed the danger. This picture concerns available actions and time, not the warning's appearance or sound.

**Question:** Before it explodes, should there be time only to clear nearby workers, or also to bring a qualified miner from a nearby work site?

**Why this matters:** This sets whether the danger offers escape alone or a practical chance to save the dig.

**Agent's guess:** Both should be possible when the specialist is nearby; reaching a distant specialist need not be safe.

**3. What evacuation saves**

**Written picture:** The ordinary station-eating beast gives its already-required day-ahead warning. People can outwalk it, but beds, workshops and valuable stores fill the station. This is separate from the instant solvent-triggered hunter.

**Question:** What should a successful evacuation let the colony carry away, and what should it expect to lose?

**Why this matters:** Retreat needs meaningful salvage choices as well as a way to keep people alive.

**Agent's guess:** Everyone and selected valuable stores can escape; saving every building is too much to expect.

**Recommended demonstration order:** An ordinary colony day, then a digging expedition, then a station evacuation.

**Route appearance — mock needed:** Before asking which signs distinguish a changed route from yesterday's safe path, show a labelled comparison with an unchanged route and old tracks still present.
