# Wasteland

Agent proposal for discussion; only the quoted experience sentence below is yours.

Your recorded experience sentence: “Where rivers come to die and nothing ever leaves: salt, ash and fallout pile up forever. The land is the monster, and the creatures are its victims.”

**Proposed root**

The Wastes is where a ruined world's rivers end and its salt, ash and fallout stay. Living here means choosing where to shelter, where to work and how much contamination a useful find is worth. The creatures are victims of this land, surviving its poison; even the enormous, non-hostile Middenshell belongs to that relationship. Some survivors offer uneasy bargains: a herd turns waste into goods, while a living furnace warms a room and exposes everyone inside to radiation. Paid waste deliveries leave real casks to contain and handle. Ash storms uncover buried salvage; brine beds and poisoned wrecks tempt people out again. The Middenshell's warned passage can flatten buildings, waste can draw it aside, and its dead shell becomes a quarry worked at a cost in exposure. Ordinary days must make the accumulated poison matter as much as these events do.

**My concern:** profitable animals and a spectacular giant could overwhelm the experience of living on dangerous ground. Plants that help people judge exposure, and storms that change ground hazards, still need to be established in play.

### 1. An ordinary working day

**Written picture:** A week has passed. No storm is running and the giant has gone. A worker leaves a sheltered workshop to recover something valuable from a wreck, then returns home.

**Question:** What should make that ordinary day a difficult choice: preparing the outing, deciding how long to stay, keeping home habitable, or something else?

**Why this matters:** The land needs to shape daily life between dramatic events.

**Agent's guess:** Choosing a worthwhile outing, limiting exposure and returning to somewhere that allows recovery should carry the experience.

### 2. Judging a route after a storm

**Written picture — mock needed:** Two routes reach the same wreck. One crosses fresh ash; the other passes dense growth. Neither description establishes which is safer. A labelled route-reading comparison is needed before choosing visual cues; no such mock is supplied here.

**Question:** Before sending a scout, how much certainty about exposure should the player have: a rough safer-or-worse comparison, or enough information to estimate a tolerable visit?

**Why this matters:** Plants are meant to reveal dangerous ground; the useful precision of that information remains open.

**Agent's guess:** A dependable rough comparison should support route choices, with closer inspection helping decide how long to stay. Appearance should be chosen from the mock later.

### 3. After the last waste payment

**Written picture:** The final paid delivery arrives. Casks remain. Later the colony buries one or departs by ship. At present, discovery of illicit burial is checked only while the contract remains active.

**Question:** What responsibility to the people paying for disposal should continue after the contract ends, especially when the colony leaves?

**Why this matters:** Lasting pollution and lasting contractual responsibility are separate promises.

**Agent's guess:** Continuing obligations should be stated before acceptance, with departure requiring a clear handoff of responsibility; physical contamination persists regardless.

**Recommended demonstration order:** ordinary salvage outing after an ash storm, then waste handling, then Middenshell diversion and a later quarry expedition.
