# LanternDeeps

Everything below is my proposal or guess, except this quoted experience sentence of yours:

> “A deep wondrous cavern of strange crystal life and haunted technology. Mineral riches for kings, until you realize they are both alive and intelligent.”

**Proposed root:** LanternDeeps takes an expedition into a wondrous cavern of glowing crystal, fungal pastures and abandoned equipment. The riches draw you in; discovering that the crystals and haunted machines are alive and intelligent changes what taking those riches means. Ordinary lamps help you work but attract danger. Living Lantern crystals offer safe light while you leave them unharvested. Orun-Ghal, a skeleton inside a walking mining suit, is an inhabitant you can study and befriend, never a worker or boss. You return to the same cavern, with the consequences of earlier visits still there. The free mod provides this place and its inhabitants; campaign additions bring particular rare minerals and uses for mindstone.

My concern: the discovery could remain something a message explains, without changing how an expedition treats the inhabitants.

**Recommended demonstration order:** discovery among the abandoned machines, a return to Orun-Ghal, then mining and camp life with their risks.

**1. The turn from riches to inhabitants**

**Written picture:** An expedition gathers valuable crystal and salvaged equipment. Later, it discovers that the crystals and machines it treated as resources are intelligent inhabitants.

**Question:** Does that discovery carry the main promise of LanternDeeps, or does my proposed root give the wrong weight to wonder, wealth or the relationship that follows?

**Why this matters:** This sets what the whole experience needs to deliver beyond a beautiful, profitable cavern.

**My guess:** Recognition is the turning point; wonder and wealth make it worth reaching.

**2. Recognising a friend**

**Written picture — mock needed for any greeting or movement:** After repeated study visits, the expedition returns to Orun-Ghal. Its description says it knows them, and it sometimes approaches colonists. This is a proposed comparison to explore, not a demonstrated encounter.

**Question:** What would make that return feel like meeting a friend, beyond being told that friendship has increased?

**Why this matters:** Friendship needs a recognisable payoff; how it is learned is already established.

**My guess:** Orun-Ghal recognises familiar visitors and comes to spend time near them, while remaining an independent inhabitant. A mock should test whether that reads as affection.

**3. Something enormous farther down**

**Written picture — mock needed for scale and passage:** Miners work beside a deeper void occupied by an enormous creature. Its presence may force them to yield space. The creature and encounter remain undecided; Galuush already fills a separate role as a mostly stationary chamber landmark.

**Question:** What decision should encountering this giant put before the crew: wait for a safe opportunity, take another route, or something else?

**Why this matters:** A giant needs a meaningful place in a cavern the player repeatedly visits, with a believable way to inhabit or pass through it.

**My guess:** A resident of the deeper void makes the crew plan around its presence. Explore the space and encounter before choosing its species or exact movement.
