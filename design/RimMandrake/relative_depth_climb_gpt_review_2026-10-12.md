**I would not approve B as “low risk” yet. Its reachability lemma is false, and wall-scrambling changes gameplay materially.** Review below uses the supplied production code; the claimed vanilla 1.6 internals remain assumptions because those implementations were not attached.

1. **Blocker — the reachability lemma fails under both B and A.** Consider a map-spanning sheer D4 trench separating two surface areas. Every surface cell is non-trap; vanilla can cross the trench. B’s `AvoidPits` blocks that crossing; A’s mask blocks the cliff edges. “Each reaches some surface” does not imply mutual reachability. **Fix:** remove the lemma; check connectivity in the actual movement graph, including routes between non-trap endpoints.

2. **Blocker — a terrain flood does not establish pawn reachability.** Walls, forbidden doors, fences, water and blocked diagonal corners can disconnect a nominal ramp from the pawn. Production `TryGetTrapRegion` and `PitComponent` already merge cells using geometric eight-neighbour adjacency, ignoring these constraints. **Fix:** distinguish terrain escapability from usable routes; validate component traversal and exit access against the request’s movement rules.

3. **Blocker — one map-wide trap bit cannot include selective ladders.** Seeding the flood with a lowered ladder makes the whole basin non-trap, potentially releasing prisoners who cannot climb it. Excluding that ladder strands authorized users unless another layer handles them. **Fix:** cache structural components independently of ladders; evaluate usable ladder exits per pawn and permission state. Do not remove confinement throughout the component merely because one pawn has an exit.

4. **Major — covers are not interchangeable with ramps.** Production excludes covered neighbours from `TryGetTrapRegion`, despite its comment saying an enqueued cover would establish an exit. The follower explicitly blocks climbing onto a cover. B would instead let one cover untrap a basin and permit escape anywhere. **Fix:** define separate deck and floor states, with explicit permitted transitions; keep collapse handling independent of escape classification.

5. **Blocker — A needs request-specific edge permissions, not wholesale unmasking.** Restoring the entire vanilla array for a ladder user permits every cliff crossing. Keeping only ladder-source bits does not restore the reverse surface→ladder edge. Own-faction immunity currently affects capture; outside pathing still avoids pits. **Fix:** specify exemption semantics and construct connectivity for each movement/permission class, restoring only authorized ladder edges in both directions; handle flyers separately.

6. **Major — “same predicate” does not guarantee mask correctness.** `ComputeAll` and incremental updates must preserve vanilla and other mods’ connectivity restrictions. Array replacement must respect scheduled job lifetimes. **Fix:** derive filtered snapshots from the actual vanilla mask; verify initialization, incremental updates, request parameterization and disposal against the exact installed 1.6 build. Do not rely on ambient “current request” state without proving scheduling safety.

7. **Major — diagonal escape rules are missing.** A D4 core can touch D2 diagonally while both orthogonal cells prevent passage. The floods count an exit; the pathfinder can reject it. Conversely, endpoint depths alone may permit diagonal passage across a cliff corner. **Fix:** define one diagonal legality rule and use it in classification, route planning, connectivity masks and follower checks.

8. **Major — R-deep is directional as written.** “Only climbs out of D4 are limited” is asymmetric; “block steep drops too” changes that rule. Depth increases downward, so A’s labels for climb/drop inequalities are reversed. **Fix:** specify `CanStep(from, to, pawn)` explicitly. For asymmetric movement, find escapable cells by traversing **reverse** edges from exits; mutual components require strongly connected components.

9. **Major — existing reachability vetoes leave important gaps.** They skip pawnless queries and starts other than `pawn.Position`. `CanReachMapEdge` checks only confinement at the origin. `AllowedDestination` and `DestNeedsPit` approximate endpoint modes with an expanded rectangle, which does not establish a legal interaction or touch position. **Fix:** validate actual endpoint candidates and arbitrary starts; include permissions, endpoint mode and topology generation in query caches.

10. **Major — the ladder planner already confuses “standable” with “reachable.”** `BestLip` can choose an inaccessible lip; the nearest ladder may be behind a wall while another works. `StayInPit` permits every open pit on the map, despite its component-specific description. Replacing requests also risks losing another mod’s customizer or request settings. **Fix:** select reachable exit edges, restrict containment to the relevant component, and preserve/compose request customization.

11. **Major — rooms cannot depend on individual captivity.** Width, faction, flight and ladder permissions differ per pawn; room membership does not. Lowering a selective ladder should not automatically erase a prison room. `regionSide` also caches its old classification. **Fix:** choose a pawn-independent structural room boundary, publish a complete classification before rebuilding regions, and invalidate every affected region/district plus reachability caches.

12. **Major — depth writes are insufficient invalidation triggers.** Exit usability changes when ladders are raised, covers collapse, buildings obstruct ramps, settings change or natural beds transition. Current per-tick caches can also remain stale after mutations within the same tick. **Fix:** use explicit topology and permission generations; batch mutations and publish consistent trap, pathing and room data together. Initialize derived bed data before the first region build.

13. **Major — large lakes expose existing caps and costly churn.** `TryGetTrapRegion` declares a component untrapped at 4,000 cells; other searches truncate it. Thus reachability can approve escape while the follower still refuses it. Width checks cost up to O(W⁴) per visited cell. **Fix:** remove “cap reached ⇒ escapable”; cache complete component IDs, exit summaries and width eligibility, using reusable buffers.

14. **Major — “once per tick” can still be expensive.** Pumping can dirty thousands of cells every tick. Pathing scans the pit signature, rebuilds two whole-map arrays and retains old arrays for 2,500 ticks. On a 250×250 map, rebuilding every tick retains roughly **625 MB** of those arrays alone. **Fix:** batch hydrology transitions, replace signatures with generations, rebuild only when passability changes, and retire snapshots using actual job/cache ownership rather than elapsed time.

15. **Major — trap status is the wrong fall predicate.** A forced D0→D4 arrival into an escapable bowl is still a fall; stepping into a trapping cell need not involve a substantial drop. Current detection also misses same-cell changes, conflates transitions between samples and treats every arrival at D4 as equivalent. **Fix:** detect falls from movement events and effective height loss, recording origin, destination, movement type and handled status. Keep injury/spike events separate from captivity and jumper lifetime.

16. **Major — W×W eligibility is neither component-wide nor an edge rule.** One too-narrow reachable arm currently makes the whole component “untrapped,” although `PlanRoute` requires an unheld cell touching a lip. Measuring width over derived trap bits can also change body-size eligibility far from an edited ramp. A would still mask the cliff a large animal is supposed to climb. **Fix:** retain a geometric width predicate and represent the size exemption as explicit usable escape edges; verify those edges are reachable.

17. **Major — natural beds need a shared effective-depth accessor.** Updating only `IsTrapCell` leaves `SuperdeepCellCount`, `SuperdeepCells`, `IsSuperdeepRaw`, descent detection and step costs blind to derived beds. Wet natural water must not automatically become walkable excavation. **Fix:** provide effective bed depth, deck height and liquid/passability separately; audit early-outs, enumeration, load initialization and all movement consumers.

18. **Major — B’s wall-scrambling is gameplay, not merely cosmetic.** One ramp effectively opens every wall: raiders gain shortcuts, fleeing animals bypass the exit, and haulers/rescuers approach from any side. The speed patch cannot steer A* toward ramps, and `DryStepBaseCost` charges **zero** for downward steps into D4. **Fix:** retain restrictions on sheer boundary crossings and route through explicit permitted exits.

19. **Major — AI coverage is asserted, not demonstrated.** Raiders, animals and fleeing pawns can select destinations without using the patched query shape. Hauling/rescue can succeed outbound but fail while carrying back. Lip-service redirection changes pathing, not necessarily job eligibility or interaction completion. **Fix:** test these behaviours end to end, including denied ladders, blocked ramps, carried pawns and topology changes during active jobs.

20. **Minor — a simpler approximation suffices for R-deep.** Retain D4 components and recognize legal D4↔D2/D3 boundary edges as **automatic ramp exits**. Extend the existing staged ladder entry/exit planner to those edges, while retaining the lip veto elsewhere. This handles dried ponds and player-dug steps without a general flood over every depth or a global cliff mask. **Fix:** recommend this constrained approximation first; reserve A for exact movement at every depth. Withdraw the 1–2-day estimate until connectivity, invalidation and large-lake behaviour are proved.
