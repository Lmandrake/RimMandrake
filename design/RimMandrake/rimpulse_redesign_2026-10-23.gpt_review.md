The document treats noise removal as the redesign. The deeper problem is an unreliable contract: what appears, what is ready, what persists, and what answering actually changes.

**1. Inventory and interaction taxonomy**

- Rank **ready review sheets, review pictures, and question cards** together as “For you.” A blocking card does not automatically outrank a promised review or a change to kept art. Rank by consequence, deadline, and downstream impact.
- Separate **owner attention** from **run health**. An idle worker, unfinished preread, and answered card awaiting implementation are agent obligations. Surface them when they threaten an owner commitment, with the agent’s recovery plan.
- “Look” means opening an artifact. Excluding `.md` by extension is arbitrary: a review sheet can be Markdown. Eligibility should depend on purpose and readiness.
- Missing lifecycle: preparing → ready → opened → answered → applied → verified. Opening is not approval; answering is not implementation. Cards must survive window closure and remain discoverable without arbitrary age cutoffs.
- Missing evidence: artifact version, recommendation, alternatives, what the decision blocks, deployment versus verification versus loaded-game state, and whether an answer still applies after revision.
- Undo belongs beside the affected change. Replacing kept art without required permission is an authority breach, not routine delegated activity.
- Zero acknowledgements does not establish alert precision. Automatic recovery, poor visibility, and unusable controls remain competing explanations.
- “Nothing needs you” requires trustworthy coverage. Missing collectors or an unwritten digest mean **unknown**, not reassurance.

**2. The three proposals**

| Proposal | Strongest improvement to make | Worst failure mode | Data it lacks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Morning Paper** | Give every summary claim a linked artifact/version, evidence, and coverage cutoff; keep new ready items live between editions. | A polished, preread brief confidently says “works” while evidence is incomplete. Requiring a judge can also hide everything when the judge stalls. | Reliable owner read position; readiness; evidence supporting claims; deployed/loaded versions; missed-delivery detection independent of the writer. |
| **B: Decision Inbox** | Replace “inbox zero” with persistent review packets. Show recommendation, consequence, artifact, and answer status together. | It rewards clearing cards, encourages shallow phone approvals, and mistakes a submitted answer for completed work. | Stable card IDs and versions; supersession; dependencies; confirmed owner response; application and verification receipts. | **C: Night Watch** | Make the timeline optional detail beneath a plain-language account of meaningful progress, stalls, and next steps. | Busy sessions look productive; quiet verification looks stalled. A phone-width chart conceals the actual review work. | Expected milestones; retry-versus-progress classification; intentional pauses; queue eligibility; measured consumption and credible forecast uncertainty. |

**3. A stronger synthesis: “Return Desk”**

Use one persistent, artifact-first surface with three layers: **For you**, **Since your last read**, and **Run status**. The brief summarizes the same records; the timeline explains them on demand. Start with links into existing review and ruling channels. Inline answers are a separate scope decision.

```text
RimPulse                 Updated 06:12
2 workers active · coverage complete

FOR YOU                         3 ready
Fauna review pictures          ~5 min
21 rows · recommendation included
[Open review]

Water criteria                  ~3 min
Blocks next validation pass
[Open question card]

Kept-art replacement proposal   ~2 min
2 comparisons · approval pending
[Open comparisons]

SINCE YOUR LAST READ
Pit rescue deployed; test passed
[Evidence]  [Other outcomes]

RUN
Foundry: preparing fauna review
Bench: validating water scenes
Next: ladder comparison sheet
[Limits] [History]
```

Unready packets stay off the ready list, but an overdue promised review appears as a delivery exception. Phone access, push, and direct ruling controls remain separate choices under the standing restriction.

**Red rules:** red requires a specific owner action, material consequence, time sensitivity, and evidence that agents cannot resolve it within their authority. Name the action, deadline, and consequence. A blocked card or twelve-hour age alone earns no red. Monitor loss shows “status unknown”; red requires a supported recovery action. Spend warnings require measured usage and a working pause/limit control. Clear red when resolved, preserving history.

**4. Questions only the owner can answer**

1. **How far may scope change?** Defects only—least disruption; Return Desk inside widget—changes organization; companion page—more room, another destination.
2. **Overnight interruption?** Silent—lost work waits; pull-only—check voluntarily; wake for actionable, time-critical incidents—protects the run, interrupts sleep.
3. **Phone authority?** Open only—decide later; answer reversible cards—faster unblocking; answer all eligible cards with confirmation—more power, more accidental-approval risk.
4. **Review order?** Highest consequence—protects priorities; oldest promised—protects commitments; shortest first—fits small gaps.
5. **Minimum review packet?** Artifact plus recommendation—fast; comparisons plus rationale—more context; evidence and uncertainties—slower preparation, stronger judgment.
6. **Delegated-decision audit?** Exceptions only—least reading; daily class summary—spots patterns; every decision available individually—maximum inspection burden.
7. **Budget behavior?** Display only—you intervene; pause at your cap—predictable ceiling; request additional allowance before the cap—maintains flow but creates cards.
