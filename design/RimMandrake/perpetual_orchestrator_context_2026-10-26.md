# Perpetual orchestrator context: grow-until-compaction vs handoff-and-restart

Date: 2026-10-26. Author: BENCH research helper. Status: FINAL for this research round (one desk test owed, section 5/6).
Owner question (verbatim, abridged): "do perpetual agents just permit [context] to expand until compaction? ... It could orchestrate until the next round wouldn't be informed by the previous: time to handoff, die, and restart. Prove me wrong."

## 1. Summary (10 lines)
1. Does everyone grow until compaction? No. The default (Claude Code, chat apps) does; many long-running harnesses restart fresh per unit of work or prune (section 2). Your idea already exists as a mod: claude-auto-handoff (handoff, `/clear`, seed prompt).
2. Anthropic itself is split: its docs say "new task = new session", but its 2026-03-24 harness post dropped resets for Opus 4.5+ and relied on auto-compaction. Whether to reset depends on the model.
3. Cache economics: a warm cached token costs 0.05x. Growth is cheap per call but grows with every call; a restart pays a cold rebuild (wake ~47-77k tokens) plus the handoff write.
4. Sim on 49 real BENCH sessions (section 8): BENCH's actual practice (peak ~347k) costs ~1.6x the best policy. Best policy: restart at ~125k absolute (S1), -37% vs today. Restart-every-round and your probe are within a few percent of that.
5. Your probe ("ask the agent if a handoff is better now") beats a fixed threshold only when the cache goes cold often. Its value is mostly the threshold it implies. The agent's own verdict is not calibrated (GPT + context-anxiety literature), so ask factual questions, not "is it efficient?".
6. Biggest lever: a lean wake. If the wake were ~47.5k and fully cached, restarts save ~35%.
7. Remote Control: a relaunch with the profile's `--remote-control` flag has RC, but shows up as a new phone entry. The owner_cards Q1 claim "cannot /clear itself / loses RC" is wrong: a mod can run `/clear` (`$.command.run`), and RC docs say `/clear` keeps the session and only resets devices. UNMEASURED locally: one desk test is owed.
8. Recommended: D1/D2, an in-process mod: at a safe boundary (no helpers, handoff `--check` passes) it runs handoff, then `/clear`, then submits `wake`. The deterministic gate decides; the agent's answer is advisory. Fallback is D4, steered early `/compact` with instructions.
9. GPT added: a cost formula, "checkpoint before idle, not after", an epoch/cursor transition protocol, and rolling checkpoints. Every source and every sum it gave that could be checked was CHECKED. None was WRONG. UNCHECKED: Magentic-One, two arXiv revision dates, and its failure-mode list (reasoning, not sources).
10. Owed: the desk test of `/clear` + RC on 2.1.308, then a mod prototype behind a flag.

## 4. Economics (prompt caching, context rot)
Prices from docs.claude.com/en/docs/about-claude/pricing and .../prompt-caching, read 2026-10-26. Claude Opus 5.5: input $4/MTok, output $20, 5-min cache write $5 (1.25x), 1-h cache write $8 (2x), cache hit **$0.20 (0.05x, not the usual 0.1x)**. Claude Code runs the main conversation on a 1-h TTL (its `/cost` cache line: "warm (1h TTL ...)"); compaction and tool-result clearing are counted as "expected rebuild" cache misses.

Back-of-envelope (the sibling simulation `src/RimMandrake/Utils/sim/ctx_strategy_sim.py` does this properly, section 8):
- **Warm turns are cheap.** Re-sending 150k cached tokens costs 150k x $0.20/MTok = $0.03 per turn; at 50k it is $0.01. So "grow" costs ~$0.02/turn extra over a lean context. The owner's "grotesquely inefficient" is wrong *while the cache is warm*.
- **A restart is not free.** It re-writes its baseline into cache (BENCH measured ~47.5k tokens for a clean start, owner_cards_2026-10-27.md Q1) = ~$0.38 at the 1-h write price, plus ~3-5k output for the handoff (~$0.06-0.10). Break-even vs a 150k context: ~20-25 warm turns.
- **Cold turns are where size bites.** After >1 h idle the whole prefix is re-written at 2x: 150k = $1.20, 50k = $0.40. An orchestrator that idles between waves (BENCH does) pays its full context on every wake. This favours lean contexts much more than warm-turn arithmetic suggests.
- **Compaction is itself a big call**: it reads the whole window and writes a summary, then a cache rebuild; it is roughly the cost of a restart, but with a worse summary (written by the most-rotted model, at a moment it did not choose).
- **Context rot is the real cost, not dollars.** Chroma "Context Rot" (research.trychroma.com/context-rot, 2025-07-14): 18 models incl. Claude 4, performance "grows increasingly unreliable as input length grows", non-uniformly, even on trivial tasks. Anthropic's engineering post (2025-09-29) adopts the term; Huntley reports quality clipping ~150k of 200k. Anthropic: context editing +29% task performance. These are quality losses no cache discount offsets.
- If a seat runs on a subscription plan (UNMEASURED here which billing BENCH uses) it pays in usage-limit share rather than dollars; the same proportions apply because limits are token-weighted (UNMEASURED how cache reads are weighted against plan limits).

## 6. Candidate designs for RimMandrake
Common substrate (already exists): handoff file + `handoff.py --check` as the "completed handoff" predicate; ledger + git as durable state; `--wake` as the read side.

**D1. Owner's design, in-process ("handoff-and-clear mod").** A mod on BENCH:
1. On `turn.complete`, deterministic signals only: `$.session.usage().context.percent`, tokens since last wake, whether helpers are running, `git status` clean, handoff `--check` passing.
2. At a wave boundary (no helper in flight, tree clean) and context above a floor (say 40% or 2x the wake baseline), the mod asks the question the owner proposed. Cheapest form: not a full BENCH turn, but either (a) a `$.model` side call to a small model with a 1-page digest (open items, last N turns' subjects), or (b) a single prompt to BENCH itself (`$.prompt.submit`), costing one warm turn (~$0.03 at 150k) and answered with a fixed token (`HANDOFF_NOW` / `CONTINUE: <reason>`).
3. On yes, BENCH runs `handoff.py`, fills it, `--check` passes, commits, says HANDOFF READY.
4. The mod sees the committed handoff (file + check exit 0 + HEAD contains it), runs `$.command.run({command:"clear"})`, then `$.prompt.submit({text:"wake"})`.
5. Guards: never mid-helper; max N clears/day; a fresh session must grow K tokens before it may hand off again (claude-auto-handoff's loop guard); veto auto-compact (`session.compact` -> `{skip}`) only when a handoff is in progress, otherwise let it run as the fallback.
Pros: same process, (probably) same phone entry, no PID kills. Cons: needs owner to enable a mod; /clear-RC behaviour unmeasured; BENCH's own "would handoff help?" judgment is made by the most-rotted model (mitigated by deterministic preconditions).

**D2. Deterministic boundary rule, no question.** Same mod, but skip step 2: hand off whenever (wave closed) AND (context > max(2 x wake baseline, 100k)) OR (idle > cache TTL with context > baseline + 50k, since the next turn will rewrite the whole prefix anyway). The question "would the next round be informed by this one?" is answered by structure: if no helper is in flight and the queue item is closed, the answer is almost always no.

**D3. Ralph-style outer loop (kill+relaunch).** Launch line becomes a loop around `claude_bounded.sh ... "wake"`; the agent ends its session by running a script that writes a marker and exits Claude (`/exit` via mod, or a supervisor kills the PID after seeing the marker). Most robust to a wedged process, worst for the phone (new entry each cycle) and needs a PID kill rule change.

**D4. Keep compaction, make it early and steered.** Mod triggers `$.session.compact({instructions: <CLAUDE.md Compact Instructions + handoff path>})` at wave boundaries at ~50% instead of letting auto-compact fire at ~95%. Cheapest change, keeps conversational continuity, but the summary is still model-written and lossy.

**D5. Thin orchestrator by construction (already the house rule).** Bulk work in helpers; BENCH holds only decisions. Lowers the fill rate so D1/D2 fire rarely. Not an alternative, a multiplier.

**D6. Context editing in place.** On the API, `clear_tool_uses` would drop stale tool results; Claude Code itself already does "tool-result clearing" (the cache line counts it as an expected rebuild). Not controllable by us beyond settings; noted for completeness.

Recommendation (kept after GPT critique and the sibling sim. Amended: the D2 gate is `wave closed AND (context > threshold)`, with idle handled as "checkpoint before idle". The transition carries an epoch and a message cursor, per section 7): D1 with D2's deterministic preconditions as the gate and the "ask" reduced to a confirmation, after one desk measurement that `/clear` keeps the phone's RC entry on 2.1.308. Fall back to D4 if the owner will not enable mods.

**Failure modes GPT added (reasoning, not sourced; all plausible, none measured here)**
- D1: the check-then-clear race: a phone message or a helper's completion arriving between "handoff committed" and `/clear` is stranded. Fix: bind the handoff to an epoch + ledger version + message cursor; pause dispatch first.
- D2: "no helpers + clean tree" can starve forever in a perpetual seat; the idle branch as I wrote it bypasses the safety precondition (my wording bug: precedence). A `turn.complete` hook cannot see an idle expiry; needs `$.clock`.

**Decision rule GPT proposed** (arithmetic re-done by this helper: all CHECKED)
- Choose among continue K / handoff-reset H / compact M by expected cost over the *same upcoming work*: `J = (4U + 8W + 0.20R + 20O)/1e6 + sum_j P_j L_j + v T` (U uncached input, W 1-h cache writes, R cache reads, O output; P_j L_j expected failure losses; v T delay).
- Warm example (C=150k, B=50k fresh, 4k-token handoff): handoff premium $0.11 + $0.40 rebuild; continue costs $0.03/request, reset $0.01/request after; tie at **25 requests**. At 5 requests reset costs $0.40 more, i.e. needs a 0.8-point failure-probability cut on a $50 failure to pay.
- Cold example: brief written while warm then a cold 50k start = **$0.51** vs continuing cold at 150k = **$1.20**; writing the brief only after going cold = **$1.68**. Rule: **checkpoint before a likely long idle, not after**.
- Hard feasibility: continuing is ruled out when `C + G99 + H99 > W_usable` (99th-pct growth to the next safe boundary plus transition headroom); keep auto-compact as the emergency floor; require >= $0.05 expected gain before an elective transition (a policy choice, not measured).
