1. **BLOCKER — “One network, one shared store” is incompatible with the retained walker.** Evidence: §1.1 and §3.1 promise pooling across Connected hoses, but `RM_LiquidNetKernel.cs:142` stops at tanks, and `:145` stops at machines. A pump beside tank A cannot reach tank B through a Connected A–B hose. Machines also seed several separate pipe runs (`RM_LiquidNet.cs:173`), while other origins cannot traverse those machines. Reachability therefore does not define network equivalence. **Fix:** define components of conducting pipes/relays, with tanks and machines attached as endpoints. Preserve the endpoint invariant and rewrite the “everything joined is one network” promise.

2. **BLOCKER — Directed transfer cannot reuse the walk unchanged.** Evidence: N1 makes `Cross` reject directed links; N3 nevertheless reuses `Across`, which starts by crossing that link (`RM_LiquidNetKernel.cs:83`). Walking from a pipe endpoint also fails to seed the pipe itself: `Walk` only seeds the origin’s surroundings and links (`:66`), while `OriginPipeCells` requires a `Thing` (`RM_LiquidNet.cs:175`). **Fix:** introduce `ResolveSide(endpoint, excludedTransferLink)`: seed pipe/relay vertices explicitly, resolve machine memberships, and treat a directly coupled tank as a singleton. Transfer across the excluded link separately.

3. **BLOCKER — Hose contents are contradictory and underspecified.** Evidence: §3.7 says a flowing hose holds liquid; §3.8 says Connected hoses hold nothing. Ruling #2 applies to switched-off hoses generally. No rules cover changing liquid/direction while wet, blocked spill locations, a full or destroyed return tank, shortened hoses, or destruction. **Fix:** use saved, typed escrow for every physically wet hose. Debit its actual contents once; serialize incompatible liquid transfers. On shutdown, split contents deterministically between open ends, or return them conservatively. Retain undeliverable contents in saved escrow. A cosmetic puddle requires an explicit owner ruling.

4. **BLOCKER — Several mutation paths assume success rather than conserve on failure.** Evidence: pump draw debits the world before adding to the tank (`Building_LiquidPump.cs:155`, `:176`); pour credits the world before removing storage (`:247`). Converter output proceeds even if input removal fails (`CompLiquidConverter.cs:233`), and failed output insertion has no recovery (`:240`). Ordinary synchronous prechecks make some failures unlikely, but these paths do not enforce the claimed invariant. Even `TankFeedTransfer` can report lost rollback units (`RM_LiquidNetKernel.cs:263`). **Fix:** reserve and validate both legs, commit atomically, and retain failed delivery in escrow. Credit flow and counters only after committed movement.

5. **BLOCKER — The foreign adapter has an unchecked conservation boundary.** Evidence: `CompLiquidPipeAdapter.cs:136` removes FlowWorks liquid before invoking `AddResource`; `:155` adds FlowWorks liquid before invoking `DrawResource`. Neither invocation verifies accepted/drawn quantity. An exception, clamp, or incompatible API can destroy or duplicate liquid. §2 explicitly says foreign method bodies were not inspected. **Fix:** implement a tested integration contract using actual accepted/debited amounts, validated finite conversion ratios, and fractional residual accounting. Fail closed on API mismatch; changing vocabulary alone is insufficient.

6. **MAJOR — Converter conservation needs a defined accounting model.** Evidence: defaults consume two units and produce one (`CompLiquidConverter.cs:28`); no supplied rule accounts for residue, waste, evaporation, or density differences. This does not prove mass loss if liquid units represent different masses, but conservation is currently unprovable. Item placement failure is ignored (`:315`), allowing already-consumed input to lose its output. **Fix:** define mass equivalents and explicit byproducts/loss destinations for recipes. Keep unplaced items in persistent pending output.

7. **BLOCKER — Pump-to-outlet operation is a separate missing feature.** Evidence: §3.3 permits irrigation without tanks, but `Building_LiquidPump.cs:135` immediately rejects a tankless network; §3.4 sources directed hoses only from stores. N5’s reach extension supplies neither downstream capacity nor a delivery transaction. **Fix:** add world→hose escrow→outlet transfers with reserved world capacity and both pump and hose throughput limits. Intake eligibility must use the pump’s complete operational predicate—settings, machine enablement, flick state and power—not merely “a powered pump exists.” One pump gets one aggregate budget across all intakes.

8. **MAJOR — “Pooled storage” still suffers fragmentation deadlocks.** Evidence: `TankToFill` requires one tank to accept the entire request (`RM_LiquidNet.cs:294`); pumps request five units. Two tanks with three and two units of room cannot receive one level. Converter input/output selection likewise uses individual tanks (`CompLiquidConverter.cs:189`, `:207`), so split stock can prevent a batch. **Fix:** aggregate compatible physical tank balances and reserve across tanks. Specify that indivisible world levels can be distributed as integer tank units; otherwise use escrow for remainders.

9. **MAJOR — Scheduling permits permanent starvation and order-dependent propagation.** Evidence: §4 explicitly uses first-come game tick order. A consumer can repeatedly drain each pump refill before another consumer runs; a continuous preferred liquid can monopolize a hose. Sequential directed transfers can forward newly received stock around a cycle during the same update. **Fix:** rotate contested allocations fairly, count shared tanks once, and limit hose exports to stock available at the start of the transfer epoch. Cycles may remain legal, with bounded work and explicit diagnostics.

10. **MAJOR — Ordering and liquid-selection rules still require builder guesses.** Evidence: §3.2 calls ordering origin-independent but retains “first reached” ties. §3.4 says “first compatible pair,” while §3.8 says “draw order first,” without defining ordering between liquids. **Fix:** choose fullest/emptiest by stored units, then stable tank ID; rotate selection among eligible liquids. Add a hose liquid selector, default **Any**. Specify tank-versus-outlet allocation precedence, intake cell ordering, exact world-volume↔tank-unit conversion, and treatment of liquids without channel forms. Use typed spills for those liquids; never silently discard them.

11. **MAJOR — Liveness checking does not validate current topology.** Evidence: `RM_HoseLinks.Live` checks eligibility and endpoint spawning (`RM_LiquidNet.cs:458`), but not changed ports or modes; an `IntVec3` is always considered spawned (`:471`). Empty routes bypass checking (`:259`). Thus same-tick pipe removal or rebinding can leave stale reachability. The index also incorporates existing 60-tick port caches. **Fix:** invalidate topology on pipe, coupling, relay, mode and setting changes. Validate current endpoint identities at commit. Revisit `IsFed` inlet substitution (`:504`) when incoming hoses are directed or Shut.

12. **MAJOR — The stated search bound and complexity are inaccurate.** Evidence: `MaxVisits` only bounds enqueued vertices (`RM_LiquidNetKernel.cs:128`); tanks and crossed links do not consume that budget. Every crossing copies its route (`:137`), making long relay chains potentially quadratic. §4 adds directed-side walks and per-tick cycle detection. **Fix:** bound vertices, endpoints and links; store predecessor routes; cache components and cycle diagnostics by topology revision. Reject transfers using truncated resolution rather than operating on origin-dependent partial networks. Benchmark the expanded fixture.

13. **MAJOR — Save/settings migration needs safe behavioral rules.** Evidence: §3.11 converts active pour pumps into active intake pumps, potentially starting unintended pumping. N9 replaces a setting without specifying migration or disabled-mode behavior. Missing liquid defs explicitly delete tank contents (`Building_LiquidTank.cs:120`); held-liquid saves need equivalent handling. **Fix:** migrate former pour pumps inactive with the stated message; preserve disabled settings and hose modes. Disabling directed transfers must not turn them into Connected joins. Preserve capacity overflow and quarantined unknown-liquid inventory; validate held amounts and stable enum values.

14. **MAJOR — Section 6 substantially understates the delta, and model B is mischaracterized.** Evidence: “walk,” “crediting,” “lease/tint,” and caches cannot all remain unchanged after findings above. N2 does not address aggregation, transactions or fairness. §5 compares B’s blanket draining behavior while §2 describes separate inlet/outlet permissions. **Fix:** add component/endpoint resolution, a common transfer service, scheduling, escrow lifecycle/save hooks, adapter API verification, converter pending outputs, invalidation, filters, diagnostics, and migration work. Update affected callers, definitions, translations, probes and documentation; delete obsolete equalization/pour callers and tests. Tank permissions can protect reserves without enabling automatic drain, but they do not replace hose direction.

**Verdict:** Choose a corrected **A**, with direction on hoses and adapters, canonical conducting components, endpoint tanks, atomic transfers, fair allocation and explicit liquid selection. B is useful for optional reserve protection, but adds controls without eliminating the need to direct portable loading and drainage. Equalization cannot express the requested intent; directed pumps contradict the free drainage/storage-transfer rulings. The supplied code shows no confirmed direct world-intake path outside a pump, but the new intake and tankless-outlet paths need explicit pump budgets. The draft is not implementation-ready, and its “small delta” claim should be withdrawn.
