## rimflow CLI verbs (python3 src/RimMandrake/rimflow/cli.py --help)
next, implemented, reconcile, show, why, namecheck, file, claim (LEASE; -> doing), start (no lease), renew (every 10 min, lapses at 45), release, close, block/unblock, verify, finding, spawn, needs (offline|deploy|game-up|bridge|harvest|owner), retarget, reassign, reclaim, drop, supersede, note, seat, bridge, game, capability (PROJECT_MATURITY_DASHBOARD_1: maturity grid function x content, self-declared), sweep, lint, artifact, queue, render, reindex. Lifecycle: proposed -> ready -> doing -> built (via `implemented`: published sha + all L0 criteria) -> done; blocked is a flag. `next` = owner==seat, ready, not blocked, needs satisfiable; claim under lease hides it. "validated" as a separate state: not seen in the verb list (the level lives on the item as "level reached").

## Takeaways for a "ratchet workflow" design
1. Levels, `built` state, leases, source-check-before-dispatch: already built in rimflow. Do not redesign; extend.
2. Per-mod ratchet already exists as modcheck status (RED/STALE/DRAFT/GREEN, hash-bound); the gap is coverage (1 GREEN of 73) and result-join (108/148 mods unseen by report).
3. Art pre-review machinery (artboard, art_facts, sheets) built but live half of artboard never run; owner policy autofix classes exist.
4. Owner inbox: no single queue; RimPulse `hand` + OWNER-seat items are the nearest.
5. Live-proof harness doc says most live findings are harness faults: a ratchet must reward mod-bug yield.
