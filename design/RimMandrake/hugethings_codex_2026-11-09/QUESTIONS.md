DRY RUN — HugeThings, proposed root and question bank. All wording and guesses below are mine, not Mandrake's words. Nothing is approved or sent.

This mod bundles several built promises: huge plants, huge animals, titan movement and damage, and the aftermath of a titan's death. Each needs its own proof.

PROPOSED ROOT

HugeThings makes huge plants and animals behave like the size you see: mass has consequences. A giant fungus has a solid stem and roots, open space beneath its cap, and a picture you can actually click; growing trunks respect the colony already around them. A huge animal has a body you can select, and a titan meets gaps, walls, roofs and trenches as a creature of its breadth: squeezing where it fits, breaking what it can, and being held by what really stops it. Its passage can wreck the colony simply because something that large walked through. A biome's own giants announce their approach with tremors. The largest dead titan becomes a landmark worked over days, with ordinary meat's rules for rot, while scavengers and visiting people compete for what remains. These are several connected promises, each owed its own demonstration. HugeThings supplies the common machinery; the biomes supply the living things, Large Pawns supplies their larger occupied bodies when loaded, and the plant and animal settings let the player tune their consequences.

My claim: making the visible size true is the common idea that joins these features.
The strongest objection: that paragraph can sound as if every part already works. Some parts are built but unaccepted; normal corpse rot and a real dispute over remains still have gaps. This is a proposed promise, not a report of passing tests.

These are eight drafts to choose from, not an eight-question interrogation. I would begin with 01–02, then serve at most three at a time as your answers make the later ones useful. A short phrase, a different scene, or “this doesn't matter to me” is enough. No reply is being assumed. I recommend the crossing-and-aftermath scene first because you already explained how its two actions belong together.

06. What a prior claim means

Picture to react to (written thought experiment, not a capture or staged game): Your arriving clan finds the older carcass you described, with people already working beside it. A colonist walks up to work at the opposite side without attacking anyone.

Question: What do the people already there regard as theirs, and what could your clan do to earn a share?

Why it matters: F2. Prior presence can mean territorial ownership, a claimed portion, or simply rivals who resent sharing. Each gives the newcomer a different first decision.

My guess, not your answer: A prior group has a recognisable claim the newcomer must respect, buy into or challenge. I would make the claim visible before the first harvest order can offend them.

What changes in the tree: A whole-site claim needs ownership and permission; a portion needs separate accounted shares; open competition needs no title system, only rivalry. A bargain adds only the specific exchange he describes. The old carcass remains a hand-staged exploration/acceptance fixture until separate authority exists for spawning it in play.

Why it matters: F2/F3. 'Worked over days' can hide an idle queue behind one worker; an army harvesting instantly can erase the race. The intended commitment is not specified by the current batch sizes.

My guess, not your answer: A meaningful expedition with several useful jobs, enough recovery to reward choosing it, and plenty left for tomorrow and the competitors. I would test both a modest colony and a strong mobilisation.

The amount recovered and arrival pressure are tuned from his picture, with ordinary-temperature and frozen controls; no new artificial spoilage clock.
