## Round 2: the rebuttal and concessions

**1. REBUTTALS**

**freeze-strangle-core:** Your diagnosis supports a common reader; it does not yet justify a common executor. The sweep found contradictory answers and incomplete caller migration; it explicitly leaves slow ticks as another explanation for interventions. Finish checking those repairs against actual execution before changing launcher and transition ownership.

Your intervention falsifier requires “at least two comparable completions in each week.” The pack establishes neither that workload nor its comparability. Insufficient completions leave your main operational claim inconclusive after the planned restructuring has already happened. Your recovery tests are useful; the scheduled mergers have not earned their place.

**replace-durable-slice:** You correctly admit that “the pack does not identify an individual interrupted-run incident with enough evidence to reproduce it.” That is the decisive missing premise. Nevertheless, replacement receives the fortnight’s primary machinery allocation before its target exists.

Your instruction to “Stop extending bespoke recovery within that boundary” also prejudges the remedy. A reproduced failure might be an obsolete reader, a missing receipt or incomplete caller migration. An outside runtime still needs the launcher, landing and stage-write adapters you retain. Testing an effect completed before acknowledgement is essential precisely because replacing orchestration does not establish that those effects become safe.

I accept your day-2 stopping condition. I reject making replacement primary before that investigation. If a current, consequential recovery defect survives verification of today’s changes, a comparison using real adapters becomes defensible this fortnight. The evidence would then identify the boundary.

**supervised-mod-sprint:** Your distinction—“manual routing may substitute for automation; manual assertion may never substitute for proof”—protects acceptance standards. Your explicit exception permitting hand acts without permanent fixes would suspend the owner’s November 15 instruction to improve the process rather than repeatedly clean up after it. Legitimate stewardship needs no automation; recurring recovery needs a repair.

Your pass falsifier applies only if readiness is established by November 24. If machinery prevents readiness, the condition fails and the pass test cannot judge the very obstruction at issue. Count pipeline-caused readiness blocks as failures of opportunity. Use supervised routing as a visible contingency, rather than committing the fortnight to it before examining the present runtime.
