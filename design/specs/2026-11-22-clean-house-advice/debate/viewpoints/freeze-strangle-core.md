## Round 1: the argument

**1. THE CASE**

Freeze machinery growth, then give the existing tick progressively more control of routine execution. The strongest evidence is that several programs answer the same operational question differently. The single-source sweep, candidate 1, found three conflicting answers for held LeaningScrub; it also records a six-hour stall while one reader said “automatic.” Consolidating authority addresses an observed failure.

There is evidence that incremental replacement can work. Six groups were consolidated that day, and seven consolidation runs cost 12.2 M weighted tokens, with individual runs costing 1.2–2.1 M (`advice.md`, §§2 and 4). That supports small replacements with proofs. It does not establish the cost of merging launchers or coordinating three stores.

Growth must be constrained while those replacements happen. The study records at least 517 M of machinery runs over nine days, 33 standing voids, and 98 hand acts (`advice.md`, §§2 and 4). Of the first 96 hand acts, 71 repeated decisions within the tick’s remit. But the sweep explicitly warns that slow ticks, rather than conflicting decisions, may explain some interventions. Measure that distinction before prescribing every fix.

I therefore sharpen the thesis: **the tick should become the sole executor of routine routing and stage transitions, while explicit judgment remains with judges, the Conductor and the owner.** The Maturation specification preserves the Conductor’s stewardship and decisions after three cycles; Stage 7 writes a brief without veto. Consolidation must preserve those boundaries.

Pyrelands supplies the real workload: it is already advancing in in-game acceptance (`pipe_show_2026-11-22_1615.txt`). Its passage should expose which launcher, result and recovery paths actually matter. Seven quiet days cannot establish that everything else is disposable.

This also respects the laboratory purpose. `PURPOSE.md` makes experimentation an equal intention. A fourteen-day stable production path, alongside one isolated engine trial, is itself an experiment: can less competing control produce dependable creative work?

The proposed destination is justified; its superiority remains unproved. Keep the current tick provisionally, test its recovery against an outside library, and decide again on December 6.

## Round 2: the rebuttal and concessions

**1. REBUTTALS**

**Repair-current-runtime.** Your strongest point is that today’s consolidations deserve verification before another migration. I accept that ordering. But you prescribe: **“Three-day engine Trial: Defer for fourteen days.”** That calendar restriction survives regardless of what reconstruction discovers. The pack identifies cancellation and duplicate completion as Core’s difficult, potentially underpriced work (`advice.md` §4). If reconstruction exposes a reproducible recovery defect, repairing its bespoke implementation before comparing alternatives could purchase work we immediately discard. Neither the 517 M historical spend nor the intervention count proves replacement would win. They justify keeping that choice open. Begin with your reconstruction; let its evidence determine the next experiment.

**Replace-durable-slice.** Your demand for an actual incident and real adapters improves my fake-worker trial. But **“Stop extending bespoke recovery within that boundary”** can obstruct the mod work your experiment promises to preserve. Suppose the reproduced defect blocks Pyrelands, an existing-home repair is known, and replacement has not passed its tests. Your plan expressly permits necessary repairs *outside* the slice, leaving this case unresolved. The owner’s same-run repair instruction covers known pipeline defects, and readiness rules prohibit advancement through a BLOCKS fault (`CLAUDE.md`). A replacement experiment needs an explicit provision for an immediate repair inside its boundary. Also, **“zero real stage transitions”** rejects rollout even when unrelated mod defects prevent a transition; that establishes unearned adoption, not an inferior recovery mechanism. Report those conclusions separately.

**Rebuild-small-core.** You propose **“transfer authority once”** and **“switch all orchestration authority together.”** That makes every orchestration boundary part of one acceptance and rollback problem. Your own contract includes holds, ceilings, selective rechecking, fresh judges, multiple stores and retained execution tools. The pack supplies neither a complete decision corpus nor completed consolidation proofs; remote behavior and historical inputs remain partly uninspected. Your 100-point corpus and 8,000-line ceiling are proposed constraints, not evidence that the boundary fits. Conditional cutover limits exposure but does not remove the cost of building the whole candidate before discovering one incompatible obligation. Incremental replacement can retire one demonstrated competing path while leaving unresolved boundaries alone. I accept your single-writer requirement **within each replaced boundary**, not factory-wide transfer as the initial unit.

- **Supervised-mod-sprint:** **“manual routing may substitute for automation”** can deliver a mod, but the necessary permanent-fix exception permits dependence on the busiest seat; its full cost remains UNMEASURED.
- **Consolidate-facts-only:** **“Preserve … storage boundaries”** is a useful default, but cannot remain binding if reconstruction demonstrates that recovery requires coordinating writes across those boundaries.
- **Controlled-live-research:** **“two matched pairs”** have no demonstrated task inventory; your feasibility stop is sound, but unmatched work and shared repairs may leave execution-mode superiority unanswered.
- **Shrink-the-fleet:** **“one concurrent helper”** is an experimental setting without measured concurrency evidence; it bundles admission reduction, serialization and role retirement, making any improvement difficult to attribute.
