This also serves both purposes in `PURPOSE.md`: build the game while conducting a bounded factory experiment. The experiment is whether consistent facts improve operation without changing the workflow underneath them.

## Round 2: the rebuttal and concessions

**1. REBUTTALS**

**repair-current-runtime:** Your strongest challenge is priority: “complete only demonstrated gaps,” on FlowWorks and Pyrelands, instead of auditing everything. I accept that ordering. But “repair failures that demonstrably obstruct” advancement is too restrictive if it requires another stall before correcting a reachable wrong answer. The sweep found a held mod advertised as automatic and an earlier overlay that covered only one of three callers. A remaining caller that demonstrably misreports an existing hold deserves correction before it launches work or misdirects supervision. Conversely, an unfinished migration is not automatically a blocker. Neither your plan nor mine should complete it merely because a study proposed it. The dividing line should be a demonstrated wrong result in ordinary use, not whether another interruption has already occurred.

**freeze-strangle-core:** “Consolidating authority addresses an observed failure” overstates what the strongest example establishes. LeaningScrub had conflicting *answers*; the pack does not show competing executors advancing it. Consolidating those readers does not establish that launcher replacement and transferring transition authority are the next necessary changes. Your own plan acknowledges that the seven consolidation runs do not price coordination across three stores. Yet it schedules launcher replacement, typed output, an engine trial and transition commitment before measuring their necessity. That changes several boundaries together, weakening attribution. Your intervention falsifier also needs “at least two comparable completions in each week.” Available comparable completions are UNMEASURED. If they do not materialize, the operational claim remains untested while the migrations still consume effort. Diagnose the active failures before committing to that sequence.

**replace-durable-slice:** Your opening requirement is sound: “If no current failure can be reproduced … stop.” That makes your proposal a conditional successor to diagnosis, not the justified default machinery allocation today. The pack identifies no reproducible interrupted-run incident. Neither 33 voids nor overlapping hand decisions establishes a durability defect. More concerning is “Stop extending bespoke recovery within that boundary.” A known repair may unblock current work before an outside runtime can safely use the adapters; withholding that repair makes adoption’s opportunity cost larger. Your explicit effect-before-acknowledgment tests are essential, because durable orchestration alone cannot establish safe retries through systemd, git and the game. Fifty successful selected fault trials would permit a bounded decision about that slice; they would not establish lower maintenance cost, which you correctly leave UNMEASURED.

**supervised-mod-sprint:** “Automate nothing new” avoids speculative construction, but should still permit completing a demonstrated reader correction; supervision otherwise inherits misleading inputs, and its Conductor spend remains UNMEASURED.

**controlled-live-research:** “Aim for two matched pairs” is conditional on work and accounting not yet established; accept your feasibility check, but do not delay correcting known inputs merely to preserve experimental conditions.

**rebuild-small-core:** “Replace the old orchestration in one guarded cut-over” concentrates the handover burden before the pack establishes which orchestration failures reader repair cannot solve; a 100-point corpus cannot establish completeness of missing policy.

**shrink-the-fleet:** “One concurrent helper” tests serialization, but concurrency’s contribution to failures is UNMEASURED; correcting contradictory readers remains necessary even when every worker runs alone.

**2. CONCESSIONS**

- **repair-current-runtime wins on scope and ordering.** I withdraw the commitment to audit every caller of every reported consolidation before choosing further work. Begin with landed changes and callers serving advancing mods. Remote and unrelated paths remain explicitly unverified.
- **supervised-mod-sprint and controlled-live-research correctly separate supervision from malfunction.** I retain that distinction and accept deliberate Conductor routing as an operating choice when existing controls permit it. It must be counted, not automatically converted into automation work.
- **replace-durable-slice supplies a better escalation test.** I withdraw a blanket fortnight-long deferral of protocol changes and runtime investigation. A reproduced active blocker that reader repair cannot solve can justify a narrowly scoped comparison or repair immediately.
- **My third falsifier could delay an obvious conclusion.** Requiring three incidents and a blocked-time majority would fail to respond promptly to one consequential non-reader defect. One reproduced blocker outside my scope is enough to reconsider that scope; it does not by itself justify an engine.
- **My cost claims need narrowing.** I withdraw the 1–3 M Conductor/GPT estimate and the implication that 6–20 M covers the whole cleanup. Conductor and GPT spend are UNMEASURED. I also withdraw 12.2 M as an audit falsifier: implementation runs and caller investigation are not comparable workloads.
- **The corrected date binds every interpretation.** The slowdown act is November 18. It is neither new authorization nor a November 22 instruction to suspend today’s advancing mods. Its continuing scope must come from the actual controls and instruction.
