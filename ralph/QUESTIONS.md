# Questions for the owner

1. **Explosive Growth's dry-crop rule is off for Pyrelands only.** It multiplied any vanilla crop more than 2 cells from water by 0.1, on top of the ash field's own rule (dry 70%, watered 140%). Rice on dry ash grew at about 7%, and rice on the "rich soil" by a pond crawled at 10%. That was the "crops at a tenth" you asked about. The biome now opts out through a new `RM_ExplosiveGrowthBiomeExtension`; no other biome changes. Watered ash still grows crops twice as fast as dry ash.
2. **Crops on watered ash now shrug off the heat ("wet roots"). Built, not yet seen in game.** The Pyrelands' tiles have a median of 53.6 C. A vanilla crop grows fully only up to 42 C, stops at 58 C, and grows only from 06:00 to 19:00, which are the hottest hours. On a 50 C landing (map `q`), the day ran 45 C at 05:00 to 58 C at 17:00. Rice read a 0 to 16% heat multiplier at midday and gained about 4% growth a day, whether it stood by water or not. That is your "crops at a tenth", alongside the Explosive Growth rule fixed earlier. It also made the biome's own text false ("wherever water reaches it, it grows almost anything"), and a canal changed nothing a player could see. Now, a plant on ash-field ground that counts as watered (beside dug water, near a pond or mud, or after black rain) reads its heat multiplier as if its cell were 15 C cooler. Dry ash keeps vanilla's heat. The plant's inspect pane says "Wet ash keeps its roots cool…". Watered rice should grow most of the day; dry rice hardly grows at all. My recommendation is to keep it: water becomes the heart of farming in a fire biome. The alternative is to paint Pyrelands tiles nearer 35 C, which would make the biome mild.
3. **Digging a canal starves the digger (FlowWorks, not Pyrelands). The fix is written, and I left it for the FlowWorks loop.** Seen again on map `r`: Sweetpea dug one canal cell for ten game hours with thirst at 0.0 while the other two drank from the pond beside them. `RM_DigCanalJob` never ends on its own, so thirst and hunger are never re-checked. Ending the job by hand sent them straight to drink. In this biome a canal is the way to farm, so the first player who digs one loses a colonist. A fix written last pass (each cell keeps its dig progress, and the job expires so needs can interrupt it) is saved as `ralph/handoff/flowworks_dig_keeps_progress.patch`, about 650 lines. I haven't committed it here, because the FlowWorks loop is rewriting the same dig code (shovels) on `ralph/flowworks`, and two copies would collide. My recommendation is that the FlowWorks loop lands it, or its own equivalent. Meanwhile my canal tests dig through FlowWorks' own `Deepen()` (the bridge's excavation shortcut) instead of colonist labour.
4. **Once black rain falls, a burn scar greens over in 2 to 3 days, not a week.** You ruled that grass bursts only when wet. On dry ash a scar does re-green slowly: last pass's probe flattened at about 45% of cells grassy by day 3. But black rain waters every cell on the map for a day, and then the whole scar bursts at once. On map w, a fixed 30x16 scar went from bare to a full carpet in 3 days, with one black rain in between (`first_look_3`, `_4`). On day 20 a dry storm burned 39,000 cells, and by day 22.2 the whole map read as grass again, after one black rain (`ov_d22_1020.png`). The north star says "visibly re-greening within a few days and largely green again within about a week". Two to three days is inside "within", so I count the bar as true. The cost is that a player sees a scar only briefly before the green closes over it. **My recommendation:** keep it, because your "bursts only when wet" rule is what makes black rain matter to the land. If you want scars to linger, the lever is to have only part of a wet scar burst (say half the cells), which would stretch a wet scar to about 5 days.
