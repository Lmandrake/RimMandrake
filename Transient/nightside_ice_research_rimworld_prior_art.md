# Nightside Ice biome — RimWorld prior art research

Status: COMPLETE

## 1. Vanilla ice sheet / sea ice

- **Sea ice** is the coldest land biome in core RimWorld (only orbit is colder); **ice sheet** is nearly as bad. Both have **no plant life and almost no animal life** — a snowhare is typically the only guaranteed animal per map, with a rare arctic fox or polar bear wandering in. [Ice sheet](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ice_sheet) · [Sea ice](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Sea_ice) · [Ice Sheet Guide](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ice_Sheet_Guide) · [Sea Ice Guide](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Sea_Ice_Guide)
- **No incidents are biome-special.** The storyteller fires raids/events at the SAME frequency as anywhere else despite the cold — the challenge is entirely about survival math (food/heat), not bespoke ice threats. With a very small colony, `PopulationIntent` biases toward recruitment-generating events (transport pod crashes, wanderers) to grow the pop back up, since cannibalizing/losing colonists to cold is expected.
- **Core survival loop players use:** cannibalism of raiders/failed visitors is the standard early food source (human meat) until a heater + sun lamp + hydroponics/growing zone under a heat lamp is running; steam geysers give free heat (geothermal) and are a prized start-tile feature; mining is mandatory (steel for walls/turrets, since no wood, no fields); starting ruins are scavenged hard. Cold triggers hypothermia risk directly; blizzards (weather event) reduce visibility/temperature further and are one of the few weather "events" that specifically bite in this biome.
- **What makes it "interesting" vs "tedious"** per community guides/discussions: ice sheet is regarded as the accessible extreme (once heat/food stabilize, mining/building proceeds at normal rates since stone/ore density is normal) while **sea ice is regarded as more tedious** — it adds the sea-ice specific problem that terrain is literally ice/ocean (no soil at all, no natural non-ice tiles for foundations in places), pushing players even harder into pure mining/hydroponics/geyser dependency with even less margin for error. [Sea Ice Extreme guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1089822620) · [Sea Ice Community Challenge thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/154644928865577264/?ctp=2)
- Disease mean-time-between on Sea Ice ≈ 90 days (comparable to other biomes; cold itself, not disease, is the pressure).
- Net takeaway: vanilla's ice biomes are a **resource-scarcity/thermal-logistics puzzle**, not a bespoke-monster biome — all "content" is the same raid/manhunter/disease incident pool as everywhere else, just survived under a brutal food/heat handicap. This is a gap our biome's under-ice tunneler + heat-attracts-threats mechanic would fill (vanilla has literally nothing unique to ice as a THREAT, only as a hardship).

## 2. DLC additions relevant to ice/cold

- **Odyssey's Glacial Plains** (new biome, released July 2025) is the closest vanilla analogue to what we're building: blizzards, "prehistoric-feeling" wildlife (mastodons, scimitar cats), **buried frozen ruins** you carve into/dig out of the ice, and **no geysers on glacial plain maps** (forces alternate heat sourcing — relevant precedent for a biome that denies the easy heat answer). Frozen ruins can hide **cryptosleep caskets "pulsing with life"** — i.e. vanilla already has a "something dormant/dangerous is frozen in the ice and you dig it out" trope. [Odyssey (DLC) wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Odyssey_(DLC)) · [Glacial plain wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Glacial_plain) · [Deltia's Odyssey rundown](https://deltiasgaming.com/rimworld-odyssey-everything-new-in-the-dlc/) · [PCGamesN on map gen](https://www.pcgamesn.com/rimworld/odyssey-map-generation)
- **Anomaly** is the DLC with the actual "something erupts from the ground" mechanic (see §4 for full mechanism): pit gates/fleshbeast emergences aren't ice-specific but are the direct mechanical template for "blind thing under the surface, telegraphed, then breaches." Anomaly has no ice-specific content itself (no cold-triggered entities found in this pass) — its relevance here is purely mechanical (the tunneling/emergence system), not thematic.
- **Biotech**: no ice/cold-specific content identified (mechanitors, xenotypes, babies — none biome-gated to cold). Not pursued further; no evidence of cold-linked mechanics.
- **Royalty**: no ice/cold-specific content identified (psycasts, throne rooms, nobles) — again nothing biome-gated to cold found.
- **Ideology**: no ice/cold-specific content identified (ideoligions, rituals) — no cold-specific memes/precepts surfaced in this search pass. (Note: not exhaustively verified — if precision on Ideology/Biotech/Royalty matters later, a targeted follow-up search naming specific mechanics, e.g. "RimWorld Ideology cold precept" or "RimWorld Biotech cold gene", would close the gap. This pass treated them as low-yield based on absence from all general DLC-content search results.)

## 3. Mods adding ice-sheet/polar content

- **[RF] Permafrost (Continued)** — adds a biome slotted between tundra and ice sheet: "permanently frozen ground," ice a few meters thick (vs. ice sheet's kilometers), harsher than tundra but not as barren as ice sheet. A pure biome-placement/flavor mod, no bespoke threat mechanic found. [Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3027775257) · [RimWorld Base writeup](https://rimworldbase.com/rf-permafrost-mod/)
- **Alpha Biomes** — 10 new biomes; its cold entry is **Propane Lakes**, explicitly framed as "a frozen wasteland, colder than the most inhospitable Sea Ice, but teeming with aberrant flora and fauna" — i.e. their design answer to "even colder than vanilla ice" was to make it *alien-life-rich* rather than barren, the opposite direction from ours (we want it colder AND more barren above ground, with life pushed under the ice). Useful contrast/precedent to cite. [RimWorld Base](https://rimworldbase.com/alpha-biomes-mod/)
- **Vanilla Animals Expanded — Ice Sheet** (now merged into consolidated "Vanilla Animals Expanded") — adds mundane polar wildlife: penguin flocks (can manhunter-swarm) and walruses (hunted for tusks). Straightforward creature-roster padding, no new mechanic. **VAE — Tundra** adds musk oxen, moose (milkable), porcupines, megawolverines — again roster padding, not mechanics. [Steam Workshop (Ice Sheet)](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1895364782) · [GitHub Tundra](https://github.com/juanosarg/VanillaAnimalsExpanded-Tundra)
- **Alpha Animals** (100+ creatures, companion to Alpha Biomes) — its stated design philosophy is that *every* new creature should bring a new mechanic (walking tanks, living resource farms, "night time stalkers," giant speedy spiders) rather than being reskins. No ice-specific burrower was confirmed in this pass, but the mod's philosophy — one new mechanic per creature — is itself a good design-process precedent for our biome's cast. [Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1541721856) · [Bestiary wiki](https://rimworldbestiary.wiki.gg/wiki/Alpha_Animals)
- **Ice fishing mods** — several small mods (**IceWorld: Ice Fishing**, **[RF] Fishing-ReWork** + **WaterFreezes**) implement literal under-ice access: pawns must **mine/cut a fishing hole through ice** to reach the fishing zone beneath, the hole doesn't refreeze while actively worked, and pawns abandon the job on severe hypothermia. This is a clean, already-shipped precedent for "there is a resource/danger under the ice sheet that you must breach the ice to reach," which maps directly onto a colony needing to break through to find/fight what's below. [IceWorld: Ice Fishing](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3531887595)
- **Old "IceSheet" biome mod (Alpha-era, ~A8/1.2)** — a very old community mod that made ice-sheet-style biomes with a "water world" variant; mostly historical, no modern mechanic content recoverable. [Ludeon forum thread](https://ludeon.com/forums/index.php?topic=7882.45)
- **"Biomes! Polar Caps" and "Rimfrost"** (named in the brief) — **could not confirm either mod exists under those exact names** in this search pass; no Steam Workshop or wiki hits. Treat as either renamed/retired or misremembered — do not cite them as prior art without a direct workshop-link verification.
- **Sandworm-style tunneling creature mods**: **LEVIATHANS: SANDWORM** — a Dune-inspired mod where a "leviathan-class sandworm lying dormant beneath the Deep Sand Echo Zone" is drawn to the surface by a craftable "Sandhammer" device that "replicates deep seismic vibrations produced during a sandworm's mating cycle" — i.e. the player actively **weaponizes/bait-summons** the buried worm using a fake mating signal, rather than the worm proactively hunting. This is a strong, on-genre precedent for "player action generates the summoning signal" (directly analogous to our heat-as-bait mechanic). One community comment noted **the vanilla engine itself has no scripted "burrowing creature that moves underground and periodically surfaces" system** — anything of that shape in mods has had to be hand-built (relevant: confirms there is no cheap vanilla hook for a moving underground threat; our mod would need to build the movement logic from scratch, likely via the same techniques Anomaly uses for stationary emergence points, see §4). [Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3713982815)
- **Tunnel and underground** mod — adds player-usable tunnel/underground traversal; appears to be about player mobility underground rather than a threat mechanic. Not deeply explored this pass. [Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3162495194)

## 4. Underground tunneling threats with surface tells

This is the richest and most directly reusable section. Three distinct vanilla/DLC mechanisms already exist, each a different "blind threat under the ground, telegraphed, then breaches" shape:

**A. Vanilla insectoid infestation (`TunnelHiveSpawner` family) — passive, location-gated, minimal telegraph**
- Natural infestations require: deep underground, far from sunlight, overhead-mountain roof, **temperature warmer than −17 °C (strong preference below −8 °C)**, darkness, within 30 tiles of a colony structure, max once per 20 days. [Infestation wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Infestation)
- Key inversion for our purposes: vanilla hives are **cold-tolerant but not cold-seeking** — they spawn preferentially in a *moderate* cold band, and can take cold damage if it gets too chilly (hives are hurt by extreme cold, not drawn to it). Light ≥60% reduces spawn chance, 100% light eliminates it entirely — i.e. **light/heat suppresses vanilla insectoid spawning**, which is the *exact opposite* of what we want (our tunnelers should be drawn BY heat, not repelled by light). This is a useful "note the inversion" reference: we are deliberately building the mirror-image of vanilla infestation logic.
- **Deep drill → "Too Deep: Infestation"**: manned drilling has a chance to hit a hive and bring insectoids to the surface around the drill; there is a **short warning message + delay before insectoids appear**, enough time to pull the miner out (exact delay/probability is undocumented/stub-status on the wiki — treat as "a short delay," not a specific tick count). [Deep drill wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Deep_drill)
- This is the closest vanilla analogue to "your own activity (mining/heating) can trigger the threat" — directly reusable as the core loop for our biome (your furnace/heater is the "deep drill").

**B. Anomaly pit gate / fleshbeast emergence — timed, telegraphed, escalating**
- Sequence: an "underground emergence" appears first (a marked map feature) → **5,000–20,000 ticks (2–8 in-game hours) later** it opens into a full pit gate → fleshbeasts erupt and attack. [Pit gate wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Pit_gate)
- After the gate is open, **subsequent fleshbeast waves are pre-warned "a few hours in advance," but deliberately *without* a visible countdown timer** — the game tells you an attack is coming without letting you clock-watch it, which keeps tension without letting the player fully optimize around it. This asymmetry (first emergence = precise timer, later waves = vague warning) is a good template for a two-tier alert design.
- The **Dreadmeld** — the largest fleshbeast — is explicitly a *tunneler*: "uses its massive claws to dig tunnels through solid rock," and dreadmelds "are rarely seen above ground, instead sheltering in caverns built from living flesh," surfacing as effectively a boss encounter. This is the closest vanilla-DLC precedent to "a big blind thing that tunnels and is mostly unseen until it commits to a surface attack." [Dreadmeld wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Dreadmeld) · [Ludeon Anomaly preview #1](https://ludeon.com/blog/2024/03/anomaly-preview-1-fleshbeasts-fleshmass-heart-pit-gate-and-dreadmeld/)
- Fleshbeasts themselves spawn from "pit burrows" that appear "in random pathable outdoor areas not under furniture" — a placement rule (must be open ground, not indoors) that's directly reusable: our under-ice tunnelers should likewise be constrained to spawn only under open ice, not under a built floor, which both makes mechanical sense (they tunnel through ice/permafrost, not through your walls) and gives players a defensible rule ("floor it and you're safe from surface breach here").

**C. Modded sapper/tunneling raids (VFE — Insectoids) — active, offense-oriented tunneling**
- VFE Insectoids ships three insectoid raid variants that all use underground movement as the offense, not a passive hazard: **sapper raid** (insectoids dig through walls/mountain rock to reach your base interior), **tunneling raid** (drop-pod-like: insectoids can appear anywhere *inside* your base, bypassing walls entirely), and **siege-with-burrows** (insectoids first dig burrows outside your walls, then tunnel inward from those burrows until the burrows are destroyed — i.e. the burrow is a **destructible forward staging point** the player can proactively hit before the tunneling completes). [Fandom wiki](https://rimworld-ve.fandom.com/wiki/Vanilla_Factions_Expanded_-_Insectoids)
- The siege-burrow design is the single most reusable mechanic here for a "blind under-ice tunneler colony": it gives the player **a visible, attackable proxy object** (the burrow) for an otherwise-invisible threat, with a race-against-completion-timer dynamic, which is more actionable than a pure "surprise, it's under you now" reveal.
- No confirmed mechanism found (in this pass) for a **continuously-moving** underground unit that telegraphs its current position as it travels (e.g., a visible "shockwave"/dust trail preceding it) — the search into "sandworm" mods (§3) confirms this is a known engine gap: nothing in vanilla or the popular mods surveyed tracks and previews a moving underground pawn's real-time position under the map. If our biome wants an approaching-tunneler tell (rumble that gets louder, cracking ice, dust puffs along its path), that appears to be **novel mechanical territory**, not something to copy wholesale — closest available building blocks are (a) the deep-drill delayed-reveal-at-a-point, and (b) the pit-gate's two-tier warned/timered vs. warned/untimered design, combined rather than a true live-tracked mover.

## 5. Heat/light/noise attracting threats

- **Vanilla insectoid infestation is the closest real precedent, and it is a widely-held (if slightly mis-stated) piece of community folk knowledge**: players broadly believe "heating a mountain base attracts bug infestations," and there's a real mechanical kernel to it — natural infestations can only spawn in locations **warmer than −17 °C**, so a heated interior converts otherwise-ineligible deep-cold rock into valid spawn territory, and community guides recommend keeping mined-out mountain sections **below roughly −17 to −20 °C** specifically to deny bugs a foothold. [Infestation wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Infestation) · [Steam discussion thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/2573194016117945388/)
- **The precise mechanic is location-gating, not active seeking**: the wiki is explicit that once a location qualifies, "neither heat nor floors have any bearing on hive spawning" beyond that threshold — i.e. vanilla does NOT have a live "heat level → threat probability" dial, only a binary eligibility cutoff. This is an important distinction for our design: if we want heat to be a **continuously scaling** threat-generator (more heat = more/faster/bigger tunnelers), that is *not* what vanilla does anywhere — it would be a genuine mechanical extension beyond the precedent, not a copy of it.
- **Light suppresses insectoid spawn chance** (60% light reduces it, 100% eliminates it) — the opposite polarity from heat. So vanilla already has two knobs that push in *opposite* directions (heat permits, light forbids) on the same spawn roll — a useful precedent for a biome where heat and light could be split into separate "invites tunnelers" vs. "wards them off" levers rather than one dial.
- **Heat can also be offensively used against spawned insectoids**: sufficiently high temperature causes heatstroke damage to insectoids already present (though they may dig out before dying) — i.e. vanilla already treats heat as a double-edged sword against this enemy type (it can summon them and can hurt them), which is thematically close to a "heat is both your lure and possibly your weapon" design.
- **No vanilla or modded precedent found for noise/sound as a threat-attraction mechanic.** Search passes for "noise attracts manhunters/raids/predators" turned up nothing — only unrelated mods (More Manhunters increases frequency/variety generally; Predator Hunt Alert just warns about existing predators; Fluffy's Wildlife is an info panel). If our biome wants sound-based detection (e.g., generator noise draws tunnelers), that appears to be **fully novel territory**, not an extension of an existing mechanic — build it as new rather than hunting for more prior art.
- **No mod found that scales raid/wildlife danger directly off a player's heat output** (e.g., "your furnace output is your threat level" as a continuous meter). The closest continuous-scaling precedent in the whole search is **`PopulationIntent`**-style storyteller adaptation (population count → recruitment-event bias, see §1) — a similar "your own base state feeds back into what the storyteller throws at you" shape, just gated on population rather than heat. Worth citing as the general *pattern* (state-driven incident weighting already exists in the storyteller) even though heat specifically isn't wired to it anywhere found.

## Most stealable mechanics

1. **VFE Insectoids' siege-burrow (destructible forward staging point)** — a burrow appears outside your walls and tunnels inward over time; destroying the burrow stops the attack before it lands. Maps directly: an under-ice tunneler colony announces itself as a **surfacing crack/mound in the ice** near your heat source, gives the player a fixed, attackable object with a completion countdown, instead of a pure ambush — turns "invisible threat" into "visible race," which is what keeps a blind-under-ice enemy fair rather than cheap.
2. **Anomaly's two-tier warning (precise timer on first emergence, vague warning on repeat waves)** — first tunneler breach near a new heat source gets an exact tick countdown (teaches the player the mechanic); once the colony is established and heat sources multiply, later breaches get only "soon, direction unknown" — maps to a −60 °C biome by keeping early game teachable and late game tense, and lets total colony heat output be the escalator between the two tiers (more heat = more frequent vague-warned breaches instead of one clear one).
3. **Anomaly Dreadmeld — rare big tunneling boss, mostly unseen until it commits** — reserve one large, rock/ice-tunneling apex tunneler as an infrequent set-piece (analogous to the Dreadmeld sheltering in flesh-caverns, "rarely seen above ground") that only surfaces when heat output crosses a high threshold sustained over time — gives the heat-as-threat-generator mechanic a top-end payoff, not just constant nuisance-level breaches.
4. **Vanilla's binary temperature eligibility gate + light/heat opposite-polarity knobs** — reuse the *shape* (a hard threshold gates whether tunnelers can path to a tile at all) but invert the sign: instead of "too cold = no spawn," make **"too cold above the ice = no tunneler interest" and "your heat crossing a threshold = a tile becomes a valid surfacing point,"** with light as a separate, opposite lever (e.g. bright light suppresses surfacing the way it suppresses vanilla infestation) so players get two independent dials (heat invites, light wards) rather than one knob — gives the biome depth without new UI.
5. **IceWorld: Ice Fishing / [RF]-Fishing's "cut through ice to reach what's beneath" job loop** — the existing mechanic of pawns needing to mine/cut a hole through ice (job aborts on hypothermia, hole doesn't refreeze while worked) is a ready-made template for **players breaching down INTO tunneler territory** (offense) as the mirror of tunnelers breaching UP into the colony (their offense) — reuse the job-interruption-on-cold logic and the "hole stays open only while attended" rule for a symmetric two-way front line under the ice.

**Not found / build fresh:** a continuously-moving underground unit whose real-time position is telegraphed to the surface (rumble that tracks a moving threat, cracking-ice trail) has no direct precedent in vanilla or the mods surveyed — the closest analogues are all *point* emergences (deep drill, pit gate, insectoid burrow), never a *tracked path*. Likewise, sound/noise-based threat attraction and any continuously-scaling (not binary) heat→danger dial are both unbuilt anywhere found — these are the two pieces of the design that would be genuinely original rather than adapted.
