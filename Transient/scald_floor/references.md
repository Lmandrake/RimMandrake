# The Scald — sea floor wonder/mechanic references

Research for making the Scald's dive-able sea floor "truly memorable." Two passes:
real-world hydrothermal/deep-sea phenomena, then games/media that nailed an
underwater "wonder" moment. Each entry: what makes it awe-inspiring / the
remembered moment, then one RimWorld-feasible game hook.

## 1. Real world

1. **Black/white smoker chimneys** — mineral-laden fluid up to 382°C (721°F)
   billowing black or white "smoke" from towering chimney structures on the
   seabed; the ocean's own volcanic geysers. Hook: a `HeatVentDef` terrain
   feature that periodically belches a screen-obscuring superheated cloud —
   walk through it unprotected and you cook instantly, but it's also the map's
   light source and heat map centerpiece.
   [Factsheet: Hydrothermal Vents (NOAA)](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/hydrothermal-vents-fact-sheet.pdf)

2. **Tube worms (giant vent tube worms)** — gutless, 2m+ long, red-plumed
   worms that live entirely off symbiotic bacteria, clustered in dense groves
   around vents. Hook: a harvestable/foragable "forest" of tall stationary
   organisms ringing every heat vent — visually reads as a garden growing out
   of a boiling crack.
   [NHM: Survival at hydrothermal vents](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/survival-at-hydrothermal-vents.html)

3. **Yeti crab** — deep-sea crab whose claws and legs are covered in dense
   pale "fur" (setae) that farms bacteria for food; found nowhere but vents.
   Hook: a small ambient critter roster unique to the Scald floor — not
   dangerous, just strange and undeniably alien, worth a codex entry.

4. **Scaly-foot snail** — the only animal known to build its shell (and even
   scale-armor on its foot) out of iron sulfide; genuinely armored in metal.
   Hook: a rare drop/resource — "iron-shelled snail" yields a scrap-metal-like
   material, a flavorful reason to dive repeatedly.

5. **Pompeii worm** — tolerates a temperature gradient from ~80°C at its tail
   (buried in a tube against the chimney) to near-freezing at its head; one of
   the most heat-tolerant animals alive. Hook: justifies gear tiers — a worm
   that "solves" heat by literally living in a temperature gradient is a good
   in-fiction explanation for gradient-based danger zones on the floor map.

6. **Brine pools / underwater lakes** — pools of hypersaline water 3-8x
   saltier than seawater, dense enough to pool in seafloor depressions with a
   visible rippling surface (a "lake" underwater, with its own shoreline).
   Completely anoxic and lethal to most life. Hook: a lethal-instantly terrain
   patch on the sea floor — walk in and you're dead/dissolving, but it's
   gorgeous and mirror-still, the single best screenshot location on the map.
   [Brine pool (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brine_pool)

7. **Halocline shimmer** — the visible optical boundary where fresh/salty or
   hot/cold water meet, distorting light like a heat-haze underwater. Hook: a
   pure visual effect layer over certain tiles — a shimmering "wall" you swim
   through that also functions as a readable danger-zone boundary (temperature
   changes the moment you cross it).
   [Halocline (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halocline)

8. **Whale falls** — a dead whale on the seafloor becomes its own ecosystem for
   decades, cycling through scavenger, enrichment, bone/sulfide-eating, and
   reef stages, supporting 400+ species found nowhere else. Hook: a rare
   one-time map feature — a colossal carcass (of a native sea-beast) that
   spawns a unique, temporary micro-ecosystem of scavenger critters and a
   one-shot resource bonanza, never repeated the same way twice.
   [What is a whale fall? (NOAA)](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/whale-fall.html)

9. **Bioluminescence** — chemical light produced by deep-sea organisms:
   flashing, pulsing, trailing light with no external source. Hook: the
   primary light source for the whole floor map instead of lanterns — plants
   and creatures glow, letting art carry the "alien wonder" read entirely
   through color instead of geometry.

10. **Marine snow** — a constant slow drift of organic detritus falling from
    above, like snow falling upward-lit in torchlight; ambient, atmospheric,
    always moving. Hook: a cheap particle-effect layer over the whole map —
    costs nothing mechanically, but is the single cheapest "this doesn't look
    like the rest of the game" signal.

11. **Pillow lava / submarine eruptions** — lava that extrudes underwater cools
    instantly into rounded "pillow" blobs, sometimes piling into fields of
    bulbous rock; active submarine volcanoes occasionally erupt in real time.
    Hook: a distinctive rock/terrain texture set (lumpy pillow-basalt instead
    of flat stone) plus a rare scripted "eruption" event that reshapes/damages
    part of the floor map.
    [Pillow lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillow_lava) (via search)

12. **Methane hydrate / "burning ice"** — a crystalline ice-like solid that
    catches fire when lit, because it's actually frozen methane; looks like
    ordinary ice but is flammable. Hook: a resource deposit that reads as
    "ice" visually but is a fuel source / explosive hazard — a nice
    contradiction (cold-looking = dangerous-hot) matching the Scald's whole
    premise of "looks safe, isn't."
    [Gas Hydrates: Burning Ice (GeoExpro)](https://geoexpro.com/gas-hydrates-part-i-burning-ice/)

13. **Cold seeps** — like vents but driven by escaping methane/hydrogen
    sulfide rather than heat, hosting mussel beds and tube worms in cold water;
    visually similar community to vents but a colder, calmer counterpoint.
    Hook: a "cold zone" biome pocket on the floor map, contrasted against the
    boiling vents — variety within one dive site.

14. **Manganese/polymetallic nodules** — potato-to-grapefruit-sized metal-rich
    lumps strewn across the abyssal plain, taking millions of years to form
    around a shell fragment or shark tooth. Hook: a literal ground-scatter
    mineable resource — walk-and-harvest nodes, visually distinct (dark round
    lumps dotting pale sediment) from every land-based mining node in the
    game.
    [Deep-sea mining background](https://eos.org/features/the-2-year-countdown-to-deep-sea-mining)

15. **Seafloor massive sulfide mounds** — chimney/mound deposits rich in
    copper, gold, silver, zinc, precipitated directly out of vent fluid. Hook:
    a "jackpot" ore vein visually tied to a vent chimney — high-value mining
    with the same one danger (proximity to the boiling vent) as the reward.

16. **Shipwrecks as reefs** — sunken wrecks become colonized artificial reefs,
    the eerie sight of a man-made structure swallowed by alien growth. Hook: a
    guaranteed wreck POI on the floor map — loot, lore fragments, and a free
    "somebody was here before you and died" story beat.

## 2. Games and media

17. **Subnautica — the Cove Tree (Lost River)** — a single, massive
    bioluminescent tree at the heart of a bone-white ghost forest; the
    landmark every player remembers and navigates by. Why it lands: scale +
    color contrast against an otherwise dark, dread-soaked biome. Hook: one
    hand-placed, enormous glowing landmark structure at the Scald floor's
    center that's visible/orienting from most of the map.
    [Lost River (Subnautica Wiki)](https://wiki.subnautica.com/sn/Lost_River)

18. **Subnautica — Lava Zone heat/pressure escalation** — the deepest biome
    pairs visual spectacle (magma, lava geysers) with the hardest survival
    gate (thermal/pressure suits mandatory). Why it lands: the reward for
    surviving gear-gating is literally hotter, brighter, and more dangerous
    scenery. Hook: gear-gate the Scald floor's deepest/hottest ring behind the
    best heat suit, and make that ring visually the most spectacular.

19. **Subnautica — Ghost Leviathans / ambient dread** — huge, rarely-seen
    creatures whose distant roar defines a zone's mood more than combat does.
    Hook: one giant, mostly-passive sea-beast that's heard/glimpsed but rarely
    fought — presence, not combat, carries the dread.

20. **Abzû — wordless wonder through schools of fish and color** — the whole
    game's "moment" is swimming through enormous, painterly schools of fish
    that part around you; no HUD, no threat, pure spectacle. Why it lands:
    scale of movement (hundreds of small agents reacting to you) reads as
    awe without any mechanical complexity. Hook: a large flocking fish-school
    visual effect the diver can swim through — cheap to fake, huge to look at.
    [Abzû (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abz%C3%BB)

21. **Dave the Diver — dive-then-surface loop** — alternating a tense
    resource-gathering dive with a cozy above-water management layer. Why it
    lands: contrast makes both halves feel better; the dive stays special
    because it's not the whole game. Hook: keep Scald dives short, resourced,
    and re-enterable rather than a persistent second colony — the surface
    (real colony) stays the anchor.
    [Dave the Diver design origin](https://tech.yahoo.com/general/articles/dave-diver-inspired-real-oceanside-195805264.html)

22. **Minecraft — Ocean Monument** — a rare, huge, glowing prismarine
    structure guarded by elder guardians that inflict a debuff just for being
    near them (Mining Fatigue). Why it lands: the structure announces "this
    is the good/dangerous place" from a distance via unmistakable material
    and color, and punishes lingering without instantly killing. Hook: a
    single guaranteed landmark structure with its own material palette
    (distinct from surface stone) and a lingering-penalty aura rather than a
    binary kill zone.
    [Ocean Monument (Minecraft Wiki)](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Ocean_Monument)

23. **Barotrauma — hull breach + monster-through-the-wall tension** — danger
    arrives as sound and pressure before it arrives as damage; leaks and
    creature strikes are heard before seen. Hook: give the Scald a pre-danger
    audio/visual tell (rumble, darkening water, a vent's roar rising) before
    a heat spike or hazard actually lands — telegraph, don't ambush.

24. **Iron Lung — near-total blindness, navigation by photograph** — the
    entire horror comes from NOT being able to see the ocean you're in,
    forcing navigation by tiny fragments of information. Why it lands: it
    proves visibility-as-resource is its own genre of tension. Hook: fog/murk
    radius tied to gear tier — cheap gear sees almost nothing, best gear
    reveals the floor's wonders in full; visibility itself becomes the
    progression reward.

25. **SOMA — mundane dread of walking the ocean floor between story beats**
    — long, silent seabed-walking segments (no combat, just pressure-suit
    footsteps and darkness) that are remembered as the game's most
    unsettling sections precisely because nothing happens. Hook: don't fill
    every tile with a mechanic — leave stretches of quiet dark seabed between
    wonders so the wonders land harder by contrast.

26. **Outer Wilds — Giant's Deep** — an ocean planet with a violent surface
    storm layer hiding a calm, glowing "Anglerfish"-patrolled interior and a
    black hole core; famous for teaching players that going DOWN through
    apparent chaos leads somewhere beautiful and coherent. Hook: structure the
    Scald's dive as a vertical narrative — turbulent/dangerous near the
    surface, opening into a calmer, more spectacular "reveal" layer near the
    true floor.

27. **Sea of Thieves — bioluminescent night sailing & the Shipwreck Bay** —
    ocean glows faintly at night, wrecks are lit landmarks; ambiance sells
    the sea as a character. Hook: cheap ambient particle/glow pass on open
    water tiles even outside named POIs, so the whole map feels alive, not
    just the set-piece rooms.

28. **Raft — scavenging the open ocean as core loop** — turns "empty water"
    into a resource stream by making debris/driftwood/loot float past
    constantly. Hook: passive floating loot/resource spawns drifting across
    the Scald floor between dives, so return trips aren't identical.

29. **Stardew Valley / Terraria ocean & underground biomes — rarity through
    depth gating** — deeper/rarer zones are content-gated by a simple tool
    tier, not a story gate, so "go deeper" is always legible progression.
    Hook: tie Scald depth rings directly to a legible gear tier (like RimWorld
    armor quality tiers), so players always know why they can't go further
    yet.

30. **Hollow Knight — Deepnest atmosphere via near-blindness + sound design**
    — a zone remembered as the game's most oppressive purely through darkness
    and audio cues rather than any unique mechanic. Hook: reuse the "reduce
    visibility, raise audio tension" trick for the Scald's outer ring before
    gear allows full visibility.

31. **Avatar (Pandora) bioluminescent forest-at-night aesthetic** — an entire
    ecosystem that visibly "switches on" and glows at a trigger (footstep,
    touch, darkness); the wonder is interactivity, not just color. Hook: make
    Scald bioluminescent flora/fauna react to the diver's presence (light up
    on approach/touch) rather than glowing statically — turns ambiance into
    feedback.

32. **The Abyss (1989 film) — the alien light-being sequence** — awe built
    from contact with something genuinely non-human and non-hostile at
    extreme depth, using light and slow movement instead of a threat. Hook:
    reserve at least one Scald floor encounter that is beautiful and NOT
    dangerous at all — a pure spectacle beat with zero combat/survival stakes,
    to contrast the rest of the dive's lethality.

33. **Endless Ocean — free-swim "just look at things" mode with a photo/
    cataloguing loop** — the entire game's loop is discovery and cataloguing,
    not combat. Hook: a lightweight "Scald bestiary/codex" that fills in per
    unique creature/feature seen, giving a non-combat progression reason to
    keep diving.

## Top 10 for a boiling sea floor

1. **Black/white smoker chimneys.** Wonder: towering mineral geysers gushing
   382°C fluid, the ocean's own volcano. Mechanic: a `HeatVentDef` terrain
   piece that periodically vents a screen-fogging, instant-lethal heat cloud —
   also the map's main light and heat source.
2. **Halocline/thermocline shimmer walls.** Wonder: a visible, rippling optical
   boundary you swim through. Mechanic: a pure-visual shader/particle band
   that doubles as a readable temperature-zone boundary.
3. **Brine pool (underwater lake).** Wonder: a mirror-still, glassy "lake"
   sitting on the seafloor, lethal to touch. Mechanic: an instant-death
   terrain patch, gorgeous and obviously dangerous — a screenshot tile that
   teaches caution by sight alone.
4. **Bioluminescence as the primary light source.** Wonder: light from life
   itself, no lanterns. Mechanic: replace map lighting with glowing flora/
   fauna, ideally reactive to the diver's presence (Avatar-style touch-glow).
5. **Tube worm groves around vents.** Wonder: alien "gardens" growing out of
   boiling cracks. Mechanic: harvestable stationary plant-analogues clustered
   at every heat vent, giving a reason to approach danger.
6. **Iron-shelled scaly-foot snail.** Wonder: an animal armored in metal it
   grew itself. Mechanic: rare unique-material drop, a dive-worthy prize with
   its own flavor text.
7. **One hand-placed glowing landmark (Cove Tree / Ocean Monument hybrid).**
   Wonder: an unmistakable, massive structure that orients the whole map and
   signals "this is the place." Mechanic: single guaranteed POI with unique
   material palette and a lingering-penalty aura rather than instant death.
8. **Depth-gated visibility (Iron Lung / Lava Zone escalation).** Wonder:
   better gear literally reveals more of the map's beauty. Mechanic: fog/
   murk radius scaled to heat-suit tier — visibility itself is the reward for
   progression.
9. **Whale-fall-style one-time carcass ecosystem.** Wonder: a colossal dead
   sea-beast that's its own temporary micro-biome. Mechanic: rare map feature
   spawning a limited-time scavenger swarm + resource bonanza, never
   identical twice.
10. **One safe, non-hostile spectacle beat (The Abyss's light-being).**
    Wonder: proof the deep isn't only threat. Mechanic: reserve one guaranteed
    encounter per dive site that is beautiful, harmless, and purely there to
    be looked at — the emotional payoff tile after a dive full of danger.
