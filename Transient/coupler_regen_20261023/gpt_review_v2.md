## Scrapper

**Critique.** The collar reads as pristine yellow brass with mechanically identical grooves. Brass belongs here, but the fixture combines burnt-orange corrosion, dark umber recesses, dull copper, small brass fasteners and uneven repairs. The collar lacks that material hierarchy. Its straight dark rails and smooth gradient look extruded rather than assembled.

The pipe is a clean brown gradient: closer to polished bronze than the fixture’s battered copper. Its broad pale highlight and nearly black underside overwhelm its material colour. Both sprites need a few deliberate wear shapes and restrained edge accents, rather than additional fine noise. Echo the rope-bound hardware through one repair binding on the collar; the pipe should remain metal.

## Futuristic

**Critique.** The collar’s silver body and single cyan rail establish the category, but stop at “metal fitting with neon stripe.” The fixture has a stronger construction language: broad ceramic-white surfaces, recessed dark graphite clamps, thin cyan inlays and selectively exposed metal.

The pipe is especially washed out. Its broad white highlight leaves little distinction between ceramic surface, metal shoulder and illuminated seam. The current smooth gradients lack the fixture’s restrained panel boundaries and firm bevels. Keep the ceramic dominant, use dark material to define the collar’s structure, and reserve cyan for one clear signal. Both sprites need fewer, better-separated value groups.

## General recommendations

**Generate the pipe as a long material study, then collapse it to a cross-section for all four looks.** The pipe prompts above use **1536×512**, a legal 3:1 source with 786,432 pixels. Average a clean central span along x in premultiplied-alpha space, resize the resulting vertical profile to 56 pixels, then repeat that profile across all 32 columns. Exclude any generated end treatment before averaging. This guarantees uniformity that prompting alone cannot guarantee.

The collar prompts use **576×1280**, exactly 9:20, with 737,280 pixels. Both source dimensions are multiples of 16. Downscale these directly to 36×80 using premultiplied alpha.

| Look | Preserve in the pipe’s averaged profile |
|---|---|
| Scrapper | Copper midtone, brown shoulders, restrained warm reflection |
| Industrial | Graphite body, muted steel crown, dark contour |
| Modern | Satin cool-grey body, gentle highlight, clean edge separation |
| Futuristic | White body, graphite boundaries, continuous cyan inlays |

**Along-axis detail cannot retain a reliable size or spacing.** A bolt might remain visible at one extension, but becomes an elongated mark at another. Averaging removes it altogether. A line running continuously parallel to x survives because it varies across **y**, not along x. Put bands, bolts, rope turns and hazard markings on the fixed-size collar.

**Lighting should describe shape through overhead illumination.** A centered cylindrical highlight with equal darkening toward both cross-axis edges rotates plausibly. Keep collar crevice shading local to joints. The fixture’s upper-left light cannot be matched consistently by one rotating baked texture.

**Budget detail for the 9×20 screen-pixel collar.** Start with silhouette, two or three material regions, one identifying accent and a couple of substantial fasteners. Four native pixels become roughly one screen pixel; smaller marks need enough contrast and area to survive filtering. Put the strongest identity near the exposed left port face, since the fixture can hide the right side.

Judge the results in the final composite at play size. The useful richness comes from material separation, purposeful construction and selective wear; dense miniature texture will mostly become mud.
