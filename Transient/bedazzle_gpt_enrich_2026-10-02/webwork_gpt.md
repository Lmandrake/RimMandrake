All five use existing, authored maps on the fixed planet. Art is static; motion uses ordinary rendering and existing pawn movement. Each feature has its own saved toggle and sliders in real `ModSettings`, exposed through `DoSettingsWindowContents`.

## 1. The Mouth’s Answer

- **Marks it serves:** **2 — discoverable technology.**
- **The player's experience:** A researcher notices that a mouth-woven gutter suspension carries enormous loads through several thin, differently tensioned strands. Recovering an intact junction requires bracing it before cutting; an ordinary harvest destroys its useful geometry and rings the existing web alarm. Studying the junction unlocks the **thrixweave traction lance**, a manually operated weapon that reels targets toward its emplacement: rescue a downed colonist, drag a mechanoid out of cover, or pull an ollathrix into sunlight. The emotional beat is mastery—you have learned something from the plantation without domesticating its owner.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** `ThingDef` junction, lance and projectile; `JobDef` extraction; `ResearchProjectDef` requiring a recovered specimen/techprint; `RecipeDef`; `SoundDef`. Main hook: custom `Projectile.Impact`, registering a tether with `MapComponentTick`. It reads target mass/body size, intervening cells and available power/cable; writes gradual position changes, interrupted movement jobs and cable consumption. Walls stop the pull, visibly; large targets demand more energy and thrixweave rather than receiving arbitrary immunity. Once researched, construction works everywhere using imported Webwork silk. Settings: enable, extraction work, range, pull speed and consumption.
- **Readable signs:** A drawn cable, projected pull path, load estimate and rising tension sound; an obstructed cable visibly slackens or breaks. Pawns move through readable steps and remain present throughout.
- **Drawn from:** **Subnautica’s propulsion cannon**, which manipulates objects and repels creatures, supplies the positioning principle; this adds an anchored, interruptible physical connection and discovery through alien construction rather than manufactured fragments. [Developer feature description](https://unknownworlds.com/en/news/alterra-science-update-released). **VFE Ancients’ discovery of concealed technology** informs the acquisition drama; this produces transferable engineering knowledge rather than superpowers. [Creator’s description](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2654846754).
- **Why it is unique here:** It shares **Pyrelands’** learn-here/use-anywhere structure, but applies mouth-silk engineering to forced positioning rather than electrical protection, while **Fever Wood’s** stakes redirect behaviour through attraction rather than physically reeling a target.
- **Tier:** **Free, RM_**; a complete exotic engineering progression using ollathrix construction and thrixweave.
- **Size:** **L**; displacement, obstruction and native flying-target interactions deserve careful integration.

## 2. They Ate the Colossus

- **Marks it serves:** **5 — the giant, encountered through its remains.**
- **The player's experience:** Across one designated Webwork map lies the suspended skeleton of an **urraveth**, an extinct plantation grazer whose thorax could contain several colony rooms. Initially, white wrapping makes its individual bones look like unrelated terrain. Examining successive sections reconstructs its death: a pinned joint, layered mouth-silk restraints, repeated mandible cuts, then feeding marks from ordinary ollathrix. Opening the final wrapping reveals the whole outline, turning awe into dread—the giant was prey.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** An authored arrangement of segmented `ThingDef` bones and wrapping; `JobDef` examination and dissection; `DesignationDef`; `SoundDef`. Main hook: `JobDriver_ReconstructCarcass`, backed by a saved `MapComponent` holding discovered evidence and load-bearing connections. It reads inspected segments and severed supports; writes a casebook, newly exposed passages and warned bone collapses using ordinary damage. This is one ancient carcass, with no living seventh species, new nest or ollathrix caste. Settings: enable, examination time, support strength and collapse warning duration.
- **Readable signs:** Each discovery outlines another part of the body on the map. Loaded bones creak; threatened cells receive a collapse overlay before anything falls. Injured pawns and their bodies remain visible.
- **Drawn from:** **Subnautica’s Lost River skull and alien examination site** provide scale conveyed through remains; this makes the evidence physically accessible in stages and makes opening it change the encounter space. [Developer description](https://unknownworlds.com/en/news/infected-update-released). **Alpha Biomes’ Gallatross Graveyard** supplies the precedent for colossal skeletal scenery; this adds one coherent death reconstruction and a support puzzle rather than scattered remains. [Creator’s biome descriptions](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=1841354677).
- **Why it is unique here:** Unlike **Greentide’s** greatbole or **Nightside Ice’s** living hessarund, this giant is dead evidence of predation, and unlike **The Sump’s** giant it creates no approaching evacuation front.
- **Tier:** **Free, RM_**; the urraveth is an invented extinct Webwork animal represented entirely by environmental remains.
- **Size:** **M**; several static bone assets, examination jobs and bounded collapse logic.

## 3. Your Hull Is a Trellis

- **Marks it serves:** **6 — gravship touch.**
- **The player's experience:** Where a living gutter reaches a building seam, threllick roots begin threading between its construction layers. On a landed gravship, the first sign is a pale root emerging through an interior doorframe—the plantation has found another structure to occupy. Left connected, the root thickens, forces the door open and eventually splits its frame; you must excise the intrusion before it reaches another compartment. Takeoff severs its supply and stops further growth, but the damaged doorway travels with you: an intimate violation that can become an ordinary vacuum breach later.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** XML patches adding a `ThingComp` to eligible doors/walls; `JobDef` excision; `ThingDef` root debris; `SoundDef`. Main hook: `CompRootIntrusion.CompTickRare`. It reads physical adjacency to supplied Webwork gutters, parent building condition and current map; writes persistent intrusion stages, door opening and conventional structural damage. Component data travels with the building through native gravship transport; severed roots become inert, visibly bleaching. The same intrusion affects stationary buildings, so the ship needs no special gadget. Settings: enable, contact delay, growth interval, damage and excision work.
- **Readable signs:** Exterior entry roots, matching interior growth, cracking sounds and an inspection line naming the affected compartment. A breach produces a letter and the usual enclosure consequences.
- **Drawn from:** **Anomaly’s fleshmass** establishes hostile growth as something that physically occupies colony space; this infiltrates individual construction seams through existing plantation infrastructure, without another heart or research-to-kill boss. [Developer preview](https://ludeon.com/blog/2024/03/anomaly-preview-1-fleshbeasts-fleshmass-heart-pit-gate-and-dreadmeld/). **Odyssey’s mobile colony** supplies persistence: damage follows the transported building instead of ending when the encounter map is left. [Developer announcement](https://ludeon.com/blog/2025/06/announcing-odyssey-and-update-1-6/).
- **Why it is unique here:** **Stillsand** takes the ship through burial and **Nightside Ice** imposes a hull survival rule; the Webwork colonizes construction seams through a visible biological connection, leaving ordinary damage after departure.
- **Tier:** **Free, RM_**; it develops existing threllick and gutter biology, independent of campaign fiction.
- **Size:** **M**; the chief verification work is component persistence and enclosure behaviour during transport.

## 4. The White Sky Comes Down

- **Marks it serves:** **8 — interesting weather**, supporting the light-moat.
- **The player's experience:** Following a thunderstorm, wind catches damaged sheets high in the canopy; white material appears overhead while the ordinary sky begins clearing. A torn sheet falls and snags between trees and outer walls, laying a physical roof across part of your carefully cleared moat. You intercept its exposed suspension knots with gunfire before it settles; otherwise, the new roof gives the owners a shaded crossing until you destroy it. The emotional beat is sudden alarm: sunshine has returned, but your defensive geometry has changed.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** `IncidentDef`, `GameConditionDef`, targetable sheet/attachment `ThingDefs`, removable `RoofDef`, `SoundDefs`. Main hook: `MapComponent_Sheetfall.MapComponentTick`, triggered by suitable transitions in the existing vanilla weather table. It reads surviving canopy sheets, wind and available snag points; writes falling debris, targetable attachments and bounded roof footprints. A roof-support patch recognizes sheet attachments; destruction removes only roofs owned by that sheet. Existing sun-scald reads the resulting actual roof state; temperatures remain vanilla. Settings: enable, frequency, sheet count, settling warning and attachment durability.
- **Readable signs:** Large descending static sprites, ripping sound, projected landing footprints and highlighted knots. Existing pellareth and web signs continue to describe any connected line beneath the settled sheet.
- **Drawn from:** **Against the Storm’s Forest Mysteries** tie environmental danger to weather; this replaces numerical seasonal penalties with visible, destructible material that changes routes. [Developer examples](https://eremitegames.com/recipes-cookbook-update/). **ReGrowth’s weather overlays** inform inexpensive presentation; the overlay here corresponds to actual objects and roof cells. [Creator’s description and credits](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2261114471).
- **Why it is unique here:** Unlike **The Abyss’s** etchfall, this fall does not erode walls, and unlike **Long Shade’s** shade economy, the shadow is an unwanted physical bridge for predators rather than something traded.
- **Tier:** **Free, RM_**; entirely generated by the existing mouth-woven canopy.
- **Size:** **L**; roof ownership, support and cleanup are the substantial work, with no new weather table or animated rig.

## 5. Testimony Beneath Sh’kaar

- **Marks it serves:** **9 — a found Salvation rite.**
- **The player's experience:** In a ruined refuge, inscriptions survive only on the outward-facing wall: the settlers sheltered beneath canopy, and the last survivors recorded how reaching open sun saved them. Reading it opens a research row for **Testimony Beneath Sh’kaar**, learned once and performable anywhere afterward. The gathering names one actual, previously unacknowledged colony failure—a death, abandonment or destroyed home—and witnesses it together under an open sky; each historical loss can receive testimony only once. Here, the same exposure that keeps the owners away risks ordinary heatstroke, producing shared humility rather than triumph.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** Inscription `ThingDef`; `ResearchProjectDef`; ritual `PreceptDef`, `RitualPatternDef`, `RitualBehaviorDef`, `RitualOutcomeEffectDef` and social-memory `ThoughtDef`. Main hook: custom `RitualOutcomeEffectWorker`, with a `GameComponent` recording eligible losses and completed testimonies. It reads colony history and attendance; writes a permanent communal account and ordinary social memories, with no material reward or power. Any favour appears only as a small bias in subsequent ordinary clear-weather selection, implemented in a narrowly scoped Harmony patch—never a guaranteed weather command or pawn status. Settings: enable, duration, social-memory strength and favour-odds contribution.
- **Readable signs:** The inscription explains why it survived; the ritual UI names the historical loss and lists actual exposure risks. Campaign narration may say, “Sh’kaar has room for the truth—and hunger enough for those who speak it.”
- **Drawn from:** **Dwarf Fortress’s memorial slabs** inform durable remembrance; this requires collective testimony about the colony’s own failure rather than merely commissioning an inscription. [Developer notes](https://www.bay12games.com/dwarves/). **Ideology’s circumstance-dependent social rituals** provide the gathering framework; this omits its psychic powers, recruits and treasure rewards and binds each performance to a distinct historical loss. [Developer preview](https://ludeon.com/blog/2021/07/ideology-adds-social-roles-and-rituals/).
- **Why it is unique here:** **The Abyss’s** rites require practices of darkness and **Pyrelands’** Struck Glass produces a lightning event; this teaches public exposure and remembrance, with danger arising from the actual environment and favour remaining uncertain.
- **Tier:** **Campaign, RUT_**; the Salvation and Sh’kaar belong there, while the four RM_ additions remain complete without religion.
- **Size:** **M**; history bookkeeping and ritual integration, with one static inscription asset.

**First: The Mouth’s Answer** — fills the strongest gap with valuable technology that carries a distinctly Webwork lesson everywhere.  
**Second: They Ate the Colossus** — delivers enormous visual identity and predator dread at moderate cost.  
**Order rationale:** establish those two signatures first; prototype Sheetfall’s roof behaviour before committing its full build.
