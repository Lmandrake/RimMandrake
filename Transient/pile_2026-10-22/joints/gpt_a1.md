**Fix the connections before polishing the surfaces.** The biggest visual problem is that several lines stop near equipment without visibly entering a fitting.

Ranked by impact; dimensions below are fractions of one map cell:

1. **Hose thickness and flat/plump distinction — H1/H2.** Hoses read as string, especially the dark upper runs. H2’s “plump” hose actually reads thinner than the flat one. Roughly double the hose body: **0.20 filled diameter**, with a rounded highlight and close contact shadow. Flat hose can be **0.20–0.22 wide**, but should look like a shallow ribbon with occasional folds. Preserve the same attached endpoints in both states.

2. **Hose → tank / pump — H1/H2.** Several tank connections have visible daylight between the hose ferrule and machine nozzle; the pump connection is difficult to read beneath the lightning overlay. End the hose **inside a socket**, with its tip hidden by the coupling. Use a **0.24-wide ferrule**, a reducing adapter to the machine port, and a short straight approach aligned with that port. On the four-sided pump, connect to the actual cardinal flange.

3. **Hose → reel — H1/H2.** The west-facing nozzle makes an eastbound hose’s route difficult to follow; cropped portions prevent verifying continuity. Start the hose **inside the west nozzle**, behind its collar. For an eastern target, draw a generous return loop around the reel, clear of its feet, or rotate the reel to face east. No diagonal shortcut through the drum; bend radius roughly **0.4–0.6 cell**.

4. **Ground pipe → pole → overhead span — F.** Ground pipes stop short of the poles, while the span and insulators strongly suggest electricity. Extend each ground pipe into a **base elbow**, run a visible riser up the pole, then connect through another elbow to the span. Use **0.08 pipe**, **0.12 collars**, and visible supporting saddles. A rigid span should be nearly straight; a flexible span needs visibly secured ends and restrained sag.

5. **Pipe → squat tank — B.** The west pipe reaches the skid’s lower edge rather than visibly entering the vessel. **Keep the specified lower-row alignment.** Add a low west-facing inlet manifold there, with a short riser/elbow into the tank shell. Hide the pipe tip inside a **0.12–0.14 flange**; keep the skid foot visually separate.

6. **Pipe → tall tank — B.** The east pipe appears to meet a foot or disappear beneath the base. Add a distinct east-facing outlet near the bottom of the vessel, connected by a short downturned manifold to the ground pipe’s height. End the pipe inside its flange. Separate the port, support foot, and contact shadow so the flow path is readable.

7. **Pipe → taps — E.** The southern taps and eastern end tap look freestanding: there is no visible feed. For each southern tap, add a T on the main and a **0.08 branch running south to the tap’s base**, then a short riser into the valve. At the eastern tap, continue the main into a base elbow/riser. The dispensing spout remains an open outlet.

8. **Hose → pipe end — H2.** The hose changes directly into the narrow striped pipe, making the join look like a texture swap. Finish the rigid pipe with a **0.12 union**, add a stepped hose adapter and **0.24 ferrule**, and tuck the hose beneath it. Align the final hose tangent with the pipe; put any bend before the coupling.

9. **Pipe T / cross; sprinkler seating — A.** The small circular centers make the branches read as lines meeting beneath a token. Draw a continuous three- or four-way fitting with **0.08 bores** and restrained **0.11–0.12 shoulders**. Where a sprinkler occupies the cell, show its short central riser and raised head; all connected arms should disappear beneath its mounting base.

10. **Straight pipe joins — A/E.** Dense repeated bands make the runs resemble ladders or segmented cables. Keep the barrel continuous at **0.08**, with one narrow coupling at an actual joint. Linked sprites must meet exactly at cell boundaries, without doubled collars, pinched sections, or tiny gaps.

11. **Pipe bends — A.** The curves are reasonably smooth, but repeated striping makes them feel like bent conduit. Use a deliberate quarter-circle elbow with constant **0.08 thickness** and roughly **0.25-cell centerline radius**. Place sockets on its two straight tangents; avoid squeezing bands around the curve.

12. **Pipe end caps — A/E.** Scrapper/Industrial ends read as dark knobs; Modern/Futuristic ends are tiny bright ticks. Use a shallow, axis-aligned cap around **0.11 wide**, with a dark seam and one restrained edge highlight. Draw caps only at genuinely unused ends; remove them wherever a fitting or machine accepts the pipe.

Apply consistent contrast across these fixes: **Scrapper:** copper highlights over dark brown, with rust in patches. **Industrial:** charcoal barrels with readable steel edges. **Modern:** deeper enamel red, fewer white bands. **Futuristic:** pale blue casing with cyan seams and a darker underside. The unchanged rusty taps particularly undermine the newer looks.

**The proposed proportions are sensible as stylized game art.** I’d set **pipe 0.08 cell, filled hose 0.20, flat hose 0.20–0.22 wide, pawn body 0.35**—about **8 / 20 / 20–22 / 35 px** at your stated scale. That gives the hoses the requested weight while keeping the rigid network subordinate.
