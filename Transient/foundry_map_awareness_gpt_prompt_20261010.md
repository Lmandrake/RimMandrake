# Task: enhance, expand and skeptically review a design on "map awareness" for a live-game debugging bridge

## Context
RimWorld 1.6 (Unity/Mono, ~600 mods, Harmony 2.4.2). Agents (LLMs) drive the live game through a "bridge" (RimBridgeServer plus our JawaBench companion DLL; tools like jawa/list_things, jawa/fire_incident, spawn tools, a storyteller_off helper). A north-star/validation harness runs scripted tests and detects "strangers near anchor". Player.log only records warnings/errors. A recent always-on TPS recorder (ordered bounded writer, game-scoped state, Harmony skip-safety lessons) is the template for any new recorder; your two earlier TPS reviews are not attached but their lessons are: hooks must be exception-contained, game-scoped, thread-aware, bounded, and acceptance criteria must not pass while defects exist.

## Owner's question
During debugging, agents are often "surprised" by creatures/state on the map. The owner sees a creature and asks "what is that?"; the agent then spends a long time working out whether it was present, where it came from, what its art connects to. Should the bridge get a debug mode (armed by the agent only while debugging) that reports on a cadence: what pawns are on the map, which are hostile, which were created by us (test spawn) versus injected by an event/raid/migration/quest/wild spawner/other, so that game-state awareness is transparent? Events also keep injecting surprises that invalidate tests, so events should be controlled/turned off better during validation and north-star runs. Is Player.log already sufficient and this just a discipline/process change? Owner is open to a brief insight/process change or to a profound bridge addition if justified.
