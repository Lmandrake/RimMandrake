## 1. The Blackline Capstan
- **Marks it serves:** 2; dirty-colony play style.
- **The player's experience:** Studying two preserved draw-joints from different dig strata unlocks a hidden research row: ancient engineers used tar-glass bearings and seepwax-packed cable to pull loads that should have torn a normal winch apart. The resulting hand-operated capstan can winch a visible pawn, corpse, animal or item along a clear line—extracting a downed colonist, spoiling an enemy firing position or recovering something beyond a burning moat. Heavy or resisting targets expose the operator and may tear out the anchor, so it is powerful battlefield geometry rather than automatic hauling. The emotional beat is ingenious relief: the Sump teaches you how not to lose what the ground has caught.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** `ResearchProjectDef`, studyable `ThingDef`, `RecipeDef`, capstan `ThingDef`, `JobDef`, `SoundDef` and rope `FleckDef`; the prototype consumes tar-glass and seepwax, while the learned recipe can make cord from chemfuel, textile and steel anywhere. The main hooks are `ThingComp.CompGetGizmosExtra()` for targeting and a `MapComponent.MapComponentTick()` tether state machine reading mass, resistance, line-of-sight and anchor HP, then writing movement, stagger and wear. Settings: enable, range, pull force, snap chance and hostile-target permission.
- **Readable signs:** A taut on-map line, marked destination cells, rising ratchet pitch and a visibly leaning anchor precede a snap; nothing teleports or disappears.
- **Drawn from:** [Subnautica’s Propulsion Cannon](https://subnautica.fandom.com/wiki/Propulsion_Cannon) moves loose objects, while [Death Stranding’s Floating Carrier](https://deathstranding.fandom.com/wiki/Floating_carrier) tethers transported cargo. Neither is operated colony infrastructure that contests live targets through mass, terrain and an exposed worker.
- **Why it is unique here:** It shares “biome-learned portable technology” with Pyrelands, but replaces electrical protection with manual, risky displacement learned from the Sump’s preserved machinery.
- **Tier:** free (`RM_`); wholly invented technology with a complete non-campaign discovery chain.
- **Size:** M.

## 2. The Fifth Stroke
- **Marks it serves:** 5, 7; dirty-colony play style.
- **The player's experience:** Every derrick acquires a slightly different audible stroke length when built or repaired. Neat banks of simultaneously started pumps gradually phase-lock: their messy thuds become one immaculate beat, followed by an impossible fifth answer from beneath the glass. Players retime individual pumps with a hands-on job, sacrificing production while the accumulating suction coupling decays; ignoring it feeds the existing tar-beast wake state, never a fightable raid. The emotional beat is dread sharpened into mastery—you learn to cherish an ugly, syncopated yard.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** Pump `ThingDef` comps, `JobDef`, layered `SoundDef`s and an optional warning `GameConditionDef`; a `MapComponent.MapComponentTick()` builds a spatial graph from active pumps, their stroke phases and overlapping suction radii, then writes physical coupling and the existing beast-wake pressure. With that feature disabled, the terminal consequence is a pump rupture plus the already-built tar belch. Settings: enable, coupling radius, grace period, wake contribution, sound volume and an accessibility pulse overlay.
- **Readable signs:** Pump rods visibly flash on each stroke, pipes shiver, the fifth beat gains direction and a persistent danger ring appears for players who cannot use audio.
- **Drawn from:** [Frostpunk’s Generator](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Generator) turns excessive operation into mounting stress, while [Factorio’s circuit network](https://wiki.factorio.com/Circuit_network) coordinates machines through explicit signals. This instead creates an unintended acoustic-pressure network from spatially overlapping machines, with manual desynchronization as the skill.
- **Why it is unique here:** Stillsand’s geophone and Nightside Ice’s rumble detect outside predators, while Forge’s clock is externally imposed; this danger is manufactured by the player’s own orderly pump cadence.
- **Tier:** free (`RM_`); it is the biome’s industrial law, independent of Salvation.
- **Size:** M.

## 3. The Buried Reach
- **Marks it serves:** 5; exploration and irreversible map change.
- **The player's experience:** A rare machine stratum exposes one building-sized knuckle from an ancient dragline, not a whole machine or assailant. Tracing mouse-line bends reveals enough static segments to calculate its single surviving sweep arc. After an expensive restoration, the player aims one enormous movement: the Reach scrapes a broad lane, crushes structures and pushes every surviving loose object into a real windrow at the arc’s end, then seizes forever. The emotional beat is awe contaminated by hubris; it can cut an evacuation apron, but it cannot injure, divert or stop a tar beast.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** Multi-cell `ThingDef`s, `JobDef`, `TerrainDef`, `SoundDef` and a dig-outcome `IncidentDef`—never a world feature. A custom `MapComponent.ResolveReachSweep()` reads the previewed arc, roofs, terrain, buildings and spawned things, then applies damage, replaces terrain and respawns surviving items in the windrow without deleting them. Static sprite-state swaps and motes avoid a new animated rig. Settings: enable, discovery weight, sweep length, restoration cost and preview detail.
- **Readable signs:** Successive knuckles remain visible, mice bend around the buried arc, the targeting overlay shows every endangered cell and a long siren/countdown precedes movement.
- **Drawn from:** [Dwarf Fortress bridges](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Bridge) turn large constructions into crushing or flinging geometry; [Anomaly’s pit gate](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Pit_gate) reveals a staged buried set-piece. The Reach offers one player-aimed, surface-scale mechanical gesture and permanent scar, not an atom-smashing loop or a new underground map.
- **Why it is unique here:** Unlike Forge’s timed giant or the weather-led exhumations of Wasteland and Blue Desert, this is a partial industrial giant whose one irreversible motion is chosen and paid for by the colony.
- **Tier:** free (`RM_`); an invented archaeological machine with no campaign dependency.
- **Size:** L.

## 4. The Reckoning of Owners
- **Marks it serves:** 9; rite teaching.
- **The player's experience:** A preserved tally-plate opens a short research row that teaches Mob’Unloo’s rite anywhere. Participants place nine owned objects openly—not buried, sealed, burned or surrendered—and recite how each changed hands; the rite itself produces only social cohesion. Six to eighteen days later, a Cross-Claim may bring two legitimate delegations carrying contradictory records for the same eligible valuable, leaving the colony to return it, compensate claimants or defend possession in physical play. The emotional beat is uneasy obligation: Mob’Unloo is not malicious; preservation has simply allowed ownership to outlive certainty.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** `ResearchProjectDef`, inscription `ThingDef`, `PreceptDef`, `RitualPatternDef`, `RitualBehaviorDef`, custom `RitualOutcomeEffectDef`, `IncidentDef` and claimant `LordJob`s. `RitualOutcomeEffectWorker.Apply()` queues the incident; its quality subtly weights claimant patience, timing and armament without creating favour, buffs or rewards. A `GameComponent` tracks the disputed actual item and faction claims. Settings: enable, delay range, minimum value, violence ceiling and eligible factions.
- **Readable signs:** The completion letter states that copies of the ledger are travelling, gives a time window, and both delegations walk onto the map with inspectable claim tokens and visible possession.
- **Drawn from:** [Pathologic’s barter system](https://pathologic.fandom.com/wiki/Barter) makes value personal and situational, while [Ideology rituals](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Rituals) combine roles, quality and communal outcomes. This rite externalizes uncertain ownership as a delayed two-faction incident instead of paying material rewards.
- **Why it is unique here:** Nightside Ice’s Cold Ledger seals a counter-gift to settle one dead person’s debt, while Pyrelands’ Struck Glass immediately weaponizes chance; this opens a living dispute that cannot be buried or aimed.
- **Tier:** campaign (`RUT_`); it explicitly teaches a Salvation rite of Mob’Unloo.
- **Size:** L.

## 5. The Kethrel’s Borrowed Skeleton
- **Marks it serves:** Fauna; dirty-colony play style.
- **The player's experience:** The kethrel is a boneless surface hydrocarbon animal that reinforces itself with loose rigid objects; it never emerges from deep tar and never digs. Dropped weapons, slag and components become actual carried inventory, increasing its armor, mass and distinctive clatter while slowing it. A handler can arrange asymmetric scrap and attempt to induce a molt, recovering everything without killing it but risking a panicked charge if the shell is turned badly. The emotional beat moves from comic exasperation to wary affection, without turning the animal into a hauling convenience.
- **How it works in RimWorld 1.6:** `PawnKindDef`, race `ThingDef`, `ThinkTreeDef`, `JobDef`, `SoundDef` and custom `CompShellLoad`; a `ThinkNode_JobGiver` selects exposed loose objects, while a `PawnRenderNodeWorker` draws one of four static shell overlays. Objects remain in a visible `ThingOwner` and drop normally on molt, death or map-edge departure. Settings: enable, spawn density, item-value ceiling, colony-property theft, molt threshold and handling difficulty.
- **Readable signs:** Its inspect tab lists every object, shell silhouette and footstep timbre change with load, and an edge molt-cairn plus letter ensures neither animal nor property vanishes unreadably.
- **Drawn from:** [Don’t Starve’s Lureplant](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Lureplant) captures nearby items through Eyeplants, and [Kenshi’s pack beasts](https://kenshi.fandom.com/wiki/Pack_Beast) carry weight-bearing inventories. The kethrel visibly turns self-selected objects into armor and relinquishes them through risky animal handling rather than digestion or player-controlled storage.
- **Why it is unique here:** Unlike Lantern Deeps’ hydrocarbon roster, Long Shade’s middens or Wasteland’s procession, this is a solitary Sump surface animal whose mechanic is readable custody and molting, not light ecology, refuse infrastructure or spectacle.
- **Tier:** free (`RM_`); the creature and name are entirely invented.
- **Size:** M.

**First — #1:** It fills the highest-priority technology gap with a broadly useful, unmistakably Sump-born player verb.  
**Second — #2:** It immediately makes sound, pumping, the tar beast and the messy-colony law reinforce one another.  
**Why this pair:** Together they deliver portable value and biome-specific mastery before committing to the larger narrative and set-piece builds.
