## 1. Nesting

The Keel Hoist should first appear as part of the ship, not as research. When the clan first claims the Odyssey gravship, the hoist is already bolted through its keel but is dead: cut cable, empty drum housing, and an old manifest identifying a replacement assembly lost in nearby fall-zone wreckage. Recovering that assembly teaches the basic loop—read a manifest, locate cargo, carry it to the cradle, and accept that lowering the cable pins the ship. The first lift should be completely legible and morally neutral: retrieving an inaccessible salvage pallet from a wreck shelf.

The Jawas should own its culture. Ancient miners invented versions of it and the Hutts industrialized it, but the Jawas turn the hoist into a named, maintained member of the clan: they paint tallies on its drum, argue over manifests, and invoke Ohm before opening the line. In RM_, this role belongs to an invented scavenger tradition called the Drumkeepers. RUT_ replaces that cultural layer with the Jawas and the Salvation without changing the mechanic.

Growth should be one research row rather than separate inventions:

1. Salvaged Rigging: basic within-map lifting.
2. Portal Coupler: paired MapPortal service.
3. Restraint Cradle: safe transport and capture of downed beings.
4. Site Headframe: player-built placed form.
5. Heavy Drum: greater mass, batch size, and speed.
6. Manifest Logic: saved loads, remote status, lottery verification, and quest cargo.

Schematics come from authored places, while research and expensive components make them usable. This lets the campaign gate power without arbitrarily forbidding cargo categories. Real Mod Settings should expose transit time, mass limits, capture rules, tether-related incident pressure, lottery odds/cooldown, and whether NPC headframes can be claimed.

The best introduction order is: broken ship fitting; safe salvage lift; personal rescue; Lantern Deeps portal; first tethered attack; placed-form dungeons; Hutt slave pit; chance chute; terminal seas; then Imperial, Rakatan, and Sarlacc finales. That progression teaches object, person, map, danger, morality, economy, and finally campaign-scale commitment.

It should feel like throwing a dependable rope into an unknowable dark. The hoist is extremely powerful, but every use declares, “We are staying here until this line comes back.” Its cable, cradle state, manifest, destination mark, and event letters must always make clear where everything went.

## 2. Plot moments

1. **The Missing Drum — RUT_.** The Odyssey contains the dead hoist, while a wreck manifest names the missing control drum. The Jawas can raid the indicated crash site, purchase a suspicious substitute, or postpone flight to rebuild it properly.

2. **The First Lift — RM_.** A valuable pallet lies on a wreck shelf nobody can reach, providing a safe tutorial for loading, deployment, transit, and retrieval. The choice is whether to pin the newly repaired ship for ordinary salvage or depart before trouble arrives. Design lineage: *RimWorld* transport-pod loading; this is reusable, two-way, vertical, and immobilizes its carrier.

3. **Someone on the Scree — RM_.** A survivor or downed stranger is visible on an isolated ledge as weather or enemies approach. The player must choose between lifting the person, lifting valuable cargo first, or risking enough time for both. Design lineage: *Save Our Ship 2* shuttle recovery; ours needs no vehicle scene and leaves the rescuing ship physically committed.

4. **Lantern Deeps Supply Line — RM_.** The cave mouth becomes the hoist’s first map-to-map destination, carrying lamps, batteries, medicine, and salvage between the surface and crystal caverns. The player decides how much equipment to commit below when the ship cannot leave. Design lineage: *Dwarf Fortress* mine shafts and vertical hauling; ours compresses z-level logistics into an inspectable portal pair with strategic tether risk.

5. **The Miners’ Last Load — RM_.** Superb equipment surrounds dead miners and automata occupied by immobile crystal minds. The hoist can recover bodies and gear or lower repair materials for the minds, forcing a choice between respectful recovery, profitable stripping, and attempted restoration.

6. **★ The Open-Line Raid — RM_.** Raiders arrive while the cable is deployed and the gravship cannot launch. The player must reel in an incomplete load, defend a divided expedition, or leave cargo below to recover endangered pawns first. Design lineage: *FTL* fights while waiting to jump; here the player voluntarily creates the delay through visible cargo operations.  
   **★ Why:** It turns the tether lock from a rule into the hoist’s signature tactical story.

7. **Foundry Base Camp — RM_.** A headframe over a Foundry tower’s deep floor becomes a two-way artery for coolant, ammunition, casualties, and loot. Players choose between a lightly supplied raid and establishing a rich but conspicuous surface depot. Design lineage: *Darkest Dungeon* provisions and retreat; ours permits continuing resupply and evacuation while exposing the lifeline.

8. **The One-Charge Vault — RM_.** An Ancient vault headframe has enough stored power for one heavy cycle. The player must choose between extracting cryosleep caskets, a mass of artifacts, or trapped explorers. Design lineage: *Frostpunk* steam-core scarcity; the rare resource here powers a specific logistical commitment rather than a permanent building bonus.

9. **The Hidden Socket — RM_.** The Assailant’s frozen impact site requires an old power core delivered into an unreachable internal chamber. The hoist can lower it to the socket, but doing so consumes a core that could upgrade the player’s own drum; thawing the site is therefore a deliberate sacrifice, not a key check.

10. **The Slave-Pit Sale — RUT_.** At a Hutt pit, the player’s pawns must personally carry a slave or unconscious beast to the cradle and lower them. The reward is immediate, but the manifest preserves the captive’s name and destination, making complicity readable and potentially reversible. Design lineage: *Kenshi* slavers and cage delivery; ours makes the player perform the handoff and preserves the same captive for later consequences.

11. **★ The Oubliette Answers — RUT_.** Before any sale, voices and marks reveal slaves already trapped below and feeding Gorga’s arenas. The player can pay for access, lower contraband and weapons, descend to organize a breakout, or walk away knowing exactly what the pit does.  
   **★ Why:** It transforms a universal cargo tool into the campaign’s sharpest moral image without adding another subsystem.

12. **Buy Them Back Up — RUT_.** A previously sold captive later appears on an arena roster, still linked to the original manifest. The player can pay an inflated ransom, trade a beast in exchange, win release through the arena, or raid the pit; the same cradle that took them down visibly brings them back.

13. **Fodder Under the Trapdoor — RUT_.** Hutts pay for unconscious Rancors, Acklay, Reek, Nexu, and lesser wild animals whether tamed or not. The decision is whether to monetize a dangerous capture, retain it for the clan, or substitute another creature to save a favored arena beast.

14. **★ The Chance Chute — RUT_.** The player loads goods plus a stake, and hours later the Hutt headframe returns a sealed crate drawn from the specified value bands. They choose stake size, whether to wait in dangerous territory, and whether to trust the house’s manifest. Design lineage: *Vanilla Quests Expanded – Ancients’* pneumatic launch port; ours makes the exchange a local, auditable wager tied to Hutt reputation and delay.  
   **★ Why:** It reuses every existing hoist verb while producing anticipation, stories, and an economic temptation.

15. **The Clerk’s Finger — RUT_.** Repeated dud crates expose mismatched drum tallies in Gorga’s casino district. The player can expose the cheating clerk, demand compensation, blackmail them for improved odds, or use the hoist to enter the underground sorting room.

16. **Droids Listed as Scrap — RUT_.** Disabled captive droids in Hutt torture chambers are transported as cargo because they cannot walk. The clan can buy them, falsify a scrap manifest, or seize the headframe during a rescue, deciding whether profit or kinship defines Jawa salvage.

17. **The Cistern Manifest — RUT_.** Palace supplies descend from the cistern court into service chambers beneath Gorga’s hall and spicehouse. The player can hide raiders among legitimate cargo, steal one shipment, or redirect the cradle toward the holding pens, trading stealth against a wider liberation.

18. **The Sea-Floor Cycle — RM_.** The hoist only opens its sea endpoint after the entire gravship has entered a terminal sea and reached the sea-floor pocket map. It then transfers crew and cargo between the present ship and seabed; it never lowers an individual from the surface or returns one without the ship. Design lineage: *Subnautica’s* Cyclops mobile base; ours makes the whole ship the mandatory transition object and the hoist its local freight connection.

19. **Last Load Before Ascent — RM_.** Damage or approaching fauna forces everyone back inside the submerged ship before it can return. The player chooses between a wounded pawn, a unique specimen, and overloaded salvage, but no pawn can escape separately. Design lineage: *Barotrauma* submarine salvage and docking; ours resolves the crisis through a manifest and the hard “ship or nothing” rule.

20. **The Sarlacc Line — RUT_.** Each authored Sarlacc depth has a paired cradle point, creating a chain rather than one magical skip to the bottom. Supplies go down and casualties come up, while the player chooses whether to advance the line another level or preserve an exit. Design lineage: *Anomaly’s* pit gate; ours is controlled, reversible freight infrastructure across several authored maps.

21. **The Frozen Rakata — RUT_.** A breached vault can either hoist the frozen Rakata casket intact or lower an old power core to thaw it in place. Extraction offers control; awakening offers knowledge but immediately creates witnesses and Imperial interest.

22. **Imperial Manifest Inspection — RUT_.** High-value or forbidden shipments leave hoist records that an Imperial patrol demands to inspect. The player can surrender contraband, erase records and lose useful provenance, bribe the patrol, or exploit the divided above/below crew to stage an ambush.

23. **Nine Gods of the Drum — RUT_.** Hoist milestones teach Salvation rites: Ohm blesses repairs, Mob’Unloo judges honest weights, Zizzik celebrates a dud return, and Ozzik’s rite accompanies liberation from a slave pit. The player decides which interpretation becomes official clan doctrine, attaching belief to repeated play rather than exposition.

24. **The Sandcrawler Salvage Well — RUT_.** A player-built headframe beside the parked sandcrawler opens a buried wreck inaccessible from its surface entrance. Keeping the line active yields heavy machinery directly into the salvage yard, but immobilizes the clan’s most valuable vehicles in contested ground. Design lineage: *Factorio* cargo-wagon loading; ours handles people and vertical pocket maps, not merely item throughput.

25. **The Fever Wood Exit — RM_.** A reactive ant hive rearranges routes behind the expedition, but its hoist landing cell remains the one reliable extraction point. Players decide whether to keep the ship tethered as insurance or reel in and risk finding another way out.

26. **The Quarantine Shelf — RM_.** A contaminated corpse, anomalous specimen, or infected colonist can be isolated on an unreachable shelf without being erased. The hoist leaves a labeled cradle marker and status notice, forcing a choice between safe containment and later recovery. Design lineage: *Biotech* waste containment and *Anomaly* specimen holding; ours creates reversible spatial quarantine rather than abstract disposal.

27. **The Smuggler’s Dead Drop — RM_.** A stranger requests an unattended hoist transfer of medicine, weapons, or a downed passenger. The return load may contain payment, evidence, or someone the smugglers expected never to be seen again; the player chooses whether to honor, inspect, or betray the exchange. Design lineage: *Sunless Sea* cargo-driven port stories; ours preserves actual stacks, pawns, and physical evidence.

28. **The Last Descent — RUT_.** A respected Jawa asks to be lowered with a treasured machine into Lantern Deeps rather than buried on the surface. The clan can honor the rite, recover the valuable machine afterward, or leave both below; a permanent wall mark records the outcome so neither simply vanishes.

## 3. Scope traps

1. **Animating ropes, bodies, cages, and pulley physics.** Use one drawn cable, empty/loaded cradle sprites, sound, a brief fade, and an arrival effect. Pawns never require suspended poses.

2. **Simulating living cargo between maps.** Put travelers in a hidden `ThingOwner` with a timer and inspectable manifest; apply only ordinary health/time ticks. Show origin and destination markers plus letters for delay, arrival, death, or interception.

3. **Building a universal portal graph.** Use authored endpoint IDs and allow one active pairing per hoist. Stacked dungeons are chains of ordinary pairs, and cargo always appears beside the configured exit.

4. **Supporting every hauling, restraint, ownership, and animal edge case.** Reuse the vanilla load dialog and carry jobs, snapshot eligibility when loading begins, and apply prisoner/captive status only on arrival. A failed job returns the target to its known source cell.

5. **Turning the lottery into a simulated underground economy.** Snapshot accepted stacks, seed one result when the chute closes, wait on a timer, and generate a curated crate from value bands. Use a per-site ledger and cooldown instead of conveyors, remote vault inventories, or a global exchange network.

## 4. Honest critique

The weakest risk is that universality can flatten distinct locations into the same loading dialog; the lottery makes that worse if it becomes a convenient value-reroll machine. I would make one change: every deployment starts an inspectable **Open Line** pressure meter. Longer operation raises a context-specific consequence—noise, hostile interest, structural instability, contamination, or Hutt scrutiny—without restricting what the hoist can carry. That gives every use a shared dramatic rhythm, strengthens the tether lock, and costs only UI, timers, and existing incident hooks rather than animation.
