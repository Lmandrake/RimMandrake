You are consulting on worldbuilding for a RimWorld-style colony sim biome: a volcanic crater land of fire and water, with open lava, superheated gas bursts, torrential rains and flooding, and lost ancient technology. Question A: a colonist can study salvaged ancient tech and learn to build machines that mount ON a volcanic geyser vent. Give FIVE genuinely different, unusual, gameplay-worthy things such a vent-mounted machine could DO, other than generating electrical power. Avoid the obvious (not just heating, not just power). For each: a name, one vivid sentence of what it does in fiction, one sentence of the gameplay loop it creates. Number them A1-A5. Be strange and specific, not generic.
codex
**A1. Fault Shepherd**  
Its buried pistons “play” the geyser like an organ, opening new lava fissures elsewhere while slowly sealing old ones with vitrified stone. Players repeatedly survey fault lines, choose where pressure should migrate, and risk miscalculations that redirect lava through the colony.

**A2. Rainhook Ossuary**  
It grinds bones into electrically charged ash and fires it skyward, dragging the next torrential storm toward the vent like a hooked animal. Colonists must supply remains and prepare reservoirs, using targeted floods for irrigation or defense while avoiding catastrophic overflow.

**A3. Mantle Angler**  
A glowing chain lowers crafted bait into the vent and reels up impossible deep-earth catches: metallic eggs, living gemstones, or furious magma parasites. Players experiment with bait recipes, assemble eruption-proof capture teams, and decide when a rare haul justifies provoking what follows it upward.

**A4. Ashstep Gate**  
The machine disassembles a person into a screaming silhouette of ash, sends it through volcanic plumbing, and rebuilds them at another linked vent. Colonists can establish a dangerous fast-travel network, but every transit requires favorable gas pressure and risks lost equipment, mutations, or arrival at the wrong geyser.

**A5. Memory Crucible**  
It burns an artifact alongside a colonist’s recorded memories, condensing the artifact’s forgotten purpose into a temporary expert personality housed in a ceramic mask. Players sacrifice treasured memories to manufacture short-lived specialists, balancing urgent access to rare skills against permanent changes to relationships and identity.
