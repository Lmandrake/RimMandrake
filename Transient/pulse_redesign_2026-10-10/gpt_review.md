**B > A > C. A hybrid would beat all three.** All reclaim space from status, but none yet makes “what needs my attention?” consistently clear.

**A — Inbox**

- Mixing rulings and artifacts makes the owner classify every row. Tiny diamonds and squares are doing too much work; “8 to rule · 8 to look at” gives totals without helping locate either.
- Newest-first buries unanswered decisions beneath fresh output. At real volume, prolific artifact production will push owner blockers offscreen.
- Truncated explanations lose the actual request. “Art texture gaps followup” names a topic; it doesn’t say what decision to make.

**Best:** Efficient, predictable scanning. Text rows fit this small window and accommodate different artifact types.

**Brief:** Mostly satisfies it, but weakens the distinction between deciding and reviewing. Colour-only authorship follows the instruction.

**B — Shelf**

- The four-column thumbnails are roughly 130×50 logical pixels of image content. Contact-sheet labels and individual assets are unreadable. These are recognition cues, not usable previews.
- Card titles truncate precisely where distinguishing information often lives: batch, subject, version. Similar sheets become indistinguishable.
- The fixed split doesn’t scale. Eight artifacts fill the shelf already; four visible rulings conceal half the decision queue. More output means competing scrolling regions or displaced content.

**Best:** Clearest separation of the owner’s two jobs: make decisions and inspect output.

**Brief:** Strongest match overall. However, spending most artifact space on illegible thumbnails limits its practical usefulness.

**C — Next up**

- “Freshest” is insufficient justification for a hero. It promotes an 18-minute art choice above potentially consequential unresolved work. The widget is prescribing priority without evidence.
- The hero consumes about a quarter of the usable body for one item. Its image still cannot support the decision at this size.
- The remaining queue mixes types again, dims old unanswered rulings, and shows only two artifacts without clearly exposing the omitted ones. The filesystem path adds clutter and clips outside the card.

**Best:** Connects a decision to the evidence needed to answer it. That is the strongest interaction idea here.

**Brief:** Partially satisfies it. The oversized hero undermines the requested focus on the whole attention queue; dimming old rulings treats age as reduced importance.

**Ranking and hybrid**

1. **B:** Best task hierarchy; replace the tiny gallery.
2. **A:** Best density and simplest scaling; mixed ordering obscures priority.
3. **C:** Best evidence pairing; weakest default allocation of scarce space.

Use **B’s two labelled sections with A’s compact rows and C’s evidence links**. Each ruling gets a plain-language request, author marker, age, and “Open evidence” action. Each artifact gets a descriptive title, format, and age. Allocate space to show at least three rows from each nonempty section; let either borrow unused space. Give each an explicit “View all N” action. Expand a selected ruling’s evidence inline; no automatic hero.

Feed this from explicitly presented artifacts. Attaching an artifact to a ruling should create one linked attention item, rather than duplicate obligations.

**Shared shell**

- **Lights:** Seven green lamps imply everything is healthy, but freshness, connectivity, and successful collection are different. Keep “live 3s” prominent; move collector details into an expandable health panel. If retained, distinguish stale/error states with symbols and text, not colour alone.
- **Bottom strip:** It still risks overflow and confusing identities. Use canonical names, distinguish agents from services, sort by meaningful activity, and collapse stale sessions into “3 idle.” A dead session is not an owner request.
- **Colour:** Teal and mauve are distinguishable, but dim variants and brown secondary text lose contrast. Keep titles and requests bright regardless of age. Use consistent author accents plus a compact BENCH/FOUNDRY key; colour alone requires learned memory.
