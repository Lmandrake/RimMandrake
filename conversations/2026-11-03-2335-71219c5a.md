**How it works:**
1. **The leaf becomes a small 3D shape.** It has a top, a long side and an end cap, cut from the 10 pieces of art. Long leaves repeat the middle section, so its length never changes.
2. **Each frame, code works out the angle.** It takes the bridge's raise progress and applies easing, so the leaf starts slowly and settles slowly. The rule that matches this camera is already worked out in the sweep:
   - Hinge on the north bank: drawn length = L × (cos a − ½ sin a)
   - Hinge on the south bank: drawn length = L × (cos a + ½ sin a)
   - East and west: the shape shears, L cos a across and ½ L sin a up
3. **The corners are recalculated and it's drawn at that spot.** The bridge code already draws its parts this way, one call per frame, so this adds no new kind of drawing. Today's `DrawLeafUp` (`D:\Luke\dev\RimMandrake\src\RimMandrake\FlowWorks\Source\Pits\Drawbridge\RM_DrawbridgeArt.cs`, line 221) scales a flat picture instead, and that's what gets replaced.
4. **The lines and shadow follow the same angle.** Ropes and cables are drawn by code from the leaf's tip to the winch, so they stay attached the whole way.

It should cost very little, because a leaf that isn't moving keeps the same shape, and bridges rarely move.
