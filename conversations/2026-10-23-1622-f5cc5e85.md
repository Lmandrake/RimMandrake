**What was wrong**
- **Every step a helper takes re-sends its entire conversation to the model.** That's how these models work, not a bug. The cache makes re-sent text cheaper, but it still counts against your usage. So a helper's cost is roughly its number of steps times its average history size, which grows much faster than its run length.
- **Our helpers never trimmed their history.** They run with a 1-million-token window, and Claude Code only compacts (summarizes and clears old history) near the top of the window. I found 6 compactions across 249 transcripts. Histories grew about 2k tokens per step, reaching 218k by step 50, 461k by step 150, and 966k at the worst. The worst helper took 452 steps and burned 229M tokens alone.
- **Your guess about inheritance:** fresh helpers do **not** inherit the parent's conversation. Only a "fork" does, and we used none today. They do load both CLAUDE.md files, the skill list and the tool definitions: about 56k tokens before they do anything. That starting load, repeated on every step, was 28% of the day.
- **What filled the history:** about 85% of the growth came after shell commands, which covers their output plus the helper's own replies. Text file reads were about 10% and the 1,058 screenshots about 5%.
- **Our own instructions caused it:**
  - The subagent skill only made sure the helper's *answer back* was short. It never put a price on the helper's own growing history. It also had a wrong line about Haiku.
  - My helper brief said "work up to ~3 hours" and "load these four skills" — that's exactly how a helper gets to 450 steps and 900k of history.
  - The night-shift rule "keep N slots full" had no limit on how big a helper may grow.
  - There were no helper definitions at all, so everything ran as a general-purpose helper with no limits.
  - Live game runs were driven by hand, one probe and one read at a time, about 200 steps per run.

**What I built**
1. **Five bounded helper types**, each set to compact at 150k:

   | Type | Model | Used for |
   |---|---|---|
   | `rm-fixer` | Sonnet | fixes a selftest or validator can check |

   | `rm-judge` | Opus | art and taste judging |
   | `rm-search` | Haiku | bounded searches the parent re-checks |

   - Its progress file is its memory across compactions.
   - Long output goes to a file, and it reads only the tail.
   - It looks at images only when judging them.
   - It can't spawn helpers of its own.
2. **A guard** that refuses the unbounded helper types (general-purpose and fork). The only override is a setting in the launch environment, which a helper can't set for itself.

The hose code only makes the pieces meet. It doesn't make them meet at the same angle or bend smoothly from one piece into the next.

Your three constraints are the textbook physics. A hose at rest takes the shape that uses the least bending energy for its length, a curve called an *elastica*. Those curves keep position, slope and curvature continuous everywhere. A real hose also has a minimum bend radius, so it loops wider rather than kinking, and a fixed length, so slack sags smoothly instead of zigzagging. Three bodies of work cover it:
- **Least-energy splines:** Levien and Séquin; Borbély and Johnson.
- **Clothoid splines:** curvature changes linearly along them, so joints match exactly in position, slope and curvature (Bertolazzi and Frego).
- **Physical rod simulation:** Discrete Elastic Rods (Bergou et al.), and the faster position-based version (Umetani, Schmidt and Stam).
