**Disputing the test:** you haven't seen it described because it doesn't exist yet. It's part of my proposal, and the redesign has to specify it. Here's how I'd have it work:
1. The builder works toward the frozen acceptance test, which it can see but can't edit.
2. If the builder believes the test is wrong, it doesn't fake its way past it. It stops and files a dispute: what the test asserts, why that's wrong, and the evidence. For example, "the test expects 40 ticks, but vanilla walking speed makes 52 the physical minimum; here is the measurement."
3. The dispute goes back to the test author, a fresh agent that didn't build the code, which either fixes the test or rejects the dispute with its own evidence.
4. If they still disagree after one round, a third agent rules. A dispute that's upheld is a mark against the test author; one that's rejected is a mark against the builder. Over time, both numbers tell us which side is making mistakes.

That loop is what makes the test author do a better job: a weak test costs it something visible. Today, a builder that finds its own test is wrong quietly edits the test, and nobody learns anything.
