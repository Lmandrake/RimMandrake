## What worked

- **The blind checker is the real discovery.** It turned back 79 of 198 checks. 38 of the 100 items were rejected at least once before passing. A worker saying "done" is wrong about 4 times in 10.
  - Foundry closes on its own word. Its rate of claiming done too early has never been measured, and it's probably similar.
- **Not letting go works.** Items reached done that Foundry had parked for weeks. Relentless worked an old backlog with a median age of 270 hours.
- **Its state lived on disk,** so workers could be swapped out, killed or lost without the item going backwards.

## What didn't

- **It restaffs items it already knows are blocked.** 8 items hit the same blocker 3–4 times each, costing 16M tokens.
- **It doesn't know when to stop.** 25 items went past generation 5 and took 35% of all spending for only 11 closes. 26% of spending went to items that never closed.
- **Clone churn:** 105 clones of 3.6 GB each, about 378 GB written and deleted, plus 118 GB of review-gate copies it never cleaned up. That this broke the disk is inferred, but it's the obvious suspect.
- **No safety check after a crash.** It came back from the outage and acted on zeroed files.
- **It ran well under capacity:** an average of 1.4 workers against a cap of 10, with 5 out-of-memory step-downs and a 7-hour stretch with no closes.
- **The spending figures are partly made up.** 23% of the spend is a flat 1.2M estimate charged to runs that recorded no usage.

## Foundry's notch-at-a-time style

- **The cost is the parking lot.** 581 items Foundry has touched are parked, idle for a median of 21 days, and 1,484 are open in total. Each time it picks one back up, it reloads context; that cost is unmeasured but real.
- **Its strength is that it takes on live-game work,** 10 of its 79 items in these windows. Relentless can't use the game bridge yet.

## How fair the comparison is

- Relentless took only offline items, never live-game or owner-gated ones.
- It drew from an old backlog, which may hold easier leftovers. The item sizes look comparable, though: a median of 3 files and 63 lines per closing commit, against 2 and 47.
- The BENCH seat's cost of supervising it (about 35M tokens) can't be separated out.

So "3 to 4 times as good" is measured, but on a pool chosen in Relentless's favour.

## Improvements, ranked by value for cost

1. **Hold blocked items** (missing predecessor, gate, judge or owner decision) until the cause changes, instead of restaffing them. Cheap, and saves the most.
2. **Keep a fixed pool of reused clones** instead of creating and deleting 3.6 GB per item, and clean up the review-gate copies. Filed as `DISK_CHURN_MEASURE_CLONES_EAT_THE_VHDX`.
3. **Check for damage at startup and after each step-down,** and refuse to start on damaged state. Filed as `RELENTLESS_BOOT_HARDEN_TRUSTED_ZEROS`.
4. **After 2 rejections of the same kind, force a different approach** (split the item, redesign it, or hand it to a person), and cap each item at about 3M tokens.
5. **Record real token usage for every run,** then fix the memory problem and raise concurrency to 3–4 workers.
