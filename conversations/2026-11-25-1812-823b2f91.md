## The sleep-and-resume study corrected me

I told you a fresh filer would cost less than a resumed one. That was wrong on cost and right on the choice.

- **Cost:** a resumed filer within the hour costs about 9k weighted tokens. A fresh one costs 113k to 236k on Opus, because every fresh Claude Code session first loads all our rule files.
- **Choice:** fresh is still right for a judge, because a resumed judge anchors on its own first refusal. So the filer brief asks for a bare model call that skips the rule files, with the earlier refusal handed to it as a short record.
- **For fixers:** a resume while the prompt cache is warm (under about 50 minutes idle) wins up to about 150k of context. A cold resume wins only up to about 100k. Our current rule resumes anything under 150k at any idle time, which loses on cold resumes between 100k and 150k.
- **Untested idea from the study:** pinging a sleeping session every 50 minutes to keep its cache warm may beat a cold restart for sleeps up to about 30 hours.

These are measured on 39 resumes and 65 continuations, but not on matched pairs, so the break-even points are about 70% confident. I have not yet changed the resume rule; that is a small follow-on once I have read the report.
