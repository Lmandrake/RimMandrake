Yes, compacting spends your tokens. But measured on our own sessions, it usually costs much less than coming back to an uncompacted session after the cache has expired.

**What happens in each case (this morning's bench compaction):**

| | What it costs |
|---|---|
| **The compaction itself** | It re-reads the whole session from cache (337k tokens, cheap at about a tenth of the normal price) and writes a summary of about 10k tokens. That totals roughly 85–135k full-price-equivalent tokens. |
| **First turn after a compaction** | Rebuilds about 95k tokens fresh: the startup files plus the summary. |
| **No compaction, return after more than an hour** | The cache has expired, so the first turn rebuilds the full 337k. Every later turn also re-reads 337k instead of about 100k. |
| **Handoff, then a fresh session** | The handoff turn is cheap, since it reads from cache and writes a few thousand tokens. The new session starts at the same ~75–100k startup load as a compacted one. |

**What this means:**
- **If you come back:** idle compaction saves money. The first turn back costs about a third as much, and every turn after it is cheaper too.
- **If you never come back:** the compaction is wasted, about 100k full-price-equivalent tokens.
- **Handoff restore vs. compact restore:** they cost about the same. A handoff is pushed and permanent, and any session can read it. A compaction summary keeps more of the work in flight, but it lives only in that one window.
