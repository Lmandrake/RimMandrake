## 2. Affordance vs. signifier — stated correctly

Confirmed, with a caution. Gibson's affordance is what the environment *actually permits*, relative to an actor's capabilities and intentions — real and independent of perception (005). Norman narrowed this to *perceived* action possibilities, then in 2013 split off **signifier**: "Affordances determine what actions are possible. Signifiers communicate where the action should take place" (005, 006). Your SPATIAL FUNCTION axis is closer to Gibsonian affordance (what a composition genuinely does to movement/sightlines/use); COMMUNICATIVE ACT is closer to Norman's signifier (the explicit marking that tells you so). This split is real and correctly assigned. The caution: Gaver's taxonomy (005) — perceivable / hidden / false affordances — means a signifier can lie. An object can be *placed* to claim ownership or warn without actually restricting anything (false affordance), or a real spatial constraint can go unnoticed (hidden affordance, e.g., a shoe usable as a bottle-opener). For your generator this matters directly: a prop meant to carry a claim is worthless if it's a hidden affordance at play-zoom, and worse than worthless if it reads as a false one.

## 3. The redundancy / three-clue principle

The literal "Three Clue Rule" as a named principle is **SILENT** in this corpus (the level-design/environmental-storytelling searches, 033–036, returned irrelevant junk — see caveat below). But its substance is strongly grounded in 010: Sandia's report insists on messages delivered through *categorically different channels at once* — architecture that reads as wrong pre-linguistically (Landscape of Thorns etc.), pictograms, translated text in successive concentric rings (Voigt), and even proposed *biological* carriers (Lem's "atomic flowers," the Bastide/Fabbri "ray cats") and *social/ritual* transmission (Sebeok's "atomic priesthood") — precisely because no single channel is trusted to survive language loss, institutional collapse, or misreading. Failure-mode evidence for single-signal reliance comes from 011: warning placement research found a label was weakest when it was purely informational and strongest when it was also a *physical obstruction* of the wrong action — redundancy across channel *type* (text + spatial constraint), not just repetition, is what worked. This directly sets your generator's floor: a claim should be carried by signals in at least two different registers (e.g., a state cue + a placement cue), not two instances of the same cue.

## 4. Cautions

- **Overwarning/clutter dilutes signal** (011, 002): too many signs together get ignored; signage clustering is a deliberate technique to build hierarchy specifically to *prevent* this. A room dense with "tells" reads as noise, not narrative.
- **Misread signals are a real, documented failure mode, including along social bias lines** (024): broken-windows critics show "disorder" is read differently depending on who is judged to have caused it — a caution that any claim→object mapping risks encoding real-world prejudice if lifted uncritically (e.g., mapping "poverty" or "disorder" signals onto in-game factions).
- **Symbols do not transfer across cultures** (038, exhaustively documented): color-coding for danger, mourning, luck, and even trend direction reverses across real cultures. A fictional culture's own conventions should be established redundantly in-fiction rather than borrowed uncritically from one real-world reading.
- **Decorated vs. lived-in** is a real, named distinction in the set-dressing literature (032) and formalized aesthetically by wabi-sabi (039): "lived-in" = asymmetry, accumulated small imperfection, wear that follows actual use; "decorated"/staged = symmetry, matched sets, pristine curation for the eye. Wabi-sabi's vocabulary (asymmetry, roughness, modesty, intimacy) is a genuinely importable register for what "lived-in" should look like at the object level.
- **False/hidden affordances waste placement budget** (005): a prop chosen to carry a claim but illegible at play zoom, or one that looks constraining but isn't, both fail — tie legibility checks to every claim-bearing placement, not just to "landmark" objects.
