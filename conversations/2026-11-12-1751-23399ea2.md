**What it does.** Mutation testing tests the tests. You plant a deliberate break in the code and see whether any test goes red. So expanded coverage is the consequence, which matches how you know it, but the direct product is a list of places the tests cannot see.

**Two sizes.** I don't have the earlier conversation in this window, but the point you quote holds for the big form only:
- **One planted break per check.** When you write a test, break the thing once and watch the test fail. This is cheap and quiet, and it applies even mid-debugging. It is already a standing rule here ("a guard is untested until you have watched it reject a broken input"), and the Relentless exam does it mechanically: a check counts only if it fails under a planted break.

This is the one that is noisy and wasted on code still changing shape, because many surviving breaks are harmless equivalents and the tests get rewritten anyway.
