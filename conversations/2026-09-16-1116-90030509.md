**LAW 1 — we dig down, we never build up.** Depth belongs to *excavated cells only*; the surface is 0 forever. No hills, no berms, no raised earth. Nothing is ever above 0, so none of that is touched — a dug cell is just a cell carrying an integer, and RimWorld already lets a cell carry a cost and a building. **Guard this law explicitly**, because "can we have a raised berm?" is a Z-system in disguise. The answer is a wall, or nothing.

**LAW 2 — depth affects movement and liquid. Never sight or shooting.** The moment depth grants a height advantage or cover bonus, we own an elevation model inside combat — the most interconnected code in the game. Stay cut: height advantage, shooting down into pits, cover from below, falling damage, thrown objects across levels, multi-level buildings, pressure and siphons. Each is defensible alone; each alone converts this into a different project.

**THE ALGORITHM — a sort plus an overflow, and that's the entire "physics".** Per pulse, over the connected excavated cells: sort deepest-first, pour until `F = D`, and **a full cell overflows into neighbours with room**. No pressure, no velocity, no simulation. It produces everything you asked for: terraces fill bottom-up, a breach into a deeper cell drains the shallower one, **spillways work** (a deliberately shallow cell between two deep ones becomes an overflow route — nobody codes it, players discover it), and draining the moat becomes a tactic.

🔴 **That overflow step also fixes a flaw your ruling would otherwise have had.** With natural sources at SUPERDEEP and *only* fill-lowest-first, liquid would **never leave a source** to fill a shallower canal — water doesn't run uphill, and the whole canal fantasy dies. Overflow saves it: a source is a SUPERDEEP cell that is *already full*, so it spills into any channel dug at its edge. Correct behaviour, produced by an integer comparison.

**Which leads to a deletion worth taking** (my proposal, not your ruling): if a source is "a SUPERDEEP cell, full, replenished," then `CompFluidReservoir` and `RM_FluidSpring_Test` can **disappear as concepts**. A lake isn't a building with a comp — it's deep full terrain. Limitless becomes "fed from off-map," limited becomes "fed by rain, season and seepage."

**Ladders are one boolean.** A ladder makes a dug cell *exitable*. SUPERDEEP without one = you fall in and are held; with one = you walk in and out. No vertical pathing, no climbing animation. And it's instantly tactical: **remove the ladder and whatever is down there is stranded** — a jailer mechanic for free, which is exactly what Pits' prisoner-cell gate already is, so the two unify.

**Three pieces of exceptional content this buys at no extra mechanical cost:**

1. **Terrace farming.** Shallow water-holding terraces on a desert world, with the reclaim rule already making lakebed `SoilRich`. Rice-paddy terracing becomes a player-authored form that looks spectacular — and it finally gives irrigation something to *build* rather than merely benefit from.
2. **The flood pipeline as a weapon.** Dig SUPERDEEP → let a raider fall in → *then* flood it. Depth turns "dump liquid on them" into a sequence with a decision at every step.
3. **Depth reads for free.** A filled cell shows its depth through vanilla's own shallow/chest-deep/deep ramp art. A dry one needs a single inner-shadow edge treatment. Four depths × dry/wet is a small finite art set — a continuous Z would need arbitrary height rendering.
