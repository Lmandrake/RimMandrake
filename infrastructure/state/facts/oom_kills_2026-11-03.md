## The 19: a test, not a worker

- The kernel's 19 scope kills equal memwatch's total of 19 in `rm-harness.slice`. The user journal's `Started` line for
  each killed scope names `bomb.py`: 19 of 19.
- memwatch says "in rm-harness.slice" because cgroup counters are hierarchical and the scope lives about 200 ms, gone
  before the next one-minute sample. The kernel's `oom_memcg=` names the true cgroup.
- **How they read:** the selftest expects CRASH with detail "memory cap", so the run reads PASS. It never reads FAIL or
  UNMEASURED. Rerun here: `run_selftests.py --only selftest_run_selftests` gave `PASS 2/2`, UNMEASURED 0, and one more kill.
- **The 14:21 to 14:49 burst** (14 kills; the "30 memwatch rows" are 10 samples at 3 rows each) is the selftest being rerun.
  10 of the 14 came from `NIGHTLY_COP_FIX_RED_ADEAUS_MRKD`'s clone, `adeaus_box` and `judge_adeaus` trees: that item's contract
  proof names this selftest. The other 4 were in walled sandboxes whose item was not identified. The rate tracks how often
  this one test runs, so it will keep rising and means nothing.

| PST | run | killed | cgroup at the kill | how the run read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11-02 07:43 | check c3, `OPS_STEVE_UNSTICK_VHRP` | git | 6.0 GB shmem, 0.3 GB anon | PASS; its report says it ran in a copy at `/tmp/vhrpj` |
| 11-02 20:25 | check c3, `MODCHECK_ULTRAFAST_CLOCK_STALL_1` | python3 | 2.1 GB shmem, 4.2 GB anon | PASS; its report says the stall-detection mutant "hang until it was killed", and counts that as red |
| 11-03 11:59 | work g3, `DONOR_OFF_LOAD_PROVE_AFTER_BENCH` | git | 6.1 GB shmem, 0.15 GB anon | see below |
| 11-03 12:00 | same worker | git | 6.2 GB shmem, 0.16 GB anon | `ga=137`; it switched method and finished |

- **None read UNMEASURED, and no run ended on a kill.** All three runs finished normally with a verdict or a publish.
- **The tree is why:** tracked files are 3.58 GB (41,563 files). `/tmp` is still tmpfs, whose pages bill as shmem to the
  writer and outlive it. Two whole-tree copies are about 7 GB, over the 6 GiB tool cap.
- 🔴 **The real hazard is a confident wrong answer.** At 11:59 `git archive | tar` was killed mid-stream and the worker's
  proof reported `flyers ... 5` and `FAIL offline` on a truncated tree (true count 24). That worker noticed. A blind checker
  that missed the `tar: Unexpected EOF` line would have failed good work. Of all recorded attempt endings, the only one that
  names a memory kill is an older note on `SEAT_MEMORY_STANDARD_FALLBACK_1`, so this has cost no verdict on record.
- **Limit of this reading:** the two checkers' transcripts are gone with their sandboxes. For those, how the kill looked from
  inside is UNMEASURED; the table holds their final reports. Attribution of the python3 kill to the mutant is inferred from
  that report and the anon size.
- **Not OOM:** `DONOR_OFF_LOAD_PROVE_AFTER_BENCH`'s later `checker_lost` ("2 checkers gave no verdict") was an `EACCES`
  making the run's temp dir, per their `.err` files. No kernel kill falls in those scopes.

- The 4 real kills came at 4, 4, 6 and 6 Relentless scopes (the sample after each kill), never at 8.
- Fuller slice, no harm so far: at the cap the kernel reclaims page cache. Anon plus shmem peaked at 22.2 of 24 GB. PSI full
  above 10% came in 5 samples, all at 2 or 3 scopes with 14.7 to 17.3 GB shmem, which points at tmpfs rather than headcount.
- `rm-harness.slice` peaked at 4.76 GB of 24 GB.
- **Watch this:** time at the cap does rise with headcount, and 76 samples at 8 or more is thin. This clears the budget of
  these kills; it does not prove 8 workers can never fill the slice.
- `rm-harness.slice`'s unit file says 12G, but a `systemctl set-property` drop-in from 2026-10-31
  (`~/.config/systemd/user.control/rm-harness.slice.d/50-MemoryMax.conf`) holds it at 24G. Who set it is not on record.
